# Example-Chapter Style Guide (writer-only)

Distilled from `assets/example-chapter-1` … `example-chapter-10`. These are the first ten chapters of a 45-chapter book: third-person limited, past tense, two alternating POV leads (Tristan, a gutter thief with a luck contract; Angharad, an exiled duelist noblewoman). Our book is first-person, present tense from Ch 4 on, so **do not import their surface mechanics** — import the craft underneath. The final section translates every finding into our constraints.

Citations are `(ch N, ~line)` into the example files.

---

## 1. Narration & voice

### 1.1 Interiority is woven into action, never parked beside it

Almost no paragraph is pure action or pure thought. The POV character acts, reads the situation, decides, acts again — in one breath:

> He crept closer to the foot of the bed, angling so that the trunk would hide him as he began to grasp the wooden chest. A tentative nudge established it was not all that heavy, intriguingly enough, so Tristan quieted his breath… (ch 1, ~41)

The thought is always *operational* — what does this mean, what do I do next — not mood weather. When the character feels something, the feeling is attached to a read or a decision:

> Tristan's hands clenched. An old and familiar anger flared in his belly. / "Palace side?" he asked. (ch 3, ~167)

**Rule:** every internal beat should change what the character believes or does. If a thought could be deleted without changing the next action or the reader's read of the character, delete it.

### 1.2 The narrator is opinionated (free indirect style)

The prose carries the character's judgments as if they were facts, without "he thought" scaffolding:

> He was also, to put it in a single word, a shit. (ch 5, ~29)

> There was no honour in Sacromonte, this horrid city of filth and rats. No one would help. (ch 2, ~3)

Two different people produce two different worlds. Tristan's narration prices everything (usefulness, threat, angles); Angharad's narration judges everything (honor, propriety, courage). The *same event* reads differently through each — that is the alternating-POV engine (see §5.4).

**Rule:** narration = the character's live opinion of what's in front of them. Neutral camera prose is dead prose.

### 1.3 One-paragraph voice test

You know whose chapter it is in one paragraph without a name. Compare openings:

> People could be funny about death, Tristan thought. / Dozens died in Sacromonte's gutters every day and no one batted an eye, but if you tossed forty bodies on pyres and made people look at them suddenly it was the greatest tragedy in the world. (ch 7, ~1)

> Angharad had been reluctant to step in, for the affairs of the infanzones were theirs to settle, but when Remund's claim was answered by the sound of Ferranda Villazur unsheathing her rapier she knew the time for such courtesy was past. (ch 9, ~21)

Cynical class-conscious pricing vs. formal honor arithmetic. Syntax differs too: Tristan runs quick and dry; Angharad runs long, courteous, Latinate.

### 1.4 Humor is dry, situational, in-voice

Never the narrator winking at the reader; always the character being themselves:

> As Lucia easily had a stone on him in muscles and belly fat while Tristan had a fondness for avoiding arguments with people who'd be able to snap his neck, he dutifully sat on the bench and shut up. (ch 3, ~361)

> "Alas, I have but methods," he confessed. (ch 10, ~241)

### 1.5 Self-correction humanizes

Characters catch themselves mid-thought and revise — it shows values under strain:

> *She is only trying to keep the peace,* Angharad decided. *That is a laudable thing.* (ch 9, ~49 — talking herself out of a doubt the reader keeps)

> He was not angry, not really. How could he be, when just yesterday he had struck one of the twins for a relic pistol? (ch 8, ~247)

## 2. Tense & distance

- Past tense, close limited. The camera never knows what the POV character doesn't (Angharad believes Tristan is a kind physician's apprentice for two whole chapters).
- **Scene vs summary:** anything with conflict gets full scene; connective tissue gets one confident summary sentence and a cut:

> The last leg of the trip to the island of Vieja Perdida… was not restful. (ch 6, ~339)

- Time skips inside chapters use a break (—) plus an orienting first line ("Tristan woke to the sound of a man coughing." ch 3, ~65). Never more than one clause of reorientation before the scene is moving again.
- Distance stays close even in summary — the summary is still opinionated ("It was no grimmer than the truth of the city he'd been born in, Tristan figured, only stripped of the usual varnish." ch 8, ~55).

## 3. Dialogue

### 3.1 Every conversation is a transaction

Nobody chats. People trade, probe, test, bargain, recruit, deflect. The information the reader needs arrives *because* two characters each want something:

- Tristan ↔ the Meng twins: information paid up front, matched measure for measure — "As was only proper between rats, he paid up front." (ch 3, ~119)
- Tristan bribes Lucia and gets the passenger roster **while peeling potatoes** — exposition delivered inside a task, with friction and attitude. (ch 3, ~357–420)
- Sarai: "Your silence, for knowledge that might save your life." (ch 5, ~409)

**Rule:** before writing an exchange, know what each side wants and what each side is willing to pay. If one side wants nothing, the scene is a lecture — rewrite or cut.

### 3.2 Engagement mechanics: interrupt, refuse, answer sideways

> "We were told-" / "I don't care what you were told," Captain Crestina sharply interrupted. (ch 7, ~164)

> "One day," he said, "I'd like to know why you left Tianxia." / "No," Yong mirthlessly smiled. "You wouldn't." (ch 8, ~285)

> "Are you going to tell me why?" Sarai idly asked. / "Are you going to tell me your real name?" he idly replied. (ch 10, ~245)

Refusals and deflections carry as much information as answers. Important facts often land on the *second or third* attempt, after a wrong follow-up.

### 3.3 Side characters get a register in one line

- Celipa: "If his name isn't on the roster, throw him into the sea. I don't care if you beat him first." (ch 3, ~11)
- Fortuna: "At her age, it would take only a slip to break her hip." (ch 3, ~41)
- Crestina: "You have from me all you'll get, inutil." (ch 7, ~167)

Each NPC has one governing attitude (gruff, vain, furious, sweetly false) and every line runs through it. They speak in full sentences, never in telegram stubs.

### 3.4 Attribution habits

- Action beat before or after the line grounds the speaker in space: "She turned to spit into the waters of the Shoal." (ch 3, ~35)
- Tags carry attitude economically ("evenly said", "quietly agreed", "flatly replied") but never explain what the line already showed.
- Long exchanges run stretches of untagged ping-pong once the two voices are established (Sarai/Tristan banter, ch 10, ~245–263).

### 3.5 Banter has a shape

Set-up, escalation, cap. The cap is a reversal or an admission, not a mutual chuckle:

> "I thought Sacromontan men were titans of gallantry." / "That's the Malani." / "Of daring, then." / "The Izcalli." / "…charm?" / "Tianxi," Tristan drawled… (ch 10, ~249–261)

## 4. Scene & chapter construction

### 4.1 Openings: in motion, zero throat-clearing

Every chapter's first line is a goal, a problem, an action, or a wry claim the chapter will pay off:

> None of the skeleton keys were working. (ch 1)
> Angharad dropped to the ground as the shot sounded. (ch 2)
> Tristan needed a way in. (ch 5)
> They'd not left the campsite for half an hour before it got worse. (ch 9)

No weather, no waking up, no recap of last chapter. Orientation (where, who, why) arrives inside the first action, within a paragraph.

### 4.2 Scenes escalate: goal → complication → turn

Ch 1 in miniature: rob the room (goal) → the mark is a Watch deserter (complication) → caught at gunpoint (crisis) → kills him (turn) → the letter reveals the whole job was a setup (reversal that launches the book). Each beat *changes the situation*, not just the mood. A scene that ends with everyone knowing what they knew at the start is a stall.

### 4.3 Closings: one clean hook, always a different kind

- **Decision:** "One more gamble," Tristan Abrascal quietly agreed. (ch 1)
- **Vow/reveal:** "…it appears it is not far from this boat after all." (ch 3)
- **Chilling implication:** One, Tristan Abrascal counted. (ch 5 — the murder count; nothing explained)
- **Shock fact:** "All forty died on the first day." (ch 6)
- **Cold intent:** It did not matter if the deed was not of his own hand, so long as Cozme and the Cerdans died. (ch 8)
- **Accepted stakes:** He picked up the sheath and the slower of his two deaths with it. (ch 9)

Never a recap, never a mood button, never the same hook type twice running. The hook is *earned in the scene* — the last line is the scene's turn crystallized, not an appended flourish.

### 4.4 Hand-offs between chapters

The next chapter opens on the state the last one created — often literally the next step of the decision:

- Ch 1 ends: Tristan agrees to go to the Bluebell → Ch 3 opens: "Tristan was the first to arrive."
- Ch 2 ends: Angharad strides toward the quay → Ch 4 opens: "They were waiting for her at Fishmonger's Quay."
- Ch 7 ends: the column marches out → Ch 8 opens: "…the road began half a mile ahead and that was where they found it."

**Rule:** end-state of chapter N is the opening premise of the POV's next chapter. The reader never re-derives where we are.

### 4.5 Alternating POV: the second camera changes the meaning

The two leads cross paths constantly, and every crossing is dramatic irony:

- Ch 5 (Tristan): he engineers Recardo's death with a poisoned needle. Ch 6 (Angharad): "What a kind soul, Angharad thought. He must be a physician's apprentice to take such joy in helping others." (ch 6, ~293)
- Ch 8 (Tristan): framed for murder, coldly notes Tredegar "trying to do me violence." Ch 9 (Angharad): she privately concedes the point and marks a debt of redress. (ch 9, ~113–117)

A POV switch always answers a question the leaving camera cannot: never a fairness rotation. Each camera runs on its own want (Tristan: revenge list + survival; Angharad: honor + Isabel + going home) — the other lead's plot is background to it.

## 5. Exposition & worldbuilding

### 5.1 Drip through task

World facts arrive because the character is *using* them: lock quality teaches economy (ch 1, ~15); crate contents teach the Watch's darkling trade (ch 7, ~43–101); a dice game teaches the gods and class fault lines (ch 5, ~5–55).

### 5.2 "A little less and a little more than you should"

The narrator explains confidently, briefly, with opinion attached — then admits the edge of his knowledge:

> Tristan knew little of the Watch's workings, for the order delighted in secrecy, but the difference between the Conclave and the free companies was common knowledge. (ch 7, ~135)

Partial and wrong knowledge is allowed to stand (Tristan misreads the twins; Angharad misreads Tristan, Isabel, Cozme). The reader is trusted to hold the gap.

### 5.3 Withholding beats dumping

Tristan's List: named in ch 3 ("the five names carved into the marrow of his bones", ~57), motive shown in ch 5 (Cozme's smile while pulling the trigger, ~37), still not fully explained by ch 10. Sarai's identity peels across four chapters (blue stitches → not Raseni → Signs → pale skin under the veil). Big truths are paid out in installments, each installment earned by a scene.

### 5.4 Description is load-bearing

One detail per person, and the detail does work later:

> One of the twins – Ju – had a small nick in the skin near her left ear… mostly it would let him tell them apart in a pinch. (ch 3, ~123)

> The boots in particular were – the thief stilled. / "Yong," he murmured. "Look at the Raseni's boots." (ch 5, ~355)

Never inventory description. If a coat, scar, or accent is mentioned, it either characterizes, gets used, or pays off.

## 6. Fights

- **Geography first:** where everyone stands, what's between them, exits (the hold, the stairs, the webbing on deck).
- **Individual moves with cost:** Tristan wins his ch-1 fight ugly — kicked, bruised, nicked, and it takes a thrown pistol, thrown sheets, and repeated blackjack blows. Nothing is clean.
- **Thought continues during the fight:** reads, mistakes, improvisation ("what was a blackjack going to help against a blade?" ch 1, ~89).
- **Powers cost on-page:** Tristan's borrowed luck snaps back visibly and promptly (flint breaks → later a bobbin trips him, ch 5, ~83; the pistol he loaded blows up against his side, ch 10, ~473). Angharad's glimpses are set off typographically as one-line parentheticals — the cost is discipline and fatigue, and overuse turns her thoughts "feverous" (ch 4, ~15).
- **Consequences persist:** the ch-2 shoulder wound is still being dressed in ch 4; Leander's pulped arm is a stump with pain-management logistics two chapters later.
- **Aftermath beat:** panting, checking wounds, the ugly decision (killing the unconscious man, ch 1, ~99–113). The fight is over when its consequences are booked, not when the last blow lands.

---

## 7. Pacing & paragraph rhythm

- Default paragraph: 4–8 sentences of braided action/thought. Single-line paragraphs are reserved for punches ("The redcloaks followed." ch 4, ~11; "One, Tristan Abrascal counted." ch 5).
- Chapters run one to three scenes. Travel and waiting are compressed; negotiation and violence are dramatized.
- Tension breathes: after the Saint fight, a flirtation scene; after the murder frame, a quiet walking chapter with trades and banter. But even "rest" scenes advance a want (Angharad courts Isabel; Tristan recruits).
- The reader is never given a status report. State lives in what characters do about it.

## 8. Translation layer — using this in OUR book

Our locked constraints (see `.cursor/rules/story-writing.mdc` and `README.md`): first person; past tense Ch 1–3, present from Ch 4 on (chapter-break switch); POV blocks of 2–4 chapters per lead post-collision; no ledger/filing narration, no metaphor-road closes, no personified places, no writer-jargon leaks, no recap buttons, copper rationed.

**House density for new drafts / next book:** match **Ch 40–46** (rationed hinge immersion, one full scene when the beat earns it) — not the leanest early chapters, and not a wall of thought between every dialogue line. This guide supplies *mechanics* from the external examples; our settled *prose bar* lives in the Ashiro stretch. See `.cursor/rules/story-writing.mdc` → HARD — House style.

### 8.1 What transfers directly (adopt)

1. **Operational interiority.** Haruki/Kaito's thoughts should read situations and drive next moves, exactly like Tristan's. Present tense makes this *easier*: "I drop my shoulders and give him a boring boy's answer" is already the pattern. Kill any thought that doesn't change belief or action.
2. **Opinionated narration.** Our first person should price/judge the world in the narrator's own register — Haruki notices costs and what people need; Kaito registers heat, throats, threat. This replaces mood-stack description entirely.
3. **Transactional dialogue.** Every ask on the road (work, lodging, directions) is a trade with friction — what does the shopkeeper want? Model: the Lucia potato scene, the Lan shakedown (ch 10, ~57–161: threat → counter → trade → both sides book a win). This is the direct cure for plot-handoff dialogue.
4. **Opening/closing discipline.** First line = goal/problem/action in the narrator's voice. Last line = the scene's turn crystallized: a decision, a reveal, a threat, a question — rotate the type, never a recap or a mood button. Both already match our hard rules; the examples show the *quality bar*.
5. **Hand-off discipline.** Within a POV block, chapter N+1 opens on the state N created, no re-derivation. Across a POV switch, open on the new camera's own want within one paragraph (voice test), and let the switch answer a question the old camera couldn't.
6. **Dramatic irony between cameras.** Our Haruki/Kaito near-misses should exploit the ch 5/ch 6 trick: same event, opposite meaning. Haruki reads a scene as evidence; the reader knows from Kaito's block what it actually was. This is cheap tension we already own — use it at every crossing.
7. **Load-bearing detail.** One detail per NPC, and prefer details that get used (a chalk mark, a limp, a dyed thumb). Cut inventory description.
8. **Withholding in installments.** The Word's rules, the birthmark, Ryn — pay out in earned scene-installments like Tristan's List. The Ch 47 monologue is the one earned exception to "never a lecture" — everything else still pays out in scene, not speech.
9. **Fight grammar.** Geography → moves with cost → power used per magic rules with cost on-page → aftermath booking. Ch 23's collision already follows this; keep it the template.
10. **Self-correction beats.** Let the boys catch themselves mid-thought and revise ("I almost say his name again" → crush). It humanizes without mood-stacking.

### 8.2 What transfers with adaptation

1. **Free indirect style → first-person opinion.** They write "He was, to put it in a single word, a shit." We write the same judgment in I-voice: "He's the kind of loader who counts your rice while telling you he doesn't care." Keep it concrete, in-register, and *about the world*, not about the narrator's feelings.
2. **Companion commentary (Fortuna).** We have no invisible goddess, but Mira and Riku serve the same function: a second voice that punctures, reads, and refuses. Give them Fortuna-grade attitude consistency — Mira's dry operational asides, Riku's one clean hit per beat — and let them be *wrong* or self-interested sometimes.
3. **Typographic device for the power.** Their glimpse-parentheticals are a house style for precognition. Our equivalent already exists: the italicized unspoken sentence (*Walk past us. You never saw this track.*) followed by the crush and one varied cost tell. Keep it that lean; it is our version of "(The musket ball went through her skull.)"
4. **Summary with opinion.** Present tense can still compress: "The fringe inland is not a road. It is a track deer use, and people who do not want their names on boards." That's the ch-6 "was not restful" move in our tense. Compress travel this way instead of scene-stacking it.
5. **Two-lead alternation.** They alternate nearly every chapter; we run 2–4 chapter blocks. Inside a block, apply their *hand-off* rules; at block boundaries, apply their *switch* rules (new want, voice test in one paragraph, hook close on both sides of the cut).

### 8.3 What must NOT be imported (conflicts with our locks)

1. **Third person / past tense.** Post-Ch-4 narration stays present. Do not drift into past for live action, and no future-memory narration ("I will carry this forever") — the examples never editorialize from the future either; they stay in the scene.
2. **Omniscient-flavored lore paragraphs.** The examples can afford two-paragraph history asides (Second Empire, Luminaries) because their narrators are educated and the register is past-tense storytelling. Our boys are not lecturers and our magic is a secret. World texture must stay at the level the camera earned: symptoms, rumors labeled as rumors, institutional facts from institutions.
3. **Said-bookisms with adverbs everywhere.** "He charmingly smiled / she evenly said" is their house flavor. Ours is leaner: attribute, then move; action beats over adverbs. Keep our canon dialogue pass (Ch 5–10 user edits) as the voice bar.
4. **Their hook melodrama.** "One, Tristan Abrascal counted" works in a revenge thriller. Our register is quieter — the equivalent close is a spoken line or a concrete next move with weight ("I know." Her voice stays level. "Walk anyway."), not a gothic beat. Match the *function* (turn crystallized), not the volume.
5. **Body-count pragmatism.** Tristan kills casually; our book's violence is rarer and always paid (Backwash, fear, aftermath). Do not let fight-grammar imports raise the body count or cheapen the cost rules.

### 8.4 Quick rubric (use when drafting or auditing a chapter)

| Check | Pass looks like | Fail looks like |
|---|---|---|
| First line | Goal/problem/action in-voice | Weather, waking, recap |
| Interiority | Reads → decisions | Mood stacks, status checks |
| Dialogue | Both sides want something; friction; refusal/sideways answers | Plot handoff, rule-card lectures, thank-you loops |
| NPCs | One register, full sentences, one load-bearing detail | Telegram stubs, wanted-poster repeats |
| Exposition | Dripped through task, partial knowledge stands | Lecture, psychic certainty, bible leak |
| Middle | Every scene changes the situation | Scene ends where it began |
| Power use | Rules exact, cost on-page once, varied tell | Copper as status check, litany re-teach |
| Close | Turn crystallized; hook type differs from last chapter | Recap stack, mood button, same close twice |
| Hand-off | Next chapter opens on created state / new camera's want | Re-derivation, muddy camera |
| Word count | ≥1,000 hard floor; 1,400–2,200 target | Padding with garnish to hit numbers |
