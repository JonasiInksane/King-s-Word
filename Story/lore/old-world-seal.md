## The short version

There used to be a **bigger world** — people living alongside beasts, older laws, a wider grammar of reality. A King's Voice at full strength can make **everything listen**, not only people: the world itself can be commanded into a new shape.

Someone tried that reshape. **Kuroda Ren interfered with Rewrite** — unwillingly / to save someone / to edit one fatal word — and the edit **stuck wrong**. The result is the sealed modern world the boys live in: trains, konbini, registration sheets. The old world is not destroyed so much as **walled out**.

**Kai** are bleed-through: beasts and laws of the prior world pressing into the seal, seeking what was lost and why. The war on the news is partly that pressure wearing a human uniform.

## Three grades of the King's Word

| Grade | What listens | When it shows |
|---|---|---|
| **I — People-command** | Living minds that hear + understand | Live now (Vol 1–2+). Both boys. What FDC hunts. Rules in `magic-kings-word.md`. |
| **II — Thing-command** | Inanimate matter + local physical law — doors, blades, **bullets**, falling bodies, locks | Develops **sooner** than throne-grade — mid volumes (roughly Vol 3–4). First cracks of "everything listens." Still not cosmology. |
| **III — World-command** | The shape of reality — beasts/kai relations, weather systems, the Seal itself | Endgame territory. Worthy / matured Voice (or both halves of the split). Extreme risk. How the Seal was made / can be unmade. |

**Grade II signature beat (plant for later):** a carrier shouts *"Stop"* at a bullet (or blade, or collapse) and **the object obeys** — gravity / momentum briefly overruled. Not metaphor. Backwash brutal. Do not stage before the boys have earned the climb; do not confuse with Grade I ("Stop" aimed at a shooter still lands on the person).

Literal→Intent still climbs **inside** each grade. Grades are *what can hear*; Intent is *how faithfully the wish lands*. Grade III is not a free skip from I — II is the bridge Kuroda is farming when he feeds their power.

## The Seal

- Modern Kirisaki (and the "managed war") is life **inside** the rewritten grammar.
- Most people cannot remember the old world; the seal edits memory the way it edits geography.
- **Kuroda remembers** — Rewrite bound him to the pre-edit sentence. That is his curse and his motive.
- Bleed (kai, wrong horizons, cities "changed") worsens when Voices awaken — the seal flexes toward a living throne.

## Kuroda's long game (end-boss — cause + intent PAID Ch 47, then he vanishes)

- **Cause:** He is the reason the reshape landed as a seal, not a clean world. He did not mean to erase his family / home; he meant to save them. He failed sideways. Stated on-page Ch 47.
- **Want:** Revert. Restore the bigger world. Get back what Rewrite cost him. Stated on-page Ch 47. Will do anything — including evil — as the boys climb toward **Grade III**.
- **Method (Ch 3–46, deniable; mask drops loud and proud at Ch 47):** Fed ideals and necessity. Helped both boys **behind the screen** without explaining the old world. Pushed them through **Grade II** (things/laws listening) as the necessary bridge. Deliberately kept both boys angry — Ch 47 states outright he wanted their fury, because rage grew Kaito's Word fastest at Shizuhara, and fury is what he needed to reach Grade III through them. Did **not** look like a villain early — Ch 18 is kindness and laughter on purpose; Ch 47 is the turn.
- **Retinue:** Ide + Katō — writer-side, both are read as more than deniable companions (present for the whole arc, unsurprised by most of it), but Ch 47's actual prose does not dramatize their foreknowledge on-page this pass. Keep as background, don't retroactively claim a "knowing cell" speech that isn't in the chapter.
- **Reveal + break:** Paid Ch 47. He stops being a strange ally/question and becomes the man who says, on-page, that he intends to finish what a king started — then the provocation overloads past what even he seems to have planned, and he **vanishes along with Kaito** in the resulting explosion. Fate unconfirmed. Do not resolve this cheaply in the next chapter.

## The moral war (endgame climax engine — live after Ch 47, now missing its two loudest voices)

| Keep the sealed world | Revert to the old world |
|---|---|
| Everyone born inside this grammar stays real (Minato, mothers, cities) | Beasts, wider laws, Kuroda's lost home return |
| Act as if the old world is lost; manage bleed | Current reality may unwrite — collateral is everyone who only exists *because* of the seal |

**Who decides?** Magic says: whoever can make the world listen. Theme says: that is the original sin. Prefer the climax to force the boys (together / in conflict) into the choice — or a third path (stabilize / renegotiate bleed without full reversion). **Kuroda must not get a clean moral win** just because his grief is real. Riku's "There are people—" (Ch 47) is the first on-page pushback. Neither Kaito nor Kuroda got to finish making their case before the explosion — Haruki inherits the argument, not the resolution.

Working lean (not locked ending):
- **Kaito** → before he vanished, answered Kuroda's roof confession with lethal Words (*Kill yourself* / *Die*), Ryn underneath every swing. Grief already taught him this world takes people; **deeper hook (sharpened Ch 47):** if the Seal unwrites and the old grammar returns, he may believe (or be led to believe) a Voice can **bring Ryn back** — not cheap resurrection, a late cosmology temptation tied to world-command. Whether he gets to act on that lean at all now depends on what happened to him. Still unpaid on-page — earn it in a real scene, don't lecture it in.
- **Haruki** → this world's people aren't drafts (found family is not acceptable collateral). Even Ryn-as-bait does not make erasing the living acceptable to him. Now has to hold this position alone, without Kaito in the room to argue the other side.
- Friendship breaks on **which world is real enough to protect** — same engine as Vol 2's clash, scaled to cosmology. Ryn is the knife in Kaito's half of that break. Ch 47 leaves the break unresolved and unwitnessed by the one person who could answer it.
