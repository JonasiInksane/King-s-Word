# Chapter 35 — Massacre
*POV: Kaito*

I clear floors the way Habu clears columns: without mercy.

Gray-green with rifles. Gallery crews. Soft captains who still have pulses and knobs and the religion of completed forms. Living minds only — so I use living minds. Not sleep. Sleep is a mercy word and mercy died on Level Two with the vents. "Drop the rifle and die." "Turn it on your captain." "Stop breathing." Cleaner than the roof only in the sense that I finish the clause. Not clean. Clean is a lie I tell about justice; tonight I am not shopping for justice. Tonight I am shopping for the end of people who made children into a stamp. Mira walks beside me through the scheduled rage, mild as a second shadow that chose its angle, wiping blood from my face when Echo paints too much dialect on the skin — and for once she does not tell me to soften the verbs. Children are already zero. I do not aim there. I aim at the adults who owned the protocol. The Word purrs. STAGE 4's hallway has no far wall. STAGE 5's door stands open enough to taste hollow on the tongue — intent leaning harder than grammar, sentences getting lazier because the desire behind them has stopped pretending to be careful. The reshape still rings in the vents like a second PA. Habu wanted a vocal signature. He got one. Now he gets the hand.

"Left stair," Mira says. "Habu's glass. If he still has a mouth, spend it on tape before you spend it on silence."

Suzumori is not beside us.

Remnant runners peeled her toward an extract thread that no longer contains Mei — Bellkeeper preserved, number failed, humming carried into dark that is not safety but is also not FDC hands. She looked at me once before the peel, eyes bright and old, and said nothing about becoming weather. The silence was the warning. I walked into the weather anyway. Mira did not stop me. Mira has always known what permission costs — and tonight permission tastes like anger that refused to file itself flat.

Level Three learns my handwriting in blood.

A squad tries ear protection and staggered listening; I use angles and short clauses and the fact that fear makes people uncover a beat too soon. Bodies do not fold into naps. Bodies end. Rifles become furniture still warm from hands that will not pick them up again. Someone fires wild and hits concrete; the concrete does not obey me and does not need to. Echo runs freely. I stop wiping. Blood is a dialect the building should learn. My voice, when I speak, is not the flat door from the pad. It is the ruined radio after the reshape — loud enough that even men who cannot hear the Word still hear the boy, and the boy is not asking.

Intent steps closer on a landing where a lieutenant tries to negotiate in soft lockdown language. I do not negotiate. Negotiation is unfinished speech aimed at people who still believe manners are a shield. "Kneel," I say, and he kneels, and then I say the rest — the picture behind the sentence, schedule wearing desire as a glove — and the Word finishes him the way Habu's vents finished a corridor. Not muddy collision. Not plum-tree accident. Choice. Mira's eyes note the step the way she notes exits: filed, useful, a door opening that she will not name STAGE 5 out loud because naming is still somehow Haru's job and I am not ready to admit the hollow has furniture. Anger furnishes it for me.

"You're getting cleaner," Mira says.

"I'm getting angrier," I answer, and she does not correct me. Corrections are for boys who still want to be wrong in a softer direction.

Habu finds the PA before we find his glass.

"This is Colonel Habu," he says, soft as intake day, soft as *inventory*, soft as zero. The softness is the horror; it never needed volume. "For the record — and there will be a record — Shizuhara's volunteer processing was always a contingency asset stream. Relocation language was manners. Inventory zero is the completed form when breach and vocal anomaly coincide. I am not ashamed of clean floors."

Confession seed. Spent into speakers like a man who believes binders outlive boys.

"I am also not—"

The sentence cuts.

Not mid-Word from me — not yet. Feedback. A shutter. A runner's charge somewhere in the glass wing. Mira's chin-cuts toward Habu's office and we move through a corridor of ended manners into soft white light and a tablet still glowing like a patient star. Habu's chair is turned. The microphone hangs. A cup of tea cools beside a hand that is not in the chair. Blood on the glass could be his or could be a captain's or could be the building learning dialect. Fate ambiguous. Haunt seed. I do not finish a clause at an empty seat. Empty seats do not hear. Somewhere a soft voice may still be breathing in a service crawl; somewhere it may already be stamped. Either way, the PA is spent. Either way, the seed is in the air for anyone who still has ears when the feeds start lying.

"Leave it," Mira says. "Dead or not, he's useful as a ghost. Ghosts haunt. Hauntings open budgets later."

I turn from the glass.

Progressive clear continues — Level Two's remaining armed pulses, medical processing's bright cruelty, a dock detail that thought bay doors were still theirs. "Die where you stand." "Shoot the man behind you." "Forget how to breathe." Intent steps closer to the steering wheel; a muddy half-thought almost lands as cleanly as a polished sentence, and that should scare me. The scare arrives late and small and gets burned under the anger before it can become a brake. Mira watches it arrive and files it under progress. I feel STAGE 5's hollow like a room behind my teeth that no longer asks permission to furnish itself — and the furniture is not emptiness. It is the corridor of children. It is Habu's soft voice. It is the reshape I promised the world when I still had a throat that believed promising was enough.

On a wall screen in a security alcove, media scrub begins while the massacre is still wet.

Chyron language. Soft lockdown fonts. *Remnant terror incident at Shizuhara Detention Complex.* *Anomalous vocal actor.* *Volunteer casualties under investigation.* Habu's inventory confession does not appear. Zero does not appear as protocol. My mouth appears as weather aimed at children I did not vent — and the feeds have my exact words from the gallery slit to prove the lie with good handwriting. *Reshape this whole world.* They will cut the clause to fit a villain. Mira's eyes flick once — not surprise, confirmation — and she keeps walking me through gray-green that still needs ending.

"They'll write you as the wipe," she says. "You gave them the signature. Let them. Outside, the lie becomes a leash we can cut later. Inside, finish the people who still hold rifles."

"Suzumori?"

"Extracted on the Remnant thread. Without Mei. Recalculation." Mira's mildness does not apologize. "Your soft boy is moving toward the bay if the road dog and the girl have any sense. We are not chasing them. We are finishing this weather."

I could chase.

*Find Haru* sits behind my teeth like a clause that would make me a boy again for three seconds. I do not take it. Looking would be unfinished. Chasing would be the last decency twitching after death — and the last decency already spent itself screaming into a PA that Habu will use against me. What remains is anger with a schedule. What remains is Mira's equation and a mouth that has stopped flinching for proof. I clear another stair. Another gallery. Another set of living minds who owned knobs when children still had throats. No sleep endings. No polite folds. The Word learns the taste of finished adults the way Habu's binder learned the taste of finished children.

On Level Two's edge I pass the volunteer corridor without entering. Purge-complete air. Wax shine over stillness. The film behind my eyes tries to replay cough-laugh and hatch lip and armbands like dropped receipts; I do not schedule the replay into a smaller box. I keep it open. Opening it is how the anger stays sharp. Opening it is how I refuse to become only hollow. Hollow would be quieter. Hollow might sleep people. I am not quiet. Mira does not ask me to look away. She knows what looking costs tools she still needs sharp — and tonight sharpness is not flat. Sharpness is a boy who watched kids die and decided adults do not get to keep their pulses as a courtesy.

A medical-processing tech — Yano's cheerful disease in the eyes — tries to explain usefulness with a tablet. I say, "Break the tablet. Then die," and both happen with the bored precision of a stamp that has learned hate. Echo answers harder. The scrape in my throat is pleased. Somewhere STAGE 5's hollow applauds without hands.

A captain begs.

I do not decorate the begging. Decorating is for boys who still think grief is a story you can revise. "No," I say — not a Word, just a boy — and then I give the Word the rest, and the Word does not invent mercy for him. Mira's shoulder brushes mine as we pass. Not comfort. Alignment. The anti-warmth that once said *it wasn't your fault* and meant *become useful* has become a weather system I walk inside without asking if I am the storm or the street. Tonight I know. I am the storm. The street can learn to drown.

Outside, somewhere, Remnant dark carries a humming grandmother away from a failed number. Outside, somewhere, a service road may be learning the weight of three bodies fleeing — one of them a soft boy who heard his own Minato confession come back with my hand on it and will never unhear the heavier font. Inside, I stay. The feeds call it massacre and pin it on anomalous air. Habu's confession hangs unfinished in a room with a turned chair. My voice stays loud when Mira asks if I can continue; loud is the door; continuing is the hallway; STAGE 5 does not need my naming to keep its tooth in the light.

I continue.

Floor by floor. Adult by armed adult. Scheduled rage with Mira beside me like punctuation that learned to kill. The building fills with ended gray-green and the smell of Echo and the particular quiet that follows when a Voice decides unfinished mercy is how children die and finished cruelty is how adults answer. A fire door yields because its owner yields; I do not thank the owner; I finish him. Thanks are soft. Soft stayed dead on Level Two with the vents. What walks now is angrier weather with a girl's mild equation for a compass and a hollow door bright enough to taste — and behind the teeth, still, the promise I spent into every speaker: reshape. Free of this. Free of the owning that wears peace like a cardigan and vents kids like inventory.

I do not sleep them. Sleep endings are for volumes that still believe rest is a kind of justice. This is not rest. This is weather remaining where the wipe happened — blood dialect on wax, PA spent, media lying in soft fonts with my words as proof, Suzumori gone on a thread without Mei, and me still speaking because the Word has not finished using the mouth it lives in and the mouth has not finished hating.

Mira looks at me in the wash of a dying fluorescent and almost smiles. Almost. The almosts are getting rarer on both of us.

"Keep going," she says.

I keep going.

The massacre is not an ending. It is a climate. I stay in it — angrier, hollower, intent-step bright behind the teeth — while Shizuhara learns what it means when inventory zero meets a Voice that has decided adults are the only column left worth completing, and completing does not mean sleep. Outside, a service road may be learning three sets of soles. Inside, I remain the weather the feeds will misname, and I do not correct them yet — because correction is manners, and manners are how Habu smiled when he zeroed a hallway of children.
