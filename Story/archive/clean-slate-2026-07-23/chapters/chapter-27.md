# Chapter 27 — Glass Approach
*POV: Kaito*

Shizuhara is glass pretending to be mercy.

We approach from a service road that believes in trucks and boredom — Remnant dark traded for work jackets and clipboards and the kind of faces gray-green stops looking at when the faces look busy. Mira walks like mild weather with a knife inside it. Suzumori hums under her breath, small enough that the hum could be age, could be prayer, could be a kept thing reminding concrete it has seasons. I keep my mouth shut until a living mind needs renting. Doors do not listen. Cameras do not listen. Dead panels do not empty behind the eyes and invent a cigarette break. Living minds only. The lesson has been expensive enough to keep.

Echo starts early, polite as punctuality. Mira's cloth is ready. Flatness sits where warmth used to keep whole cars soft by laughing. STAGE 4's door does not ask permission to stay open. I walk through it because the alternative is inventing a gentler clause for a soft boy locked in a cellblock I have not entered yet, and gentler clauses are how unfinished mouths kill what they meant to save.

"Who," Mira murmurs at the edge of the loading approach.

"Serizawa Mei," I say, clean. Extract. Living. Out. Suzumori's legitimacy hunger spoken without a crown sits behind the name like a second map. I do not say symbol. I do not say person. I say the next sentence the building will require.

A bay clerk remembers a false parts delivery hard enough to badge us past the first camera. Clean. Who. What. Finished. His eyes empty and fill with the sudden holiness of being helpful on a Tuesday. He goes back to his tablet like weather. We go into fluorescent geometry that smells like bleach and boys who arrived as kindness.

---

Facility map, spoken in Mira's mild inventory while we walk a corridor that thinks we belong:

Blocks A and B — volunteers. Youth civil-defense leftovers, quota meat, armbands that were never books. C — irregulars. Alleys, soft problems, road dogs, paperwork that refuses a neat stamp. East wing — specials. War-prisoner holds, asset-adjacent mysteries, the rooms Remnant math cares about. Medical processing — adjustment language, usefulness language, the floor where kindness learns arithmetic. Purge vents sealed behind polite panels. Habu's glass somewhere above like a pupil that files children as inventory without changing tone.

I do not see Habu. I do not need to. The building wears his soft voice in the PA: corridor codes, compliance reminders, relocation as a lullaby. Gray-green indoor manners pass us without biting because Mira's forged routine is better theater than their boredom. Suzumori's humming threads the fluorescents like a second PA that remembers kings. A volunteer line shuffles past a crossing — twelve, fourteen, eyes already learning to be stock. Something in my chest flinches. The flinch is late and small. Decency left. I file it and keep walking because flinching mid-extract is how mud starts.

"Tools first," Mira says when I look too long at a boy whose hands shake around a cup. "People after. If you try to free every cup, we lose the girl."

"They're the reason," I say, and my voice comes out flatter than the reason deserves.

"They're the reason we win," Mira corrects, mild. "Winning looks like extract completed, not sermons in a hallway."

Suzumori's eyes open. "A mouth that only frees symbols will forget what mouths were for." She does not look at Mira when she says it. She looks at the air by my teeth. "Decide before deeper floors. The Word will hear the decision even if you do not confess it."

I taste metal. Birthday candles. A hand opening. Jun under a plum tree. Aihara in a cardigan. Haru's face fighting a current I aimed like weather. Tools versus people is not a new argument. It is the same argument wearing Shizuhara glass. Mira converts guilt into mission the way she always has — open palm, beautiful reason, a gun that refuses to call itself a gun. I am tired of pretending I do not know which dialect I am speaking when my sentences come out clean.

"Living minds," I say. "Short clauses. No mud. We recon to Mei's floor. We do not empty the building."

"Yet," Mira says, and smiles like yet is a schedule.

Suzumori hums disapproval soft enough to be manners.

We pass a mural that is not a mural — a wall display inventing today's volunteer totals, green numbers pretending completeness. Mira does not look. Looking is how soft missions grow sermons. I look anyway, long enough to file the lie, short enough that the scrape does not offer a clause for the display. Displays are not living minds. The Word would spend itself on glass and leave me bleeding for nothing but poetry. Poetry is Haru's rent. Mine is extract.

---

Recon is faces in order.

A night porter takes his break on an east stair because I ask him to. A camera watcher looks at the wrong bank of screens for seven minutes because seven minutes is finished enough. A medic corridor clerk forgets our side passage because forgetting is a kindness I rent without calling it kindness. Each clause costs blood. Nose. Pressure behind the eyes. The scrape pleased to be used. Flatness thickening like soft lockdown — polite fist, my logo. I do not wipe every smear. Blood is a dialect. The corridor learns it.

We map without completing. That is the discipline. Mira wants speed; speed wants Mei now; now wants a Word wide enough to clear a wing, and wide Words are how children become weather I did not schedule on purpose. Suzumori steers us with humming and old angles — service core, observation gallery approach, the medical adjacency that smells like alcohol and quiet screaming that has learned to be paperwork. Through one glass panel I see a volunteer bunk room: rows, thin blankets, a chalkboard with counts that invent order. Not disposal. Not yet. The glass makes them look like a lesson. My stomach files the lesson as unfinished horror and refuses to open it. Opening would be a different chapter's arithmetic.

"East wing is two floors down from specials processing," Mira says, checking a stolen schematic against the real geometry. "Mei should be in the war-prisoner hold off corridor E-4. We do not kick that door tonight if the rotation is wrong."

"If," I say.

"If," Mira agrees. "You are not a key for every lock. You are a key for the locks that matter."

Tension lands between us like Echo — intimate, metallic, refusing to be only one voice. I want the cups. She wants the extract. Suzumori wants a legitimacy that can walk out breathing. Haru would want a kitchen door and a spoon and a refusal to call any of them inventory. Haru is not here. Haru is somewhere in C if the transfer radio did not lie, filing pad thunder under panic, keeping a vigil I cannot afford to imagine too clearly. Imagining him makes my clauses lean toward *come with me*, and *come with me* is muddy, and mud is Jun, and Jun is the lesson I keep rewriting as cleanliness.

A gray-green turns the corner too early. Mira's hand finds my sleeve — not stop, warn. I say, "Go back the way you came and take the long route," clean, finished, living mind only. He goes. Eyes empty, then full of the sudden importance of a long route. Echo paints my lip. Mira wipes. Suzumori's humming never breaks.

Two corridors later a camera watcher almost becomes a problem — not because he sees Remnant, because he sees three people who do not belong to his boredom. I rent him a coffee craving on the opposite bank of screens. Clean. Who. What. Finished. He leaves with the holiness of caffeine. We pass under the dome while it watches an empty chair invent significance. Mira's mildness flickers approval. Approval tastes like Mira's cloth: useful, pink-edged, refusing to be love.

"Good," Mira whispers. "Keep that temperature. Flatter is finer control."

Flatter is the door. I hate that she is right. I use it anyway. Using is what dependence sounds like when a boy still pretends he could put the gun down tomorrow. Tomorrow is Mira's favorite unfinished sentence. Mine used to be Haru's name. Now mine is Mei's corridor and the next living mind in line.

---

We reach the threshold of deeper floors where specials language begins — thicker glass, softer boots, PA codes that stop pretending to be lullabies. The air changes the way mansion air changed before a garden became a closed file: expensive quiet becoming a direction. Suzumori stops humming for one breath and listens with her whole small body.

"She is near," Suzumori says. Not drama. Season. "Not through that door tonight. Through patience. Through the right living minds in the right order. If you kick now, the building invents teeth."

Mira's jaw ticks. "Every hour she stays is an hour Habu can refile her as useful or gone."

"Every muddy hour you force is an hour the Word spends children you have not counted," Suzumori returns, calm. "I did not bring a Voice here to teach a stack new tragedies before it finishes its old ones."

I stand between their sentences like a pad between two frequencies. Tools. People. Extract. Decency. The volunteer cups upstairs. The soft boy downstairs if he is downstairs. Mei as a name without a face, legitimacy without a crown spoken on-page. My voice, when it comes, is flat enough to end the argument without winning it.

"We hold the threshold. We finish recon. We do not take her until the corridor math is clean."

Mira looks at me like an equation that just refused to simplify. Then she nods once — not surrender, recalculation. "Dawn rotation. Living minds staged. No hero temperature."

Suzumori's humming returns, softer. Approval or mourning; keepers do not always separate the two.

Beyond the threshold, deeper floors breathe medical adjacency and specials glass and a girl who has not yet corrected a count in front of me. Behind us, volunteer blocks invent sleep under fluorescent honesty. Somewhere in C, if the world still runs a ledger, Haru keeps a page open. I do not send a sentence toward him. Sentences toward him skid. Skids shake cities. Cities are not this mission's who.

I wipe Echo myself this time. The cloth comes away dark. Flatness holds. STAGE 4 does not close because a threshold asks for courage; it closes when a mouth stops needing verbs. Mine still needs. The building knows it. The Word knows it. Mira's hunger knows it.

"Deeper tomorrow," I say.

We do not cross. We mark the edge like a held breath. Glass Approach ends where glass thickens, and Serizawa Mei remains a next extract not yet taken — living, waiting, a legitimacy the Remnant can taste through concrete — while I stand on the polite side of a deeper floor and feel the scrape in my throat offer a wider clause I refuse for one more night.

Refusal is not softness. Refusal is scheduling. Scheduling is what I have left that still looks like a choice.
