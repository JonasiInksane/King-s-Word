# Chapter 39 — Elder
*POV: Kaito*

The elder's table does not do second nights of victory rice.

Kurohata's pine wind moves the black flags. The flags do not snap — they slide, heavy cloth over heavy air, the way verdicts move when they have already decided and are only waiting for mouths to catch up. Mira walks me back to the warm shed with the incense that pretends it is older than diesel. The pretense works if you do not breathe near the generator shed. I breathe near everything. The Word does not let me rent ignorance by nose.

Suzumori comes because keepers attend tables that set cups like verdicts, even when the verdict will taste wrong. Her sandals make almost no sound on the packed earth. That is not stealth. That is habit — the habit of a woman who has spent decades walking beside bells that still ring true and does not want to startle what is already frightened enough to listen.

Okina Shizuka's hands still carry that faint smoke-debt smell. Old fire. Old accounts. The kind of debt that does not show on paper because paper was invented by people who wanted fire to forget its own name. She does not waste breath on my anger. Anger she already filed as useful. Useful things get shelves. Shelves get reached for when the season turns hungry.

"The girl," she says.

Suzumori's humming stops before it starts. That is how I know the word matters. Suzumori hums at weather. Suzumori stops humming at names that can break seasons.

"Serizawa Mei," Mira supplies, mild, already equation-ready. Her voice sits in the shed like arithmetic on a clean table — no drama, all reach. "East wing special. Extract failed mid-reach when Habu zeroed the corridor. She fled with the soft one and the road dog. Ashiro market rumor puts three irregulars on a reed-road roof. Smog road after — Susuichi, if they have any sense. Code towns hide what paper towns stamp."

The elder nods once. The nod is not agreement. The nod is receipt. "She is not a volunteer. She is claim. Civil name. The house's last recognized thread while the Word walks in commoner throats." Her eyes cut to me, then to the air beside me as if birthmarks can be smelled. "Split inheritance. Two infants. One Voice broken into two spoons so Chinmoku could not break the line clean. Suzumori has always known. Mira has been learning. You —" a cup set down, verdict soft, ceramic against wood like a stamp finding its pad "— are one spoon. The quiet boy is the other. The girl is the flag the Remnant can raise without choking on blood alone."

The shed goes very still. Not Silence-still. Human-still — the kind where everyone is doing math at once and nobody wants to be the first to say the sum aloud.

Suzumori speaks into the incense. "She is a living pulse first. Flag second. Harvest language is how keepers lose seasons."

"Keeper language is how Remnants stay hungry," Mira says, not looking at her. "Hungry things eat what they can reach. Mei is reach."

I taste metal. Not Echo-metal — the older kind, the kind that lives behind teeth when a sentence lands wrong and the body tries to spit it out and cannot. Mei. Failed number. Mid-reach face through a shutter — blunt, warm, furious at freeze. Not a volunteer. Not a corridor tally. A civil name with knuckles that corrected change in a market like numbers were always hers. I saw that face for half a second through glass Habu owned and I filed it under extract-failed and moved on because moving on was the only verb the corridor gave me.

I was wrong. The ledger in my skull is already rewriting the entry.

Soft boy drowning in protocol while I screamed his confession into Habu's PA. Two Voices. Split vessels. The hollow behind my teeth likes the word *harvest* too much. Anger likes schedules. Schedules like names. I think of the quiet boy's mouth — sticky, apologetic, the kind of mouth that makes living minds decide things before they know they have decided. My mouth is the other kind. Weather mouth. The kind that completes forms other people bleed into.

We were one Voice broken into two spoons. Chinmoku could not break the line clean, so the line broke us instead.

"Who," I say. Clean. Finished enough. The Word purrs at *finished*. I do not let it purr long. "What."

"Take the girl," the elder says. "Alive. Legitimacy prefers living flags. The soft Voice — if he can be turned, turn him. If he cannot, do not invent a soft ending that leaves Chinmoku a second spoon." She drinks. The cup is small and the drink is dark and the gesture says this conversation has happened before in other sheds with other angry boys who thought anger was the whole inheritance. "Iori."

A boy steps from the shed's edge as if he has been waiting for the cup, not the name. Seventeen or eighteen. Warm face. Funny mouth even when the room is incense and verdict. Pain under the warmth like a second coat — FDC math, Silence sweeps, something that made Remnant look like a kitchen with a spoon. Kanda Iori. He bows without fear-gratitude. That is rarer than Echo. Most Remnant boys bow like they are asking permission to keep their lungs. Iori bows like he already paid for the lungs and is now paying for the road.

"Soft contact," Mira tells him. "Ashiro if they linger. Susuichi if they move. You do not take her by the wrist in a market. You spend a day being a person. Honesty is a better hook than a muzzle — for this girl." Her eyes flick to me. "The loud one collects when the day ends. Moving out. No pad speeches. No soft reunions."

Iori's grin is real. That is the knife. Real grins in Kurohata are either weapons or wounds, and his is both at once. "I can be a person. I'm good at days. Bad at pretending the dead were weather."

Suzumori looks at him the way keepers look at bells that still ring true. "If you lie to her with your mouth, the season breaks. If you tell her truth under our flag, the season still breaks — but at least the break is honest."

"I don't apologize for joy," Iori says, and means it. The words land without Echo. Without the hollow's help. That is what makes them dangerous. "I also don't apologize for the road I took. He—" a chin tip toward me, not worship, recognition "—is the king of that road. Voice. Weather. Whatever word you want. I walk it."

King. The Word purrs. STAGE 5's hollow likes furniture with crowns. I do not correct him. Correction would be unfinished. Unfinished is how we kill what we meant to save — and I am not sure what saving means when saving Habu's adults meant endings and saving children meant a shutter I could not open.

I look at Iori's coat — patched at the elbow, road dust in the seams, the kind of garment that has learned geography by being worn through it. He will walk into Susuichi with that grin and that pain-under-warmth and he will spend a day being a person for a girl who spent her life being a number. The day will teach her she can still laugh. I will arrive after and say we are moving out, and the laugh will become a scar, and the Word will call the scar schedule.

That is the work. I hate that I am good at the work.

"Go," the elder says. "Smog road. Code town. Bring the flag home."

Iori goes. Light on his feet. Sincere as a stove in winter — heat you trust because it does not perform heat, it simply is. Mira's mildness watches him leave like an equation that just acquired a variable with a smile. Suzumori watches him leave like a season watching a bell that might still ring when the frost comes.

Suzumori turns to me. "You will collect a girl who spent a day learning she can still laugh. That is not extract. That is theft with manners."

"Extract failed," I say, flat. "Manners are what Habu used. I'm not shopping for manners."

Mira's shoulder brushes mine — alignment, not comfort. Comfort would be a soft verb and soft verbs are how people die in rooms that set cups like verdicts. "Susuichi cover starts tonight. Remnant cells already rent tarp space. Chinmoku will smell Voices in a town that laughs at paper — let them. Lawless places make Silence clumsy. Clumsy hunters miss."

I think of Susuichi — soot, tarp, code thick as grease on a gate. A town where *whom* has no ministry to hide behind. Where a soft boy's sticky mouth might open doors and a loud boy's weather mouth might close them. Where Mei will correct a stall's change and smile and Iori will see that smile and mean his day, and then I will arrive.

Somewhere east, Ashiro's reed market may be teaching three irregulars how to breathe. Brown lanterns. Canal slap. A landlady forgetting faces because forgetting is the best gift market towns offer fugitives. Somewhere a soft boy still files *Stop!* under panic and *Yet* under everything else. Somewhere Mei corrects a stall's change and smiles like numbers were always hers. Iori will find that smile. Spend a day. Mean it. Then I will arrive and say we are moving out, and the day will become a scar, and the Word will call the scar schedule.

The elder sets her cup down again. Empty now. "Two spoons," she says, to no one and to all of us. "One flag. Do not confuse the table setting for the meal."

I walk out under black flags. Pine. Oil. Echo. The shed's warmth falls off my shoulders like a coat I did not know I was wearing. Kurohata's night is clear and cold and the generator shed mutters its diesel prayer one ridge over. Black flags slide. Pine moves them. The Remnant hill smells like verdicts that outlived the people who wrote them.

The elder's cup still sits in my skull like a soft stamp. Split vessels. Two spoons. One flag. One king of a road I did not ask to own and will not put down.

Suzumori does not hum behind me.

Mira does not ask me to soften the verbs.

I think of the quiet boy's face — not the PA version, not the confession I spent through speakers until the building shook. The face behind the shutter. The face that flinched at amber light and still walked. My spoon. My split. My inheritance with a sticky mouth and a panic file and a *Yet* that keeps renting tomorrow.

Weather remaining. Next who: Mei. Next where: soot. Next clause: collect — and the hollow behind my teeth is already practicing the word *king* like it has always belonged there.
