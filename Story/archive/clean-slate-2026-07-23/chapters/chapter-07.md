# Chapter 7 — Kill Yourself
*POV: Kaito*

The sweep came at the hour when the port pretends it's only fish.

Morning light thin and gray-green through Sugi's kitchen window. Kettle tick. Rice cooling. I'd been at the table shaping leftover grains with my fingers into ugly little mounds, whispering when I thought no one could hear: *you in the gray — drop — step back — finish it.* Practice. Mira's shapes. Who. What. No spare words. My throat burned on every clause like the sanded place wanted rent. Haru watched from the doorway with a cup he wasn't drinking. We moved around each other like people sharing a train platform after a fight — polite space, wrong silence, the almost of conversation rotting between us.

Mira did not come to the door. That should have reassured me. It didn't. Absences are how she teaches. Absences are also how storms arrive. Through the window I could see the plum tree at the lane's bend, leaves twitching, early fruit hard and useless. A widow hurried a child past with a look that said *indoors* without spending the word. Somewhere a radio muttered verification schedules in the same calm font as every other lie.

Then engines filled the lane.

Boots. A loudspeaker voice polite as a knife, telling residents to remain indoors while the Federation Defense Command conducted a *security verification* of west-coast refugee concentrations. The words had the same font as FRONT STABLE — clean lies for dirty work. Gray-green flooded the shrine path, the dried-fish shop, the bend where the plum tree stood with its early fruit still hard and useless. Mira's people scattered between sheds like paper caught in a downdraft. Sato's sling flashed white once, then vanished. A widow pulled a child inside by the wrist hard enough to leave a mark that would matter later and not matter now.

Jun went out into the lane anyway.

His aunt said *no*. I saw her hand on his good arm in the shrine doorway — saw her mouth shape the word, saw the sunken-eye fear that already knew the arithmetic — and then he was in the lane anyway, limping on ribs that hadn't finished healing, papers in his hand like papers still meant a door. Haiga dust in his shoe seams. Temple bandage. He walked toward the rifles with that umeboshi-festival grin cracked into something braver and dumber. Backup energy with nobody left to back.

"On the ground," a soldier shouted. Mint and boredom. The particular calm of men who have been told this is paperwork with a trigger. "Last time."

Jun shook his head. "I have—"

I was already moving. Chest first. The old way. The wrong way. Mira's shapes in my mouth stacking like bricks I hadn't finished firing.

"Stop," I said.

Not a scream. Built — who, what, stacked behind my teeth. Aimed at the soldier. Aimed at the barrel. Aimed at the moment before the world invents an ending.

The soldier's shoulders hitched. For half a second the barrel dipped, like a man remembering a dream, and then it came back up, clean, and Jun was still standing, and the thing in my mouth had only *almost*. Almost is a ledge. Almost is a pier hand that opens while the other stays closed.

"Stop," I said again, louder. "You — stop. Put it down. Stop aiming at him. *Stop.*"

Mud in the clean. Too many jobs. I heard the pier in it. I heard the alley. I heard a boy trying to finish a sentence the world would not accept half-made. The soldier blinked hard, shook his head once like he'd walked through a cobweb, and stepped in on Jun instead of away — the wrong direction, or no direction the command had successfully owned. Boots on wet lane stones. Plum leaves twitching in a wind that didn't care.

Jun's eyes found mine. Trust. Clean and stupid. The roof opened under my feet again — not Kōgetsu's roof, not the birthday, just the same falling feeling in a smaller sky.

"Stop it—!" My voice cracked on the seam. "Don't — don't touch him — you with the rifle, stop moving, stop—"

The shot was small. Practical. Under the plum tree at the lane's bend. Not a movie shot. A form completed. Papers half in his hand. The trust-look still on his face for a quarter-second longer than his body could use it. He went down the way people go down when the floor has been removed without asking them first.

Not again.

Not again.

Haru's ledger voice somewhere in my skull saying *skill problem* and Mira's mild voice saying *finish every clause* and Ryn's joke about cheating and the cat's one eye and Jun's umeboshi grin and I was full of unfinished sentences and the world kept inventing endings for them.

"**No—!**" The sound tore out of the sanded throat. "Not again—"

And then the sentence arrived fully built, the way a blade arrives when you've stopped pretending you brought it for some other reason:

"**Kill yourself.**"

Clean. No spare words. Who and what and finished. Aimed. Owned. The kind of clarity Mira had been leaving on the floor for me to trip into. Not loud like a crowd. Not muddy like the pier. A person tool used on a person until the person stopped being available for argument.

The soldier did.

I will not describe it. Describing it makes it a story. It was not a story. It was a body learning a new instruction faster than a person can refuse. One moment a man with mint and boredom. Next moment an empty shape completing an order. The lane held the sound of it and then held the silence after, except someone's radio still muttering verification procedures and someone's crying that wasn't mine yet — aunt, widow, lane, throat, I don't know, I couldn't inventory.

Mira's hand found my shoulder. Mild. Certain. The open palm from Sugi's kitchen, finally closing around the tool. She did not look at Jun long. She looked at the road inland like the road had already been paid for.

Haru arrived at a run that was too late for every useful definition of late. Shopping-bag competence abandoned somewhere behind him. He looked at Jun. He looked at me. He looked at the soldier. The ledger behind his eyes did the math and did not forgive the answer. I wanted him to say it wasn't my fault. I wanted him to lie the soft way he lies when kitchens need facts that fit. He didn't. His mouth opened on nothing. His hands were open — the flats again — and I hated him for it and loved him for it and neither feeling had a clean clause.

Sugi's hand caught his wrist when he stepped toward me — hard, tide-flat hard — and she said one word to him, soft as a shut door:

"No."

Gray-green shouted further down the lane. More boots. Someone's radio kept saying *remain indoors* to a street that had already failed the instruction. The plum tree kept being a tree. Papers fluttered once against Jun's fingers and then didn't. A drop of something dark hit the lane stones and spread the way ink spreads when you've already written the wrong sentence. Mira turned me inland by the shoulder. Lists. Hiding places. The next town. Kuroishi somewhere in the dark ahead like a tooth waiting in a jaw.

"Haru—" I tried. The seam wouldn't hold a longer sentence.

He stood on the other side of Sugi's arm with the plum tree and the papers and the thing I had done sitting between us like a third boy neither of us could carry. For a second I saw us on the flats again — his hands open, mine closed — and understood we'd only been rehearsing this. The sea wall. The dock luck. The alley almost. All of it practice for a fork that didn't care about our feelings.

We were not on the same side of the water anymore.

I left with Mira because staying would have meant letting him look at me until I became only the sentence. My nose bled — more than a pinprick, a thin warm line I wiped on my sleeve without checking the color. My throat burned like I'd swallowed the breakwater whole. Behind us Nagihama kept being a port — diesel, salt, widows, a shrine with one less living boy on its floor. Ahead of us the road unrolled, dust instead of wet sand, ridge haze instead of harbor glare, and every step sounded like a clause finishing itself without my permission.

We cut between sheds first, then up toward the inland track where the salt thinned and the wind smelled like dry grass and old smoke. Mira's people had already dissolved; paper machinery doesn't wait for funerals. Sato's white sling was gone. The loudspeaker kept being polite somewhere we weren't. My hands wouldn't fist. They wouldn't open properly either. They just hung there like tools that had forgotten which job they were for.

Mira did not praise me. That almost made it bearable. She only said, walking, "Next time you finish it before the world does," and the worst part is that some hollow new room in me nodded yes. Clarity. Who. What. Finished. The pier's muddy almost and the alley's unfinished *Don't—* and the lane's lethal clean — three lessons, one mouth, a boy who had decided *never lose* was the same vow as *never again* and was learning, step by dusty step, that it wasn't.

"Kuroishi," she said, not looking back. "Then the city that thinks a harbor makes it safe."

I nodded. Nodding didn't require a clause. Nodding was the only shape I trusted.

I did not look back at Sugi's light. Looking back is an unfinished *Don't—*. I walked. The echo sanded. Somewhere behind my ribs Jun's trust-look kept standing, clean and stupid, waiting for backup that had already arrived too loud and too late. Haru's face on the wrong side of Sugi's arm stayed there too — ledger open, forgiveness closed — and I filed both under *cost*, which is Mira's word wearing my guilt's clothes, and kept putting one foot in front of the other because stopping would mean hearing the lane again at full volume.
