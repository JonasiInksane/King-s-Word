# Chapter 37 — Weather Remaining
*POV: Kaito*

Kurohata smells like pine resin and machine oil and a victory that will not wash off.

The Remnant billet sits west of the smog rumor — old textile sheds under black flags that pretend they are older than the war. Curtains over missing glass. A kettle that never finishes boiling because someone always needs the plug for a radio learning how to lie about Shizuhara. Tar paper peels in slow ribbons. Someone has hung laundry between two dead looms — gray shirts, gray trousers, the color of a cause that does not need to be pretty to be worn.

Mira's people move like punctuation that learned to kill. A runner passes with a bandage still wet at the elbow. A woman sharpens a blade on a whetstone without looking at the edge, as if the edge were a column she has already finished. Someone bows when I pass. Someone does not. The ones who bow taste like fear wearing gratitude — knees that learned the war faster than the spine. I do not correct them. Correction is a soft job. Soft stayed dead on Level Two with the vents.

Echo still paints under my nose when I speak too hard. Copper and salt. The taste of a dialect the body keeps even when the mind would prefer silence. I wipe it now. Not manners. Maintenance. Blood is a dialect; dialects need not announce themselves every sentence. STAGE 5's hollow sits behind my teeth like a room that has stopped asking permission to furnish itself. The furniture is anger. The furniture is a corridor of children becoming a stamp. The furniture is Habu's soft voice calling inventory without changing tone. The Word purrs at the furniture. I let it. Letting is not surrender. Letting is how you keep a hollow from eating the rest of the house.

Suzumori does not purr.

She sits on a crate with her shoes neat and her humming small enough to be mistaken for a draft under a door. Since the Remnant peel at Shizuhara — Bellkeeper preserved, number failed, Mei mid-reach behind a shutter — her silence has gotten older. Not sulking. Structural. Keepers do not hum for murder the way they hum for seasons. Seasons have rain and harvest and the slow grammar of things that return. Murder is inventory weather. Murder is a cup set down without asking the table if it wanted to drink.

Mira offered her rice. She ate. Mira offered her a report on the wipe lie. She listened. Mira did not offer her a blessing for the massacre. Correct. Blessings are for boys who still want to be wrong in a softer direction. Suzumori's hands rest on her knees like two small verdicts that have not decided whether to stay open or close.

"You're being celebrated," Mira says, mild as filing, eyes equation-bright in the shed light. Oil lamps throw her shadow long against stacked crates — medical supplies, radio parts, a crate stamped with a Federation font someone scraped half off. "Remnant weather likes a Voice that answers protocol with endings. Don't waste the weather arguing with it."

"I'm not arguing," I say. Flat. The flatness is still a door. Behind it the reshape still rings — *I'd reshape this whole world* — Habu's feeds cutting my clause into a villain chyron while the real wipe stays soft and official. Let them. Outside, the lie is a leash. Inside, anger still has pulses to finish if the road offers them. The shed's roof creaks when the pine wind shifts. Somewhere a dog barks once and stops, as if it remembered barking is a report.

A runner brings a handset with soft lockdown fonts. The screen glows in the lamplight like a patient star that learned to lie. *Remnant terror.* *Anomalous vocal actor.* *Volunteer casualties under investigation.* Habu's confession does not appear. Zero does not appear as protocol. My mouth appears as weather aimed at children I did not vent. The runner's eyes flick to my face and away again — not fear exactly. Accounting. A Voice that answers endings gets measured differently than a man with a rifle. Mira's chin tips once — confirmation, not surprise. Suzumori's eyes open a slit and close again, as if looking too long at a lie with good handwriting is a kind of rudeness keepers refuse.

"They'll write you as the wipe until someone useful needs a different story," Mira says. "Useful is us. Story is later. Tonight the elder wants your face at her table."

"Elder," I repeat. Who. What. Finished enough for a shed.

"The woman who pulled me out of an FDC fire when I was still inventing reasons to live," Mira says, and for one breath the mildness thins into something younger — smoke, heat, a hand that dragged her through a door she had not earned yet. Then it thickens again. "She does not ask twice."

Suzumori hums once — not blessing. Warning. The sound dies against the tar paper like a moth against glass. "Tables like that set cups down like verdicts. Listen for the cup."

I walk.

---

The elder's shed is warmer. Incense that pretends it is older than diesel. Cedar and something bitter underneath — the smell of a kitchen that remembers fire as debt, not accident. A low table. Cups. Hands that still smell faintly of smoke from a fire I did not see — Mira's debt made flesh. Okina Shizuka — someone whispers the name at the door like a stamp — does not raise her voice. She does not need to. The room arranges itself around her the way gray-green arranged itself around Habu's softness, except this softness wants restoration and harvest, not clean floors. Her hair is iron-gray and bound without ornament. Her kimono is plain dark cloth that has been washed so many times the dye has become a kind of honesty.

"Sit," she says, and I sit because living minds listen when the Word is not the only grammar in the room. Mira sits at my shoulder. Suzumori remains standing, shoes neat, humming offline. The floorboards are smooth from decades of feet that learned this table's weight.

"You answered Habu's protocol," the elder says. Not praise. Inventory. "Children became zero. Adults became your weather. The feeds call it terror. We call it proof that the Federation's mercy was always a ledger." Her eyes find mine and do not flinch from Echo. The copper taste is still under my lip. She sees it the way she sees seasons — as weather that has a season of its own. "A Voice that can reshape must not apologize for the hand. Apologies are how thrones stay empty."

Suzumori's breath changes. Small. Sharp. The draft-under-a-door hum does not return. "A Voice that cannot put the hand down is not a throne. It is a vent with better manners."

Mira's mildness does not look at Suzumori. "Keeper law is for seasons. We are in inventory weather. Different kitchen."

The elder sets a cup down like a verdict without raising her voice — camera moment paid without naming cameras. Ceramic on wood. The sound is small and final. "We will speak of the girl later. Legitimacy is hungry. Tonight I wanted the loud one's face where my people can see that anger has a mouth and the mouth answers us." She drinks. The tea is bitter. I can smell it from where I sit — roasted barley, something that tastes like discipline. "Do not become Habu's weather. Become ours."

I taste metal. Habu's weather erased children with seals. Ours wants a reshaped world with my throat as the spoon. The Word purrs at both pictures and does not help me tell them apart. Anger still furnishes the hollow. Anger does not care who owns the flag over the shed. The incense curls between us like a third opinion that refuses to take sides.

"Keep him sharp," the elder tells Mira. "Blunt tools invent soft endings."

Suzumori looks at me the way she looked before the Remnant peel — bright, old, refusing to hum. "Do not become his weather," she said once about Habu. Tonight the *his* might mean the elder. Or Mira. Or the hollow behind my teeth. Or me. Her eyes hold the brightness of a woman who has watched too many seasons get filed as inventory and decided humming is a kind of lie she will not spend on murder.

I stand. The shed learns my blood dialect without asking. My boots find the floorboards with the weight of a boy who has stopped pretending walking is neutral. Outside, Kurohata's pine wind moves black flags like a rumor. The flags snap — not loud, but persistent, the sound of cloth pretending it is prophecy. Somewhere east, a service road may still be learning three sets of soles. Somewhere a soft boy carries my heavier hand in his ears and will not forgive the font. Somewhere Mei is a failed number walking. The Word offers a clean clause for finding her. I do not take it yet. Calendars do not pause for proof, but calendars can wait one night while victory rice cools and a keeper refuses to hum.

Mira walks me back through shed light. Runners nod as we pass. One of them — a boy with a scar through his eyebrow — looks at my mouth the way people look at weather they cannot predict. "You're quieter than the massacre," Mira says.

"I'm angrier," I say. "Quiet is just anger that learned schedule."

She almost smiles. The almosts are getting rarer. Oil lamp light catches the almost and lets it go. "Good. Tomorrow the elder names the next who. Tonight, eat. Tools that forget fuel invent accidents."

I eat. Rice. Pickle. A strip of dried fish that tastes like salt and patience. The taste of a cause that has decided my mouth is a flag. Around me, Remnant mouths eat with the efficiency of people who know fuel is not sentiment. Someone laughs at a joke I do not hear the punchline of. The laugh is short and surprised, as if laughter forgot it was allowed. STAGE 5's door does not close because rice is warm. Doors like this close when the mouth stops needing them. Mine still needs. Needs the next adult pulse that owned a knob when children still had throats. Needs the reshape to stop being a chyron and start being weather I control.

Suzumori passes my cot later. Does not hum. The crate beside my boots is pine-scented and nicked from other boots before mine. She sets a small bell on it — not a gift. A reminder. Kept things. Manners. Seasons that refuse to become inventory. The bell is brass, worn smooth at the clapper. It has been kept a long time.

"I will not hum for what you did," she says quietly. "I will still walk beside what you might become, until becoming chooses Habu's font."

"Then keep walking," I say, flat, and hate that flatness is the only door I trust.

Weather remaining. Pine. Oil. Black flags. A hollow with furniture. A soft boy somewhere on gravel who once roared the same confession and now has to hear it as massacre handwriting. The kettle still does not finish boiling. The radio still learns how to lie. Kurohata does not ask me to soften the verbs. I do not sleep toward him. Sleep endings are for volumes that still believe rest is justice. This is not rest. This is climate learning my name — and Kurohata learning not to ask me to soften the verbs.
