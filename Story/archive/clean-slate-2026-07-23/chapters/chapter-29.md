# Chapter 29 — East Wing
*POV: Kaito*

The wrong floor does not announce itself as wrong. It announces itself as medical.

Dawn rotation, living minds staged, Mira's schematic clean enough to bet blood on. We move deeper — specials language thickening, glass thickening, Suzumori's humming thinning into the pitch she uses when kept things are near. Serizawa Mei is a corridor away if the stolen map is honest. Extract heat rises in Mira's mildness like a second pulse. My voice stays flat. Flat is finer control. Flat is the door STAGE 4 refuses to close.

A clerk remembers a supply audit on the east stair and walks us past a badge point he will forget we used. Clean. Who. What. Finished. Echo paints. Mira wipes. We take a turn the schematic colors as E-4 approach.

The turn is wrong by one door.

Not sabotage. Architecture. Buildings lie the way mild men lie — with confidence and tea. We enter an observation gallery before the war-prisoner hold: a glass throat overlooking medical processing, fluorescents harder here, air sharp with alcohol and the sweet chemical lie of kindness. Mira's hand finds my sleeve. Too late. Looking is already happening.

Below us, volunteer disposal is not a folder.

It is live.

A line of youth — armband ghosts, quota meat, ages that still remember school hills — stood in thin gowns under a PA that speaks soft as Habu. Useful. Not useful. Adjustment. Processing. Words that pretend they are weather. Medic hands move cheerful. A small bright posture at the edge of the floor — Yano-shape, school-nurse energy — marks a chart while a boy sits on a bench and answers questions like answers can rent him a future. Then the future ends without theater: a room beyond a second glass, a restraint that looks like a chair invented by paperwork, a procedure that does not need my Word to empty a body of its next morning. The boy goes still the way inventory goes still when stock is zeroed. No calamity. No roof. No unfinished wish. Just arithmetic wearing gloves.

Another volunteer is walked forward. Another chart. Another soft sentence about relocation that does not relocate. Purge vents wait in the walls like polite throats. Someone cries and is told crying is noncompliant. Someone does not cry and is praised for maturity. The praise is worse.

Backwash hits me like a second tongue biting the first.

Not Echo from a clause I spoke — I have not spoken. This is older weather: the roof, the hand opening, Ryn's weight becoming air, Jun under the plum tree learning that unfinished *stop* is a kind of murder, Aihara's eyes emptying on schedule because I asked them to. Empathy arrives late and tries to stand up. Flatness sits on its chest. STAGE 4 likes flatness. Flatness does not argue with the next sentence. I taste metal that isn't command-cost. Birthday candles. A cup shaking in a corridor yesterday. Mira saying tools first. Suzumori saying decide whether person or symbol. The building's true arithmetic writes itself on the glass in fingerprints and fog: volunteers are not held. Volunteers are spent. Quiet Field is a kitchen that cooks silence and calls the recipe kindness.

"Kaito," Mira says, soft for once. Not mild — careful. "Eyes on extract. That floor is why we take her. Faster. Now."

Suzumori does not hum. The absence is a crack in the air. Her small face is grim the way keepers get grim when language has finished pretending. "I knew the rumor," she says. "Knowing is not the same as watching a child become a zero." She looks at me. "Do not schedule this room today. If you open your mouth wide enough to free it, you will invent a massacre the building will blame on Remnant weather, and the girl will die in the filing. Steer."

My hands want the glass. Not to break it — to schedule the room into mercy. Mercy muddy. Mercy wide. Mercy the Word would interpret as weather and spend every living mind on the floor until Backwash left me a stamp with no boy left inside. I see, unwanted, Haru's face filing this under kitchen. I see Riku's crooked tooth learning the gown-line as personal weather. I see Mira's burn-scar memory stay offstage where it belongs while her equation rewrites *faster* in bright ink. Decency flinches. Flatness sits on the flinch until it stops being useful.

Steer.

The Word offers wider clauses — *stop*, *open*, *let them go* — and each one arrives muddy with roof-memory, each one promising Backwash deep enough to flatten whatever softness I have left into a stamp. Living minds only still applies. The medics are living. The guards are living. The volunteers are living until the chart says otherwise. A clean sentence could rent a pause. A muddy sentence could rent a floor. I am cold enough now to hear the difference without pretending difference is mercy.

"Move," I say — to Mira, to Suzumori, to the scrape that wants the gallery. Flat. Finished enough. We leave the observation glass the way you leave a grave you did not dig: without speeches, with the dirt still under your nails.

---

East wing proper smells less like alcohol and more like waiting.

War-prisoner holds. Specials. Reinforced quiet. Mei's corridor finally matches the map — E-4, a door with a window slit, a cuff-tag flash on a duty board: Serizawa Mei. Alive. Present. Not on the processing line. Legitimacy still breathing. Mira's equation brightens into extract hunger. Suzumori's humming returns, thin, like wire.

"Tonight," Mira says. "We stage the last minds. We take her before Habu's tablet invents usefulness for a war stamp."

"Before the building invents more zeros," Suzumori adds, grim. "You saw the arithmetic. Do not romanticize delay as caution now. Delay is how kitchens finish their meals."

I nod once. The nod is colder than agreement. Cold is what Backwash leaves when it cannot leave blood. Through a narrow pane I see a girl with short dark hair correcting a junior guard's posture with two fingers and a smile that says numbers belong to her — not my camera long, not extract complete, just confirmation that the who still has a face. We do not kick the door. Rotation wrong by four minutes. Living mind not yet staged. Mira swears once, mildness cracking. I do not swear. Flat mouths save breath for clauses.

A gray-green approaches. I say, "You need to verify the west stair for six minutes," clean, and he needs it the way lungs need air. He goes. Echo. Cloth. The scrape pleased. Somewhere below, medical processing continues its soft gospel without my help. That fact sits in my chest like a coin I cannot spend and cannot throw away.

"You are colder," Mira observes, not unkind. "Good. Cold finishes. Soft hesitates until the chart does."

"Cold is not good," Suzumori says. "Cold is what happens when a Voice watches a state invent death and decides feeling is inefficient. Inefficient feeling is still the only thing that keeps a mouth from becoming only a muzzle."

I do not answer. Answering would invent Haru's dialect — ledger, kitchen, spoon, the soft refusal to call children inventory. Haru may be in this stack. Haru may already smell the vents. If he does, his ideals will bruise. If Riku is with him — road dog, crooked tooth, volunteer wound paid on a roof with sake — the bruise will become a detonation. I file that under later. Later is how I keep moving.

We pull back to a service pocket to reset the stage. Mira redraws faces: which clerk, which camera bank, which radio murmur. Suzumori sits on a crate that is not a crate so much as a pause. I face the wall and let the gallery replay until the replay becomes temperature instead of story. Boy on bench. Chart. Stillness. Soft PA. Zero. Another gown. Another kindness. The Word offers *kill yourself* for every medic in the room and I taste Aihara's garden and refuse the offer — not because I am soft, because Suzumori is right about massacres and filing, and because STAGE 4 wants the offer too much.

Mira speaks into the pause like mild weather inventing a forecast. "When we take her, we do not stop for cups. You saw why cups end. Stopping mid-extract makes more zeros, not fewer. Remnant legitimacy walks out breathing, or this whole approach was theater."

"Theater is what they call kindness," Suzumori says. "Do not become their dialect while opposing them." She hums one thin note at my shoulder. "Your flatness is a door. Doors can lead out of kitchens. Doors can also lead deeper into them. Choose the hallway before you choose the verb."

I almost say *out*. The almost dies flatter than the gallery. Out is still a who and a what and a finished that has not been built clean. Mei first. Then whatever STAGE 4 makes of the rest. The order is Mira's. The grimness is Suzumori's. The cold is mine.

"We know what the building is," I say, flat. "We take Mei. We do not decorate the knowing."

Mira nods. "Faster."

Suzumori hums once, grim assent. "Faster, and still living minds only. Still no mud. If you muddy this, the zeros will include her."

Outside our pocket, Quiet Field continues pretending relocation is a lullaby. Volunteer blocks breathe. Irregular blocks invent sleep or vigilance. East wing holds a girl who smiles at numbers. Medical processing holds a truth that is no longer rumor in my mouth. Backwash sits behind my teeth beside birthday candles and a soft boy's unfinished name.

I wipe blood I earned from staging, not from saving. The distinction is the building's favorite joke. My voice, when I tell Mira to reset the west approach, is flatter than grief, flatter than the gallery, flat enough that even Mira's hunger pauses to listen to the temperature.

"We go again at the next clean window."

We know the building's true arithmetic now. Knowing does not free anyone. Knowing only removes the right to call what we came for a simple theft. Extract is still the who. Disposal is the why the who cannot wait. Cold is the how I have left that still walks.

Deeper floors wait. Mei waits. The glass behind us keeps showing zeros whether we look or not. I do not look again. Looking twice is how unfinished boys turn into sermons. I am done with sermons. I am becoming the schedule that answers kitchens that cook children — and the answer, tonight, is still a name on a duty board, still living, still not taken, still the next clause I will clean until it opens a door without opening a massacre.
