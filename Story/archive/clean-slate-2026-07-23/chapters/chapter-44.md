# Chapter 44 — Clear Day
*POV: Haruki*

Mei peels off before the soot has finished deciding what color morning is.

She does not ask permission. She tells geometry. "Soft boy, cover my tarp debt with Riku. I have a day to spend." Riku clicks his tongue once — partner sound, tuition sound — and looks at me like a man pricing whether my mouth is finally worth the rent Auntie Bazzu charges for not selling children. I nod because nodding is not a Word and because Mei has earned the right to spend a day that is not a corridor count.

The boy who comes for her is warm before he is useful.

Kanda Iori — Remnant runner, Susuichi cover, laughter that does not apologize for existing in smog — bows to Mei the way you bow to a person, not a flag. His hands are clean enough for street food. His eyes are not clean enough for what the war did to them, and that is the honesty that makes my ledger nervous before the day even starts.

"Clear edge," he says. "Food that isn't only debt. Games that aren't only exit maps. If you'll walk with me."

Mei looks at me once. Not asking. Informing. "Don't invent a speech at the gate."

"I won't."

"Good." Almost kind. Same line as the bay. True again.

They go.

---

Riku and I work Auntie Bazzu's morning ledger — crate tally, tarp seam check, a silence-for-hire runner who wants to know if Remnant weather is renting west again. I keep my sticky mouth on living doors and try not to file every agreement as luck. Luck is getting expensive. The soft shelf tips when a warehouse foreman decides our crate count was always correct. Riku watches. Says nothing useful. That is his curriculum when he thinks I am learning something that might keep us alive past a soot Tuesday.

Through tarp gaps Susuichi shows me pieces of a day I am not inside.

Mei and Iori at a skewer stall — soot-light turning their laughter into something almost like Ashiro's ordinary lie. Mei corrects the vendor's change and smiles like numbers were always hers, and Iori steals a extra bite off her stick and she swats his wrist and does not stop smiling. Camera paid without ceremony. Joy that is not performance. My chest does something complicated — relief that she is still a person who can laugh, dread that joy in Susuichi always has a debt attached somewhere.

They invent a bottle-cap game at the clearer edge where kids still play and the haze thins enough to pretend the sky remembers blue. Mei vaults a puddle like she is sixteen and not a war-prisoner file with scarred knuckles. Iori loses on purpose once and wins honestly twice and the honesty is the point. Soot-light catches in his hair. For an hour the market sounds like a town that decided law was a joke and forgot to punish itself for laughing.

I am on a roof lip mapping exits when the heart-to-heart finds me.

Not because I hunt it. Because Susuichi's tarps are thin and the clearer edge is quiet in the way serious talk requires quiet, and Mei’s voice carries when she is finished being fearless for other people's benefit. I sit two seams over with a debt slate in my lap like I belong to geometry, and I listen because leaving would be louder than staying.

"I wanted to be useful without being inventory," Mei says. Flat. Warm underneath. "Volunteer forms lie. Prisoner forms lie worse. Shizuhara taught me the difference between a tally and a throat."

"Zero taught you faster," Iori says. No flinch. Pain under the warmth like a second coat.

"Zero taught me the Federation's mercy was always a ledger. Vents don't apologize." She is quiet a beat. "I don't forgive the building. I don't forgive the boy who screamed my confession into every speaker while children were still —" She stops. Starts again. Cleaner. "Remnant says the Voice is a spoon the powerless were waiting for. Keeper says the spoon is a season you don't get to hold wrong. I say living first. Then flags. Then whatever kitchen still has pulses."

"And the loud one?"

"Weather I can't un-hear." Mei's laugh is small and not amused. "He reshaped a warfare I didn't finish naming. My ideals got spent in his throat. I still know why people grab the spoon. I grabbed you today because grabbing is allowed before dusk."

Iori is quiet. Then: "I walk his road. Not because he asked nicely. Because FDC made Silence and Silence made graves and Remnant was the first kitchen that didn't pretend the graves were weather." A breath. "I won't apologize for joy. I also won't pretend joy pays the debt. Today is sincere. Tonight is schedule."

"Honest," Mei says. "That's rarer than Echo."

Below us, a child shouts at the bottle-cap game. Ordinary invents itself for three breaths. I write nothing on my slate. Some pages you don't balance until after the knife.

---

Dusk arrives flat.

Not storm-flat. Stage-flat — the particular weather Kaito wears when the hollow behind his teeth has already chosen the verb and the room is only waiting for the grammar to catch up. I feel him before I see him: twin metal, pad bruise, massacre handwriting still stuck in my ears from a stolen handset. Riku's hand finds my shoulder on the roof. Click of tongue. Exit map becoming real-time.

At the clearer edge, Mei and Iori stand with street food grease on their fingers and a day still alive on their faces.

Kaito comes up the alley with two runners and no speech. Mira is not on his shoulder. That is somehow worse. He stops like a door that has finished opening. Flat. Finished enough.

"We're moving out."

Three words. Schedule dressed as weather. The day shivers — not smog, not Echo — the shiver of a joy that just learned it was always a ledger column on someone else's page.

Iori does not perform surprise. That is the second knife.

"You knew," Mei says. Not a question. Her voice stays level one beat too long — geometry holding before the crack.

"Amatsu civil heir," Iori says. "Remnant brief. Elder cup. Split spoons and a flag that prefers living." He looks at her with the same warmth he wore at the skewer stall. No mask dropped. No mask found. "I was honest. The honesty was the hook. The day was still real."

"You spent it anyway."

"I spent it anyway." No apology in his mouth. "I don't apologize for joy. I won't apologize for the road I took. He—" chin tip toward Kaito, recognition not worship "—is the king of that road. Voice. Weather. Whatever word you want. I walk it."

King. The word hits my sternum like a coin that finally admits what it spends. Kaito's jaw does not move. STAGE 5 does not need to perform surprise either. The hollow behind his teeth purrs at furniture with crowns.

Mei’s scarred knuckles whiten. "You made me laugh."

"I know."

"You let me talk about zero like it was a kitchen I could name."

"I know."

"Next time we meet," Mei says, and her voice is still level and that is the most frightening thing in Susuichi all week, "it's as enemies."

Iori bows — person-bow, not flag-bow. "Next time. Enemies."

Kaito's eyes cut to me once. Not reunion. Inventory. The loud boy and the quiet boy on opposite tarps of the same split inheritance, and his gaze says he has filed me under *later* the way Habu filed children under *zero*. He does not soften. Softening would be unfinished.

"Collect's done," he says. Flat. Brief. No pad. No *Clear* clause. No clash that shakes soot off warehouse teeth. Just schedule completing itself in a alley where bottle caps still litter the clearer edge like evidence joy happened. He turns. Runners turn. Weather moving out.

Iori goes with them. One look back — not pleading. Not cruel. The look you give a good meal you know is the last free one on a road that eats people. Then pine-shadow Remnant cover swallows him.

---

Mei returns to the tarp room shattered in the particular way glass shatters — still one piece, wrong light inside it.

She sits on the crate edge and does not cry. Crying would be a softer verb than what she is doing. She is re-counting a day that was real and a dusk that was also real and finding the arithmetic does not cancel. Riku leaves the room without being asked. Curriculum: give people one kitchen at a time.

I sit across from her with the ledger open and nothing useful to write.

"He knew," she says finally.

"Yes."

"The day was still —" She stops. Starts. "I would not give the laughter back. That is what makes it a knife."

I understand. I understand in the way I understood hearing my Minato confession come back through Habu's vents with a heavier hand. Sincerity does not protect you from schedules. Joy does not erase the elder's cup. Honest hooks still pierce.

"You don't owe me an apology for watching," she says, blunt warm, eyes dry. "You owe me the truth that the day was real before he arrived. Say it."

"It was real," I say. "Skewers. Bottle caps. You laughed like fear was boring."

"Good." She exhales. "Soft boy."

I wait.

"My turn to be honest," I say. The words come slower than sticky doors. "When he looked at me — inventory. Same shelf Habu used. Same metal taste. I wanted to say *stop*." I touch my tongue. "Not panic file. Not pad echo. Aimed. I didn't. But I wanted to. And wanting felt like owning."

Mei watches me like a corridor count that has decided the tally includes a new column.

"Owning is work," she says.

"I know."

"You don't." Almost kind. Third time still true. "But you're closer than luck."

Outside, Susuichi breathes coal-sweet indifference. Remnant weather moves west. Chinmoku will smell Voices in a town that laughs at paper. Somewhere a mute hunter prices Shizuhara's dead as evidence. My mouth sits in my skull like a spoon I can no longer file under charisma and apologize for later.

Mei wipes street food grease off her knuckles with mechanical precision. Riku's footsteps return on the tarp stair — partner without the word, exits checked, dawn debt still unpaid. I leave the ledger open on the crate. Open is the only gesture I trust tonight that isn't a command.

The day was joy. The dusk was theft with manners. Both entries stand. I do not close my eyes and call it rest. I watch the door because soft jobs are still jobs, and aftermath is a weather you survive by keeping the page open.
