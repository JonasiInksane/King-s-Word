# Chapter 36 — Service Road
*POV: Haruki*

They drag me out through a loading bay that still smells like trucks and unfinished escape.

Gravel bites through thin prison soles. Night air hits like a door that forgot it was supposed to be locked — colder than recycled mercy, honest in the way Habu's fluorescents never were. Behind us Shizuhara Detention Complex holds its completed quiet and its unfinished storm. Ahead the service road runs inland between chain-link and scrub, a gray ribbon that thinks it is nothing. Riku's arm is iron under my shoulders. Mei's scarred knuckles are a second opinion on my wrist. I am cargo that remembered it has legs and then forgot again every third step. Useless still fits. Flee still leans. The empty blocks still sit behind my eyes like a second film.

"Don't look back as a hobby," Mei says, blunt warm, breath steady. "Looking once is information. Looking twice is how you grow roots in a graveyard."

I look once.

The complex's upper floors stutter with lockdown amber and the particular wrongness of a Voice writing adult bodies into endings, not naps. I do not see him. I feel him — twin metal, pad bruise, the angry weather of a boy who watched vents finish children and answered by spending my Minato confession through every speaker until the building shook. Mira's mildness is a rumor at his shoulder even from here. They stayed. Of course they stayed. Remnant math and scheduled rage prefer the building that wronged them. We are the ones on gravel. The ledger accepts both entries if I don't shake the page until it tears — and I am still shaking from hearing myself come back with his heavier hand.

Riku spits blood into ditch weed and keeps walking. "Your jacket's painting the place. Good. Bills should be loud."

"He's—" I start, and stop, because *he's becoming* is a sentence I have said too many ways since a plum tree, and tonight the sentence has my words in it, roared angrier than I ever dared while contractors laughed.

"He's weather," Riku says. "We're road. Different jobs."

A phone tower blinks on a ridge. Somewhere a soft lockdown chyron is already learning our dead wrong. Mei steals a dock guard's handset before we clear the last camera angle — not for calls, for proof of the lie in progress. She tilts the screen so I have to see it if I want to keep having eyes: *Remnant terror incident.* *Anomalous vocal actor.* *Volunteer casualties under investigation.* Habu's soft confession does not appear. Inventory zero does not appear as protocol. The river we opened appears as weather aimed by a mouth that did not vent them. The feeds are already lying with good handwriting.

"Don't learn your dead from that," Mei says again, and this time the again is mercy. "Learn them from the fact you can still hate the font."

I hate the font.

I hate my sticky doors. I hate Habu's binder. I hate the overlap that made open into a road into a gun. I hate that Kaito stayed and that staying makes a kind of terrible sense. I hate that I am fleeing while massacre climate writes itself into waxed floors. Ideals lie vain in my chest like a dropped spoon — and still Mei and Riku drag me, because living first includes soft boys who want to become statues as apology.

We cut off the ribbon onto a narrower access track — tire ruts, puddle sky, the inland dark Takamatsu pines would recognize as cousin. No bird waits. No humming grandmother. Suzumori's extract thread went elsewhere without the girl currently hauling my useless weight; I infer it the way I inferred Worded guards from doors that opened without our keys. Parallel plans. Failed numbers. Same locks that learned they were never the building's until the building reminded us who owns vents.

A siren tries the distance and fails to find us — verification weather aimed at the complex, not yet at three irregulars on a ditch road. Riku times our pauses to the blink of the ridge tower like a man who has practiced not being a silhouette. Mei counts camera poles the way she counted corridor tallies: as if numbers were always hers to finish. I count breaths because breaths are the only column that still adds without becoming a stamp. One. Two. The third catches on empty blocks and has to be dragged forward like the rest of me.

"If you freeze again," Riku says without looking, "I'll invent a tuition fee."

"Bill me," I say, and for half a second the old bar-road cadence almost lives — soft boy, crooked tooth, Tuesday arithmetic — before zero sits down between us again and the cadence remembers it is aftermath.

My mouth tastes metal.

Not only horror. The other dialect — panic file, refusal file, pad skid, the soft shelf that tips when I say things and people decide helping me was their idea. I still do not own it. Owning would mean accepting Mira's two Voices as something other than a knife drawer I refuse to open. Tonight I file it under *yet*. *Reshaped by whom?* Yet. Yet is gravel. Yet is two people refusing to let me freeze. Yet is a coin I still cannot spend while chyrons spend our dead for us.

"Say something sticky," Riku mutters when headlights ghost far off on a parallel road — verification weather, maybe, or ordinary night traffic that hasn't learned our names. "If a mouth shows up, rent it. Don't invent a speech. Invent a turn."

I nod. Sticky I can do. Speeches get stamped. Turns keep you unpaid by Habu's manners.

Mei glances at me sideways — weighing hinge against weight, the same look she used when she corrected a corridor count and smiled like numbers were always hers. No borrowed grave-names. No easy engine stolen from a roof I still carry. Just present tense: "You open your mouth again, soft boy, you aim it at living doors we need. Not at your guilt. Guilt doesn't unlock cuffs."

"I know."

"You don't," she says, almost kind. "But you will walk like you might."

We walk.

Behind us the complex's amber stutter thickens — gunfire thinning into the quieter wrongness of grammar doing what rifles do slower. I do not hear his clauses. I do not need to. A loud boy becoming hollow. A quiet boy refusing the name of his own mouth. A service road and a lie on a stolen screen and three bodies leaning inland while whatever comes next waits like weather that has not introduced itself.

From a birthday roof to a pad. From a mansion lock to a van to Habu's glass lobby and a child called inventory without a tone change. From Mei's corrected tally to disposal truth to open locks and a river and zero. From useless knees to gravel. From ideals roared in Minato to ideals vain on wax to ideals that have to learn feet again or admit Habu's soft voice won the kitchen.

The road remains. Riku's crooked tooth catches what little light the ridge tower offers. Mei's knuckle scar is a pale hyphen on the night. My hands are open because open is the only gesture I have left that isn't a stamp.

Far behind, Shizuhara's PA stays spent and ambiguous. Far behind, Mira stays beside a flatter voice through massacre climate. Far behind, children stay zero and feeds stay liars. Ahead, the service road bends around scrub and a ditch that smells like wet iron and ordinary weeds. No sleep. Sleep would be a closed eye calling itself peace. We have not earned peace. We have earned motion, and an overheard lie on a small bright screen, and the knowledge — unfinished, unpaid — that the louder boy stayed in the weather he became while we dragged the quieter one into dark that is only not-there.

Riku fist-knocks my shoulder once, partner without the word. "Survive the road."

"That's my line," I try, and it comes out thin, and thin is still a sound, and sound is still not Habu's completed form.

Mei steals one more glance at the handset. The chyron refreshes. Same lie. Better font. She kills the screen with her thumb like killing a mild insect.

"Part of the world just decided what tonight meant," she says. "We don't have to agree. We have to get far enough that disagreement has a door."

Inland dark takes the next bend.

Wind moves scrub like a rumor. Somewhere a freight line clicks a distant argument. The handset in Mei's pocket stays dead by choice; the lie does not need our attention to keep running. I think of Habu's soft voice on intake day — *inventory* — and of Habu's soft voice on the PA — *zero* — and of Habu's soft voice cut mid-sentence into ambiguity, and I cannot tell which version the world will keep. Haunt seed. Hauntings open later. Tonight is gravel and haul and a soft boy learning that fleeing is not the opposite of witnessing.

Eyes up. Feet moving. Word unnamed. Coin unspent. Kitchen spoon waiting somewhere that is not a vented corridor. Behind us a complex finishes learning blood dialect. Ahead something else will try to name us — Silence, Remnant, Federation fonts, a still man with a phone who asked *whom* and left a coin that still won't spend. I do not sleep toward it. I walk. Riku bills the walking later. Mei prices the dark as geometry. I am still here — not the chyron's version, not Habu's completed form, not the version that makes him only a monster or me only a statue.

The service road does not sleep. The story turns toward the next weather without asking permission, gravel under three sets of soles, an overheard lie already faster than breath, and three living pulses refusing to become inventory one more bend at a time.
