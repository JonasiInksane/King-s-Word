# Chapter 45 — Hollow Practice
*POV: Kaito*

Collect is brief. The hollow is not.

Susuichi's smog alley learns *we're moving out* and learns nothing else from my mouth — no pad, no reshape, no confession stolen from a bar and spent through vents. Schedule completing itself. Mei standing with street food on her fingers and a face that has not yet shattered because geometry holds one beat longer than glass. Iori beside me, warm coat still smelling like clearer-edge laughter, honesty still stuck in his teeth like a hook he refuses to spit.

Good. Honest hooks pierce cleaner than lies. Mira's equation said so. The elder's cup said so. The Word said nothing useful because the Word stopped asking permission three massacres ago.

We walk back through tarp dark toward Remnant cover west of the bruise. Runners ahead. Iori quiet — not sullen, *full*. The quiet of a boy who spent a joy day he will not apologize for and watched it become dusk anyway. I do not offer comfort. Comfort is a soft verb. Soft verbs are how people die in rooms that set cups like verdicts.

Mira meets us at the pine line with a handset that has already learned new lies. *Remnant terror.* *Anomalous vocal actor.* She does not show me the screen unless I look — equation courtesy. I look. The font is good handwriting on a kitchen fire. Habu's zero stays absent. My reshape stays present. Inventory weather for a world that prefers one villain spoon.

"You painted the sky," she says. Mild. Not accusation. Confirmation. "Chinmoku will hunt soot now. Federation will hunt Chinmoku hunting soot. We rent the confusion."

"I didn't come to paint," I say. Flat. "I came to collect."

"Collect is also weather." Her eyes flick to Iori. "Did the day hold?"

Iori nods once. "She laughed. She meant the enemies line. I meant it too."

"Good." Mira's mildness almost touches something like pride and decides against it. Pride is soft. "The elder wanted pulse, not a muzzle. Pulse delivered. Next pass is harder."

Kurohata's pine finds us before the shed row does. Resin and machine oil and incense pretending it is older than diesel. Black flags move like rumors that have learned to kill politely.

---

Practice is not training. Training implies you might stop.

Mira has cleared the textile floor — mats, a kettle that will not finish boiling, a chalk line that means *living minds only* and never means what FDC thinks it means. STAGE 5 sits behind my teeth like furniture that has memorized the room. Intent before grammar. The lesson the massacre wrote in wax I can still taste when I speak too hard.

"Again," Mira says. Mild. Equation-bright. "Not the words. The want behind them."

I look at the runner on the mat — volunteer from a shed I do not ask the name of because names are hooks and hooks are for flags. Living mind. Willing. Eyes empty of heroism. Good.

I want him to sit.

I do not say *sit*. I hold the want like a finished clause behind my teeth and let the hollow aim it.

He sits. Hard. Knees crack. His breath jumps. Echo paints under my nose and I wipe it because maintenance is not manners anymore — it is the only way the blood dialect does not become the only language I speak.

"Cleaner," Mira says. Pleased is a small thing on her face, like a stamp that finally found the right pad. "Grammar is weather. Intent is ownership. Habu owned vents because he wanted zero finished. You wanted a boy on a mat. The mat learned."

Suzumori sits on the crate by the door with shoes neat and humming offline. She watches the runner stand when I release the want — not a command, a withdrawal of aim — and her eyes are keeper-old and refuse to bless what just happened.

"Again," she says. Not to me. To the air. "Again is how hollows grow rooms."

Mira's mildness does not look at her. "Keeper law is for seasons. We are in inventory weather."

"We are always in someone's inventory," Suzumori says. "The difference is whether your mouth admits it."

Second round. Third. Intent before grammar until the runner's knees learn my wants before my mouth invents verbs.

I want him to forget the last thirty seconds.

He blinks. Frowns. The frown is human — good, human costs something — and then his face smooths wrong, like a ledger line erased without understanding what was written.

"Stop," Mira says. Sharp for her. "Not erase. That's Chinmoku's dream. We don't unmake. We aim."

I release. The runner shakes once and sits back down without being asked, which is worse in a different direction. I taste metal. Frustration. Backwash wearing patience thin.

Fourth round. I want him to remember his name.

He opens his mouth. Nothing comes. His eyes go wide — panic, not heroism — and for one breath I feel the old roof, the old leverage, grammar failing when the world still says no. I release before the want becomes a cut. The runner gasps his name like air. Good. Living minds cost something.

"Intent without mercy is Habu's font," Suzumori says from the crate. "Intent without keeper law is yours."

"Phantom," Suzumori says quietly.

I freeze. Not the runner's freeze. Mine.

She means the word that slipped out of the practice air when I aimed too hard — or the word that slipped in. *Stop!* Not my panic. Not my grammar. His. Pad bruise. Twin frequency. The quiet boy's refusal filed as weather in my skull since a mansion roof shook and a city learned two Voices could disagree loud enough to shudder.

It came out of me like an echo that forgot who originated it.

The runner flinches at *Stop!* — not at me. At the frequency. Twin metal in the shed air though only one Voice is aiming. His body learned the pad clash before his mind did. Mira heard it too. I see it in the equation — recalculation, pleasure, something colder than pleasure underneath. "Two spoons," she says softly. "Even the hollow knows."

I try again. Intent only. I want him to walk to the door.

He walks — and the phantom rides the want, *Stop!* bleeding through like rust through water, and he freezes mid-step, one foot lifted, a living mind caught between my aim and his echo of my brother-spoon's refusal. The shed holds its breath. Suzumori's bell does not ring. Mira's eyes brighten — hunter math, not keeper mercy.

"Release," she says.

I release. He stumbles. Sits. Shakes. I taste blood dialect and something worse — complicity. His freeze is my freeze filed in another skull.

"That," Suzumori says, "is why Silence hunts."

Suzumori stands. Shoes neat. Bell on the crate untouched — reminder, not gift. "The Word is wearing you," she says. Not metaphor. Inventory. "STAGE 5 is not a crown. It is a room that stopped asking who furnishes it. You furnish it with anger. With reshape. With a boy's phantom *stop* that isn't his anymore and isn't yours cleanly either. That is hollow."

"Hollow," I repeat. Flat. Tasting it. The hollow behind my teeth purrs at the name like a cat that has finally been called what it is.

"Practice without keeper law is how seasons break," Suzumori says. "You collected a girl who spent a day learning she could laugh. Theft with manners. The boy Iori won't apologize. Good. Joy that apologizes is already Remnant liturgy." Her eyes find mine. "But you — you arrived flat and brief and correct. That is also hollow. The king of his road does not need to shout. The shouting is already inside the room."

Mira almost smiles. "She's not wrong. That's annoying."

"I am frequently annoying," Suzumori says. "I remain correct."

I look at Iori on the bench by the kettle. He has not spoken since Susuichi. Warm face dimmed — not extinguished. Dimmed like a stove turned to coals so the next meal can still happen. He meets my eyes. Recognition. No worship. No plea.

"The day was real," he says. Quiet. "She said next time enemies. I meant it."

"I know."

"I won't apologize for joy."

"I didn't ask you to."

"Good." He looks at his hands. Street food grease still faint under the nails. "She laughed like fear was boring. That is —" He stops. Starts. "That is worth a scar. Even if the scar is schedule."

"Would you spend the day again?" I ask. Not soft. Inventory.

Iori thinks. The thinking is honest too. "Yes. Even knowing dusk. Even knowing she'll hate me right until she doesn't." A breath. "Maybe especially."

"King of the road asks no apology," I say. Flat. Testing whether the title fits my mouth.

"King of the road didn't ask me to lie with my joy," Iori says. "That is why I walk it."

Mira's shoulder brushes mine — alignment. "The elder got her pulse on the road. Legitimacy prefers living flags. Next pass is harder. Chinmoku is sniffing soot. The soft one is still in the market with the road dog and the girl who now knows what collect tastes like." Her voice drops half a register — almost kindness, almost knife. "He is closer to owning. That makes him dangerous to us and to Silence both. Good. Danger is a rent Remnant can pay."

"Harvest language," Suzumori says.

"Inventory language," Mira corrects. "Same kitchen. Different ladle."

Two spoons. One flag. One king of a road I did not ask to own.

Practice again. Intent before grammar. I want the runner to stand. He stands. I want him to walk to the door. He walks — phantom quiet this time, no bleed, and Mira's pleased is a full stamp now, not almost. "Grammar is for boys who still think the world listens to sentences," she says. "Intent is for kings."

Kings. Iori watches from the bench. Suzumori watches from the crate. The runner watches the door like it is the only exit that ever mattered. I do not feel like a king. I feel like furniture that has learned to aim itself.

Phantom *Stop!* lingers in the shed air anyway — ghost grammar, brother frequency, the quiet spoon's refusal wearing my hollow like a coat that isn't mine and fits anyway.

Suzumori does not hum. Mira is pleased. Iori is quiet after a day that was sincere before dusk. The Word wears me and I wear the Word and the difference between those sentences is getting too thin to price.

Outside, black flags move like rumors. Somewhere east a soft boy is learning that watching aftermath is also ownership. Somewhere a mute hunter writes Shizuhara's dead on a slate and calls it creed. Somewhere a still man with a festival laugh waits for *whom* to stop being a coin and start being a knife.

I do not sleep toward any of them. The hollow practices. The furniture stays. Pine and oil and Echo and a bell that will not bless murder. Schedule says tomorrow is weather. The phantom says *stop* in a voice that is not mine alone anymore.

I wipe blood from under my nose and practice wanting without naming it, and the room learns.
