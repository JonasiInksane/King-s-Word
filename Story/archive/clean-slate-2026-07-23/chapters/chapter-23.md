# Chapter 23 — Lift
*POV: Kaito*

The H-pad is wind and rotor-wash and a city laid out like an argument.

Concrete under my boots still holds the day's heat. Rotor-wash tastes like fuel and rain that hasn't decided to fall. Takamatsu tilts below the mansion roof — tram lines like stitches that never learned to close a wound, harbor rumor two ridges away, soft lockdown holding the streets in a polite fist that just felt a garden room become a closed file. Behind my teeth the clause that killed Aihara is still warm. Ahead, the bird. Between them, a soft boy climbing stairs like unfinished business. The bird is already waking, blades finding their scream the way sentences find targets. Suzumori stands small and calm near the skid, humming under the noise — the only person on the roof who does not flinch when my mouth starts working. Mira counts exits with her eyes: stair, service hatch, gray-green approaches that will thicken once the hill understands inventory has been rewritten. The pilot is already ours; I asked him to be, clean and finished, living mind only. Echo runs freely now. I don't bother wiping it. Blood is a dialect. The roof learns it. STAGE 4's door is open somewhere behind my teeth; I do not name it. Naming is Haru's job. Mine is sentences.

Haru bursts onto the pad with Riku half a step behind, both of them looking like a Tuesday that refused to die. Soft boy. Road dog. No Gorō — good. One less person to turn into collateral. The garden room sits under our feet like a closed file. Aihara's tea is cooling in a world that has already moved on to rotors. Haru's face is ledger and panic and something metal under the skin that he will refuse to own. Riku's crooked tooth is a threat aimed at the whole sky.

"Don't," Haru says, which is rich coming from him — unfinished again, a mirror of the hallway, as if the word could be a leash if he throws it first. Soft boy learning my dialect without admitting the lesson.

"Sit down," I tell Riku, clean — who, what, finished — and Riku's body drops like a cut string, cursing me with his eyes, fists still inventing a fight his knees won't fund. "Stay."

The scrape in my throat answers like a second tongue pleased to be used. Riku fights the stay the way men fight tide — visible, useless, dignified. His shoulders shake. His mouth shapes debts. Good. Debts mean he's still himself under the grammar. Haru keeps coming. Of course he does. Soft boy. Ledger. Same side of the water until he wasn't. Until Mira. Until Jun. Until a market almost and a garden corpse and a word that means free the way erase means free. Until *he's in the way* landed in my ear and I didn't flinch, and the not-flinching scared me more than the body in the chair, and I walked toward this roof anyway because calendars do not pause for proof.

We shout in the same breath.

"**Clear the pad!**" — who, what, finished, aimed at him like weather.

"**Stop!**" — raw, unfinished, aimed at me like a plum-tree ghost that finally found a throat.

Something in the air *hits*.

Not clean. Not empty eyes. Not the polite emptying Aihara did in a cardigan while koi circled and tea cooled. Two living-mind commands collide mid-pad — mine polished enough to schedule a lieutenant, his a naked *stop* with no object finished, and the Word does not know which spoon owns the roof. His clause skids into mine. Mine skids into his. Neither lands. Pressure without obedience. A frequency meeting itself and refusing to be one voice. For a fraction of a second I taste his panic the way Echo tastes metal — twin, wrong, intimate — and then the fraction becomes physics.

I push again without meaning to — not a second sentence, just intent leaning on *clear* — and he is still *stopping* me with the same raw syllable burning in the wash, and the skid deepens. The Word wants a clear pad. The soft boy wants the world to halt before the bird leaves. Neither gets what it wants cleanly. Rotor-wash flattens our clothes. Blood paints both our faces like matching stamps from a clerk who has a sense of humor. Suzumori's eyes open for one beat, bright and old, and then close again as if looking too long at split inheritance is a kind of rudeness.

The city below us shudders.

Windows. Trams. A pressure like two radios on the same station fighting for the song, neither willing to become static. Somewhere a shutter slams like a fist learning manners too late. Somewhere a phone's soft lockdown language flickers as if the polite fist clenched harder and forgot it was polite. Suzumori's humming spikes sharp — not fear, recognition, the pitch of a kept thing hearing a split she has always known in her bones. Mira's mildness cracks into awe and calculation at once. The pilot flinches hard enough that I feel the bird lean on its skids. Gray-green on the stair hesitates the way soldiers hesitate when the weather stops being weather.

"Two," Mira breathes. "Two Voices."

Haru doesn't look like he understands. He looks like panic and refusal wearing a uniform that was never issued — nose blooming red, eyes wide, the shape of *Stop!* still on his mouth as if the ledger can veto grammar by shouting first. I feel the scrape in my throat answer his like a twin wound. Blood for blood. Echo for echo. The One Throne, if Suzumori's old world is true, was never meant to hold this argument. Split inheritance. Untrained. Unknowing. He files the shout under panic. I can see the filing on his face. Soft boy refusing the knife drawer Mira just opened with two words — even after he fired one of the knives himself.

I want to stay. I want to finish a gentler clause — *come with me*, *eat*, *I'm sorry* in a language that can't kill. The Word offers none of those cleanly. Softness has been getting quieter since Nagihama. Sentences have been getting louder since the quiet floors. The door behind my teeth does not close because I want a reunion. Doors like this close when you stop using them, and I am not ready to stop. Stopping would mean putting the gun down. Putting the gun down would mean admitting the gun is me.

"Get on the bird," Mira snaps at me. "Now. Before the hill grows a regiment."

Gray-green spills onto the pad — boots, rifles, the polite fist becoming a hand with fingers, asset posture waking into capture math. I say, "Sleep," at the ones who can hear me, and bodies fold like the command was a mercy they didn't request. Not Haru. Not Riku — *stay* still has him nailed to Tuesday. Not Suzumori, who hums through blood-scent and wind as if humming were a kind of earplug for the soul. Folded soldiers are not an ending. They are a cleared path. I grab Suzumori's wrist. Her small hand is dry. Mira hauls me toward the rotor scream. The bird wants sky. The hill wants inventory. I want a sentence that means *Haru* without meaning *location*. The Word offers *clear* again and tastes his *stop* still ringing in the concrete and skids again. Good. Let it skid. Let the city shake. Let Mira's equation rewrite itself mid-lift.

Haru's mouth shapes my name — after *Stop!*, after the collision, a plea or a curse or the first line of a confession he started telling someone before any of this had teeth. Riku fights my *stay* like a man who will invoice the tide later, crooked tooth red, eyes promising a bill. The skids leave concrete. Wash becomes world. Takamatsu tilts. Below, the mansion becomes a tooth in a jaw that just bit its own tongue. Suzumori's humming steadies into something older than rotors. Mira stares at me with a new hunger — not for a tool, for an equation that just proved it has two sides and a public classroom.

"He countered you," Mira says to me over the wash — eyes on my face, equation-bright. "Same breath. Untrained. Unknowing. Split inheritance."

Then she turns toward the humming beside us. "Suzumori—"

"I know," Suzumori says, eyes closed, humming through the lift. "I have always known. The tragedy is that they are learning in public."

I look down until Haru is a white face and then a speck and then the city again — lanterns, teeth, a garden room that filed a lieutenant into obedience and then into silence. My voice, when I answer Mira, is flatter than grief. Flatter than the boy who used to make whole cars warm by laughing. The flatness is the door. I walk through it because the alternative is looking at him until the bird falls, and falling would be unfinished, and unfinished is how we kill what we meant to save.

"Keep flying."

Wind. Metal. Birthday candles somewhere in the taste of Echo. A hand opening on a roof that is no longer this roof. Jun under a plum tree. Aihara in a cardigan. Haru's *Stop!* colliding with my *clear* like weather arguing with weather. Mira's burn-scar memory stays offstage where it belongs; what stays onstage is her new hunger, equation-bright, already rewriting the mission around two Voices instead of one useful mouth. Suzumori's hum threads the wash like a kept thing reminding the air it has manners. I do not look at the soft boy again. Looking would be unfinished. The bird takes us into dark that isn't safety — Remnant dark, Mira's dark, the dark of a schedule that has learned my handwriting — and behind my teeth the Word sits awake, pleased, already offering the next clean clause for a world that just learned it has two Voices and no idea which spoon to fear.

Keep flying. The city shrinks. The argument continues below us without my mouth. That is not mercy. That is only altitude. My voice stays flat when Mira asks something else I do not answer. Flat is the door. The bird is the hallway. Haru is the unfinished *Stop!* falling away into teeth and lanterns and a lock I did not schedule and cannot unsay from the sky. STAGE 4 does not need my naming to keep the door open. The Word names enough for both of us.
