# Chapter 25 — West of the Pad
*POV: Kaito*

West of the pad is not a direction. It is a temperature.

The bird puts us down in Remnant dark that smells like diesel and cold rice and a cause that has learned to keep its lights low. Concrete billet. Curtains that pretend windows are optional. A table with maps that have been folded too many times and a kettle that never finishes boiling because someone always needs the plug for a radio. Mira's people move like punctuation — short, useful, already rewriting the evening around a city that learned it has two Voices in public. I wipe Echo with the back of my wrist and do not look at the smear. Looking would be unfinished. Flatness sits behind my teeth where warmth used to keep a room. STAGE 4's door is open. I do not name it. Naming is still Haru's job. Mine is the next clean clause.

Suzumori sits on a crate with her shoes neat and her humming small, as if the rotor-wash never happened and the pad never split a frequency across a hill. Mira checks exits with her eyes the way other people check weather: service stair, alley cut, gray-green rumor that will thicken once Takamatsu finishes filing its broken tooth. Someone offers rice. Someone offers a cloth. Someone asks if the loud one needs a medic. I say, "No," and the no is flat enough that the asker decides hunger is safer than curiosity. Good. Curiosity is how soft boys invent rescues. Flat is how tools keep schedules.

Aihara's tea cools somewhere east of my last sentence. The clause that killed him is still warm behind my molars, polite as a calendar. Haru's face on the pad — blood, refusal, the skid that should not have existed — sits in the same mouth and refuses to become a file I can close. *Two Voices,* Mira said, equation-bright. Soft boy filing it under panic. Soft boy tearing a step forward against *clear the pad* like ledgers can veto grammar. I wanted a gentler clause. The Word offered none. Altitude offered distance. Distance is not mercy. Distance is only the bird agreeing to keep flying.

"Sit," Mira tells a runner who is vibrating with report-need, and the runner sits because Mira's mildness has always been a kind of verb. She looks at me. Mild. Hungry in the new way. "Your voice."

"It's working," I say.

"It's flatter." Not accusation. Inventory. She wipes my face again anyway, cloth pink at the corner, the same motion she has made since Kuroishi taught her that blood is a dialect tools leave on floors. "Dependence likes flat. Flat doesn't argue with the next sentence."

Suzumori's eyes open a slit. "Doors like that do not close because a boy wishes for a reunion. They close when the mouth stops needing them." She hums once, soft, not blessing, not curse — a kept thing reminding the air it has manners. "Yours still needs."

I almost apologize. The almost dies unfinished, which feels like Haru's handwriting wearing my throat. Softness has been getting quieter since Nagihama. Sentences have been getting louder since the quiet floors. The not-flinching at *he's in the way* scared me more than the body in the chair, and I walked to the roof anyway because calendars do not pause for proof. Proof is still bleeding under my nose. Proof is a soft boy locked under a hill while Remnant dark teaches me to speak like a stamp.

---

They give us a room with two cots and a map of inland roads that pretend farms are geography instead of cover stories. Mira pins Shizuhara with a dull nail. Not a farm. A modern stack: glass lobby, fluorescent corridors, cellblocks that invent mercy by calling it processing. She says the name like a equation that already has an answer.

"Shizuhara Detention Complex," Mira says. "Multi-floor. A and B for volunteers. C for irregulars. East wing for specials. Medical processing above the quiet arithmetic. Official cover is relocation. Real function is silence that can be zeroed."

Suzumori does not flinch at silence. She flinches at zeroed, the way keepers flinch when language pretends erasing is filing. "The next extract is there."

"Who," I say — not because I don't know she has a name ready, because who is how I keep sentences from becoming mud.

"Serizawa Mei," Suzumori says. Small. Calm. The name lands like a bell that refuses to be dramatic. "War-prisoner hold. Eastern front paperwork. The Remnant needs her living and out. Legitimacy is a hungry animal; she is the meal it will accept without choking." She does not say heir. She does not say throne. She says need the way seasons say rain. "Federation will not keep a girl like that forever without deciding she is either useful or inventory. Useful girls get rooms. Inventory gets arithmetic."

Mei. A name without a face yet. A legitimacy target spoken like a delivery, not a crown. Mira's eyes sharpen — not surprise, alignment. Extract math rewriting itself around a new who. I taste metal and do not ask what legitimacy means when the One Throne was never meant to hold an argument between two boys on a pad. Asking would be unfinished. Unfinished is how we kill what we meant to save.

"Living minds only," I say, because the lesson has teeth now. "Doors don't listen. Cameras don't listen. Dead things don't."

"Good," Mira says. "Keep it clean. Mud is how we lose floors."

Suzumori hums. "Clean is not kind. Clean is only precise. Precision can still be a muzzle if you aim it at the wrong throat."

I do not answer. Answering would invent a speech about Jun under a plum tree and a wish that never finished and a cardigan that learned obedience before silence. The billet kettle clicks uselessly. Outside, Remnant dark holds its breath the way soft lockdown holds a city — polite fist, different logo.

---

Night in a billet is not vigil. Night in a billet is rehearsal.

I sit facing the wall the way I sat facing walls before Takamatsu taught me gardens can be schedules. Mira sharpens times into faces: which intake clerk must remember a false transfer, which service corridor must invent a cigarette break, which radio must murmur routine while gray-green looks the wrong way. Suzumori listens with her eyes closed, humming under the plan like a second map. Echo runs freely when I practice small clauses into my sleeve — *look away*, *take five minutes*, *forget the side stair* — living minds only, unfinished on purpose so the Word does not invent corpses out of chores. Flatness likes practice. Flatness does not ask if Haru is sleeping under a bulb that hums like a cheaper cousin of Suzumori's pitch. I ask anyway, privately, uselessly, and the asking does not warm the room.

He countered me. Untrained. Unknowing. Split inheritance learning in public while a soft boy files thunder under panic. I can still taste his refusal the way Echo tastes metal — twin, wrong, intimate. Mira called it two Voices. I called it keep flying. Keep flying is not forgiveness. Keep flying is altitude pretending to be a decision.

At the edge of the billet window, for half a breath that might be tiredness and might be rewrite caution, a coat-shaped stillness refuses to soften when a runner laughs too loud. Not a face. Not a name. The temperature of a Rail Loop car that once held a phone screen toward two boys who thought birthday was weather. I do not turn fully. Looking too hard at still things is how still things become conversations, and conversations with that kind of coat are how soft boys get asked *whom* before they have a spoon. The stillness passes, or never was. Mira does not mention it. Suzumori's humming spikes one note and settles. Optional edge. Private weather warning. I keep my mouth shut on purpose and feel, absurdly, like silence is the only unfinished mercy I have left that does not schedule a death.

"Sleep," Mira tells a runner in the hall — ordinary verb, ordinary night — and I flinch because my mouth almost offers the same word as grammar. Dependence likes helpfulness. Helpfulness is how doors stay open. I bite the scrape until it tastes like copper and birthday candles and a hand opening on a roof that is no longer this roof.

"Tomorrow we move," Mira says. Not a question.

"Toward Shizuhara," I say, flat. The name sits clean. Who and what assembling themselves without my permission: Serizawa Mei, extract, living minds, a stack that invents mercy out of processing language. Behind my teeth the Word sits awake, pleased, already offering clauses for corridors I have not walked. STAGE 4 does not need naming to keep the door open. The Word names enough for both of us.

Suzumori opens her eyes. "When you find her, decide in advance whether you are freeing a person or collecting a symbol. Mouths like yours will hear whichever you schedule."

I almost say *person*. The almost dies flatter than grief. Person is Haru's dialect. Symbol is Mira's. Legitimacy is Suzumori's careful hunger spoken without a crown. I am becoming the mouth that carries all three and pretends the carrying is justice.

Outside, Remnant dark thins toward a pre-dawn that will not be safety. Maps wait. The nail in Shizuhara waits. Somewhere under a hill, a quieter boy keeps a page open so the wrong version cannot fill the quiet. Somewhere a crooked-tooth road dog invoices a *stay* that failed. Somewhere pines hold an old man who walked. I do not look east for them. Looking would be unfinished. Looking would invent a gentler clause the Word will not fund.

"Pack," I tell the room — who implied, what finished enough — and bodies move because living minds still rent themselves to clean sentences. Mira's smile is mild and equation-bright. Suzumori stands like a kept thing preparing to enter a building that files kept things as inventory. My voice stays flat when I say the next word, and the flatness is the door, and the door does not close because I miss him.

"We go west of the pad until west becomes a stack with her name in it."

The billet exhales diesel and cold rice. The bird is no longer the hallway. The road is. Shizuhara waits inland like an argument made of glass and fluorescent mercy, and I walk toward it with blood already deciding it is a dialect, and a Bellkeeper humming the only manners left that do not belong to gray-green, and a girl named Mei who has not yet become a face — only a next extract, only legitimacy spoken carefully, only the schedule after the schedule that emptied a garden chair.

Keep moving. Flat is the door. The stack is the hallway. Haru is the unfinished sentence I do not get to finish from Remnant dark. The Word offers a cleaner verb. I take it, because taking is what STAGE 4 sounds like when a boy stops pretending he can put the gun down and still call himself soft.
