# Chapter 19 — Make Him Talk
*POV: Kaito*

The mansion smells like polished wood and quiet money.

We go in through people, because doors do not listen and locks have never owed me anything. A gardener walks us past the first camera because I ask him to remember a delivery that isn't ours. A maid opens a side door because I ask her to fetch a coat from a room that does not contain a coat, and she believes the errand hard enough that her hands find the latch. A captain looks away because Mira's runner gives him a reason that isn't my mouth — paper, timing, the soft machinery of fear that already lives in gray-green corridors.

Suzumori waits in the van with her bicycle bell. She refused the hill. "I will not hum while you choose a throat," she said. Fair. The bell is for kept things. Tonight I am choosing an interview. The distinction sits in my chest like a hairline fracture — talk first, schedule second, the promise I made over cold rice and am already bending toward something sharper.

Echo starts early. Mira wipes my face once in the service stair and does not comment. The scrape in my throat is awake, a second tongue pressed against the first. Mira's mildness is a temperature I have learned to walk inside. She does not tell me to be careful with kindness. She tells me, with a look, to be careful with unfinished sentences. Unfinished is how Jun happened. Unfinished is how Ryn happened.

Aihara receives us in a garden room with koi.

Mild civilian cardigan. Soft shoes on stone that cost more than a bar family's month. Eyes like folders — tabbed, ordered, already half-empty before I open my mouth. Tea already poured, as if curiosity arrived on a schedule he wrote. Two cups. He expected company. He expected shape. He knows what I am before I speak. You can tell by the way he watches my mouth instead of my hands. Captains watch hands. Filing hands watch verbs. He has filed enough carriers to know the difference between a boy and a weapon is grammar.

"School night," I say. Clean enough. Who implied. What named. Bounds held in my teeth like a bit. "Kōgetsu. Tell me what the dragnet was."

He smiles with none of his mouth. The koi circle under lantern light like punctuation that refuses to end a sentence — gold, white, black, looping, looping. Water sound. Garden sound. The war reduced to ambiance in a room built for it. "Asset acquisition. Two boys. Awakening threshold. The calamity was weather we could aim. You were the lightning rod we couldn't admit on paper." His gaze flicks to Mira. "Remnant always arrives late to its own blood."

The word *weather* scrapes. Haru's kitchen ideal, inverted and filed — the hand on the spoon replaced by the hand on the net. I taste metal and do not wipe it yet. Wiping is a tell. Tells get filed. "Tell me about Ryn," I say, and hate that her name becomes a tool in my mouth. Hate is still a feeling. I file that as proof I am not hollow yet. Hollow would be easier. Hollow schedules cleanly.

"Collateral," Aihara says, almost kind. Kindness from him is a stamp. "Leverage that failed upward. Instructive, if you care about lessons."

Something in me schedules itself. Not panic. Not the roof-hot roar that killed a soldier because unfinished *stop* left only one clean clause. This is colder. This is a calendar square darkening while I still sit in a chair. Collateral. Contingency. The language of men who file cities and call it peace. The plum tree in my chest is not a tree anymore. It is a ledger with branches.

Mira's hand is on my shoulder. Not stopping. Steadying. "Ask what you need. Then decide."

I ask.

About the quiet floors — the rooms where humming was hard to file, where kept things waited for transfer east. About carriers — the language he uses for boys who have not learned their own mouths yet. About who signs the orders that turn birthdays into nets: offices, chains, names that sound like furniture until you attach them to a roof. He answers. He answers because I tell him to answer, sentence by sentence, who and what and finished, and his eyes empty and fill and empty while the body stays upright in the cardigan like a man attending his own inventory. The Word does not make him theatrical. It makes him precise. Filing hands are already half-empty; emptying the rest is almost polite.

Each answer costs me. Nose first, then the dull pressure behind the eyes, then the flattening that Mira calls progress when she thinks I am not listening for the word. I keep the sentences clean. Mud costs more. Mud is how Jun happened. Mud is how Ryn happened. Clean is the lie I tell myself about mercy.

"Who was on the school stair with the watch?" I ask.

He names a lieutenant I already carry like a scar. Kanzaki. Procedure in a human shape. Gray-green certainty standing too straight at a gate that was built for a school night, not a war. Aihara's mouth says it the way ledgers say totals — without heat, without apology, a number that happens to have a throat.

"Who wanted us alive?"

"Everyone with a budget," Aihara says, emptied and filling. "Dead carriers teach nothing. Living ones rewrite maps."

"Who called her leverage?"

A pause — not resistance, processing. The pause of a filing hand checking a box before he stamps it. "Local command. Contingency language. The drop was authorized if acquisition failed."

Authorized. Contingency. The plum tree in my chest grows a new branch and does not leaf. Ryn on a form. Ryn as weather aimed. Ryn as instructive. I breathe through my mouth so the scrape does not sharpen into something I cannot unsay yet.

When I have enough truth to choke on, the next clause sits on my tongue already built. *Kill yourself.* Who and what and finished. Jun's lesson wearing Aihara's cardigan. The koi keep circling like nothing grammar can reach. Mira's hand does not tighten. She is waiting for the decision she already believes I will make, and the waiting is almost kindness, and almost kindness is how I have been learning to drown. The garden room is quiet money and quiet murder and quiet tea going cold while I choose which clause to leave on the cloth one day longer.

Not yet. Suzumori's warning. Talk first. Killing is a clause you don't get to unsay. I have unsayable clauses enough for one lifetime. I leave this one on the cloth a day longer. The leaving feels like sharpening anyway. The calendar square darkens in my chest without ink.

I release him from the answering. The release is a quieter sentence — stop talking, sit, remember us poorly — and he blinks back into a man who offered tea. The tea is going cold. The captain outside the garden wing will not remember our faces cleanly; Mira's runner has already rented that failure, paper and timing and the soft machinery of fear. Aihara looks at me with something that might be professional interest if interest can survive obedience. Filing hands respect assets that ask good questions. Filing hands schedule returns.

"You will return," he says, mild. Not a threat. A filing prediction. "Assets should be curious."

I do not answer. Answering would be a schedule spoken aloud. Schedules get filed. Schedules get aimed. I stand. Mira stands. The garden room releases us the way it received us — through people, through borrowed belief, through doors that open for errands that aren't real.

Outside, the hill air tastes like rain and paper. Mira searches my expression for disappointment and finds something colder. The cold is not victory. The cold is a postponement with a name. Tomorrow. Calendar. Sharpening.

"Next time," she says.

"Next time," I agree, and the agreement feels like sharpening. Echo paints my upper lip. I wipe it with the back of my hand and keep the red where Haru would have offered a cloth and a joke and a soft refusal to let the night become a mission. Mira offers neither. She offers a path down. Down is extract. Down is van. Down is Suzumori's bell and a map and an arcade room that smells like old sugar.

---

On the way down I see him.

Half a breath. That is all the world budgets for boys who used to share a train bench. Across a service road and a market spill of lanterns — paper light, oil smell, a vendor shouting about skewers like lockdown is a rumor — a soft boy stands beside a crooked-tooth road dog. Haru. Ledger-faced. Looking at the mansion the way he used to look at war screens: as if naming the wrong thing would make it real, and naming the right thing would make him responsible for moving. His hands are visible. His mouth is closed. Good. Closed mouths survive verification language.

Nick through my eyebrow. Blood under my nose. Mira at my shoulder like punctuation. Patrol gray-green cutting the road into lanes you do not cross without becoming a report. Two of them. Asset posture. Folding table theater. The city learning to be a fist without screaming. Crowd pressing — market night, skewers, the stubborn commerce of people who have decided curfew is optional if you sell food loud enough. His friend's hand already in his sleeve, reading exits. Riku. Crooked tooth not grinning. Exits priced. I know that look. I used to stand beside it.

Neither of us can cross. That is the mercy and the cruelty in the same held breath.

I want a sentence. Any sentence. *Come here. Stay. Don't look at me like that.* All of them are weapons. All of them would make him a location. I keep my mouth shut hard enough that my teeth hurt. His mouth stays shut too. The almost is worse than a fight. Fights end. Almosts schedule themselves into the chest and live there.

The crowd closes. Lanterns take him. Paper light swallows ledger-faced Haru and crooked-tooth Riku and the market that pretends the war is skewers and oil. Mira's grip finds my elbow — not yanking, directing. "Extract path," she says. "Bellkeeper in the van. Don't invent a second mission in a market."

I wipe my nose and keep walking. Scheduling has a calendar now. Aihara's name is already written on tomorrow in a handwriting that used to be mine — the loud boy's handwriting, the one that wrote *let her go* like love and learned it was grammar instead. Behind us the mansion lights hold their birthday-cake stare. Ahead, the arcade room waits with a map and a bicycle bell and an old woman who will ask, without mercy, whether *free* means release or erase.

I already know which one the Word will hear when I stop pretending I don't mean it.
