# Chapter 28 — Count
*POV: Haruki*

Morning count is how Quiet Field teaches you your new name is a number.

Fluorescent honesty. Soft-voice PA. Boots that have learned the exact length of a corridor so their rhythm can become a clock. They run us out of C in a line that pretends lines are kindness — irregulars first, then a bleed of volunteer transfer mistakes who got stamped wrong and never got stamped back. Riku's swollen eye has ripened into something theatrical; he wears it like a receipt. I wear my soft face because soft faces make guards invent fewer reasons. Stickiness stays pocketed. Habu's inventory still sits in my ear from the glass lobby, mild as rain.

"Eyes front. Mouths shut. Numbers honest," a guard says, tablet up, already bored of his own script.

We count.

Or rather — he counts, and we exist as the substance of his arithmetic. Corridor E-adjacent for a processing spill, then back toward C through a crossing where specials language brushes irregulars language like two weather systems refusing to merge. I ledger the geometry because ledgers are how I refuse to become only stock: left to medical adjacency, right to A/B volunteer breath through vents, east wing rumor thicker here — war-prisoner holds, specials glass, the rooms that pay different rent. Riku prices exits without moving his eyes. Nine-count boots. Camera dome. A door with no handle on our side. Same song, new building.

The guard loses the number at seventeen.

Not dramatically. Softly. The way men lose numbers when they believe the numbers belong to them by uniform. He frowns at the tablet. Recounts mouths. Skips a girl who shifted half a step. Invents a duplicate on a boy who only blinked. Boredom making math into fog.

"Eighteen," he mutters, then, "sixteen," then a curse polite enough for Habu's house.

"Seventeen," a voice says from the line — clear, amused, not loud. "You double-counted the blink and skipped the shift. Start at the fire extinguisher again. The extinguisher doesn't lie."

Heads turn.

She is already moving before the guard decides whether movement is allowed — seventeen or eighteen, taller than the ghost I try not to invent when girls are sharp, short practical dark hair, war-prisoner issue worn like a choice instead of a stamp. Pale scar across the left knuckles, not romantic, work or cuff. She takes the tablet from his hands with the polite theft of someone who has always believed displays were public property. Smiles like the numbers were always hers.

"One through corridor mark," she says, walking the line, tapping as she goes — not cruel, not gentle, precise. "Volunteer spill, irregular core, two medical holds returning, one empty slot because your rotation lost a body to processing and didn't update the wall. There. Seventeen present. Your tablet was a paragraph behind the hallway."

The guard stares. Someone in gray-green almost laughs and turns it into a cough. The girl — Serizawa Mei, a name that arrives later on a cuff-tag glance, but the face arrives now — finishes the tally, returns the tablet, and steps back into line as if she borrowed gravity for thirty seconds and is politely giving it back.

"Who told you to—" the guard starts.

"The corridor," Mei says, still smiling. "It was loud about being miscounted. I hate loud mistakes."

Riku's crooked tooth shows despite himself. "She's going to get someone billed."

"She's going to get someone corrected," I murmur, and my ledger does the thing ledgers do when a new column opens without asking: underlines her, stars her, refuses to pretend she is weather.

---

Orientation continues because Habu's house loves scripts even after scripts get stolen.

A different guard — smarter, less bored — walks us past painted marks: A/B that way, volunteers, do not fraternize; C this way, irregulars, do not invent status; east wing not your privilege, specials, war paperwork, eyes forward. Medical processing above, usefulness language, if you are called you go without speeches. Purge drills are not drills you discuss. Compliance keeps kindness from needing other tools — Habu's soft gospel secondhand.

Mei walks like she owns the yard's geometry even when the yard is a corridor. Blunt warmth. Tease in the eyes. Fear in the "fear is boring" register — not reckless, decisive. She glances at Riku's bruise and clicks her tongue once in a dialect that is not his and somehow rhymes.

"Road dog," she says. "You fight like you think exits owe you interest."

Riku bristles on schedule. "War stamp. You talk like you think corridors owe you tablets."

"They do," Mei says cheerfully. "Corridors are bad at math without supervision." She looks at me, sharper than kindness, warmer than Mira's mildness. "Soft boy. You count faces like you're afraid they'll tip into fists. Relax. Fists are honest. Tablets are the rude ones."

I almost apologize for having a face that can be read. The apology dies unfinished. Riku snorts approval at the death. Mei's smile widens a millimeter — filed, not mocked.

"Serizawa Mei," she offers, because names are also tally marks if you place them yourself. "Eastern front paperwork. Not volunteer. Don't put me in their arithmetic; I hate being misfiled."

"Haruki," I say. "This is Riku."

"I know," Mei says. "You two walk like a ledger and a bill stuck together with spite. Cute. Don't make it a speech."

Riku's ears color. Bristling into teased. "Survive a Tuesday first. Then talk cute."

"I survive counts," Mei says. "Tuesdays are just counts with worse lighting."

We are moved again — bunk checks, a stretch of wall where volunteer disappearances get whispered as relocation weather. Someone says processing. Someone says east. Someone says don't ask. Mei does not whisper. She watches the wall display update a volunteer total down by three and smiles without humor.

"Numbers left," she says quietly, for us, not for the PA. "Numbers don't relocate. People do. Or they don't."

My kitchen ember kicks once under the ribs. Hand on the spoon. Inventory without changing tone. Habu's tablet. Aihara's cardigan somewhere behind us in a closed file. Kaito's flatter voice west of a pad I still file under panic. The building is a kitchen. Mei just stole the recipe card and corrected the salt.

Riku prices the whisper-wall with his eyes and finds no exit that is not also a throat. "Soft boy. You hearing what I'm hearing?"

"Relocation that doesn't relocate," I say. "Processing that doesn't process people back into corridors."

"Don't invent a rescue off a wall display," he mutters, which is Machi's dialect wearing his crooked tooth. Then, quieter, almost for himself: "Volunteer math always ends ugly. I just hoped ugly was inland dust, not glass."

I do not ask him to open the wound here under fluorescent honesty. Wounds open when the building forces them. The building is already clearing its throat.

---

Back in C, the day invents chores that are not kindness: wipe, carry, stand, be tallied again. Mei appears in our corridor twice more — once on a work detail that makes no sense unless someone wants a war prisoner visible among irregulars, once leaning on a doorframe telling a younger volunteer how to stand so the count camera catches him as present instead of blur. She does not speechify. She organizes without banners. Moves bodies into better geometry. Corrects a cuff fold the way Machi corrects a bowl placement — receipt mercy.

Riku watches her like exits that learned a new language. "Don't get sticky about her."

"I'm not," I say.

"You're ledgering," he says. "Same disease, prettier symptoms."

He is right. I ledger her scar, her tablet theft, the way she said *not volunteer* like a boundary drawn in permanent ink. Sharper than the engine I will not name — the birthday engine, the arcade engine, the girl who laughed at claw machines and died as leverage. Different face. Same gravitational pull toward people who refuse to let the room miscount them. I do not say the other name. Saying it would be a reunion with a roof. Mei does not ask for ghosts. Mei asks for accurate tallies.

Between chores I wash a sink that does not need washing because finished jobs still lie usefully. Mei passes the glass and taps once — not hello, punctuation. "Your friend bills the air," she observes. "You apologize to it. Between you, someone should invent a door."

"We're working on Tuesday," I say.

"Make it count-day," Mei returns. "Tuesdays are sentimental. Counts are weapons if you hold them right."

She is gone before I can invent a soft answer. Riku pretends he did not hear. His ears color anyway. Teased into orbit. Gravity does that to road dogs and ledgers alike.

At evening count she is two places down from us. The guard's tablet behaves. Mei does not steal it. She does not need to. Her smile says she could. That is enough ownership for one fluorescent day.

"Soft boy," she says as we file past. "If you dream, dream in numbers you can check when you wake. Unchecked numbers become Habu's favorite lullaby."

"I don't sleep clean," I say.

"Good," Mei says. "Neither does the building. Stay useful to your own count."

Riku clicks his tongue once — the tell before help, or before admitting interest. "Bill her later," he mutters to me. "She's expensive."

"She's gravity," I answer, surprising myself, and Riku's look says the ledger just wrote a true line and he hates true lines that aren't exits.

Night approaches without pretending to be rest. PA softens. Volunteer blocks breathe through vents. East wing holds its specials like a second throat. Somewhere, if rumors have bones, processing invents usefulness and invents the end of usefulness with the same cheerful hand. Mei sits within the block's new center of mass — not elected, not announced, simply where eyes go when the corridor feels wrong. Riku bristles and orbits anyway. I keep the page open to a column titled *seventeen* and another titled *she smiled like the numbers were always hers*.

I still file the pad under panic. I still refuse two Voices as inheritance. But I can recognize a different kind of Word when I see it — not grammar that empties eyes, just a girl who takes a tablet and finishes the world correctly in public.

Mei is the new gravity in the block. The count ends. The pull does not.
