# Chapter 21 — "Under Curfew" (fallout / pressure)

Pays the Ch 21 compass in [lore/plot-outline.md](lore/plot-outline.md): lockdown; **someone from the bar family hurt** by the mansion’s crackdown; ideals harden into resolve; festival-under-curfew keeps the city alive.

**Who takes the hit (locked for this plan):** **Gorō** — already seeded in [lore/characters.md](lore/characters.md) as the plausible crackdown target (freight web; almost named “the hill” in Ch 17). His overnight hold gives Ch 23 a human reason to go to the mansion without turning into clue-hunting. (If you’d rather Machi or the tofu couple, say so before write.)

## Setup (days after Ch 20)

- City still under **informal lockdown**: more checkpoints, shorter evenings, karaoke tower quiet or rationed hours, festival lanterns still going up because canceling would admit something happened.
- Depot work thinned — “medical” runs dried up; foreman nervous; boys on odd scraps.
- Haruki carrying two weights: Kaito acted here; they may have been weather for it.

## Beats (~2,200–2,600 words)

1. **Festival under glass:** Open on lantern hang / street prep with the shotengai association — brightness first, wrong underneath. Curfew announcements polite as always. Joy allowed: Riku banter, a kid’s lantern joke, Machi sending them out with leftover broth in a jar. Texture, not montage dump.
2. **The squeeze hardens:** Not a random shakedown — collectors / gray-green with mansion paper hit the arcade harder. Minato gets a second visit (or a worse “contribution”). Machi pays flat again; the room knows this is aftermath of the stack, not “season.”
3. **Gorō taken:** Soldiers / tea-suit men collect him from the stool or the lane — “questions about freight routing,” “irregular manifests,” polite and irreversible. He goes without a fight; leaves his unfinished beer; maybe one line to the boys (*don’t follow / keep the fact-toll warm*). Machi’s face goes harbor-hard. This is the chapter’s gut punch — human, not plot coupon.
4. **The night without him:** Bar thinner; tofu couple quiet; Haruki’s complicity itch spikes (Gorō’s crew got them the clean run). Riku: curriculum talk without speeches — you don’t storm a hill on guilt; you stay useful. No Word slips. No chase of Kaito.
5. **Ideals harden (close):** Festival lanterns lit under curfew — city refusing to die on schedule. Haruki says (to Riku, not a crowd) the kitchen thought turned sharp: reshaping means nothing if you won’t stand for the people in the room when the hill reaches for them. Resolve = **get Gorō back / don’t let the mansion eat the bar family** — human objective, teeing Ch 23. Close on Gorō’s empty stool or lanterns-under-curfew — **not** on mansion lights (Ch 20 spent that) and not on “Kaito sleeping.”

## Guardrails

- **No clue-chain.** Nobody finds a list, a map, or Aihara’s name. Haruki still doesn’t know the Bellkeeper or the mansion’s real runner.
- **No Kaito/Mira on-page.** Pressure is the city’s answer to their off-page win.
- **Gorō alive.** Held / questioned is fine; death would break the Ch 23 enter-for-a-human-reason beat. Prefer **still missing at chapter end** so Ch 22–23 have a live hook.
- Motif budget: don’t clone Ch 17’s squeeze scene beat-for-beat (escalate, don’t remake); fresh festival texture; no “not Sugi’s.”
