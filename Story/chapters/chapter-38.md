# Chapter 38 — On Your Feet

The taillights are gone before I can make my legs do anything useful.

Mira's hand is still over my jaw. Her forehead is still against mine. The almost-sentence sits unfinished in my mouth — *Stop. Get out. Get away from him* — and the copper is already there for a Word I did not get to spend. Behind us Shizuhara keeps its lights on like the lobby slaughter was a rumor somebody else told. Ahead, the service track is empty mud.

"He had him," I say. Not a question. "The coat from the glass. Habu's mezzanine. He had Haruki in the bed of that truck."

"I saw it." Mira does not let go of my belt. "You are shaking and half empty. If you Word that truck while it is moving, you could kill him with the stop."

"I am not leaving him with—"

"You already did." Her voice is flat with the effort of holding me. "The truck did not ask your permission. Contact is this way. Suzumori is this way. You can hate the coat when you can stand without me."

I walk because she makes me. West along the ditch, away from Habu's white glare, away from the place where Haruki's face was for one breath and then was not. My legs still do not trust the grass. Blood is dried on my chin from the lobby. The vents are still in my head — kids thanking Habu into a white room, the hose between groups, the soft voice on the speakers. I left Habu alive with his mouth shut. I emptied his west answer. None of it put Haruki on our side of the wire.

On the ridge above Nagihama, Mira once told me I could grieve on my feet. I am still on my feet. It does not feel like grief anymore. It feels like a hole with a sentence trying to grow in it.

The contact's truck is where we left it, pulled further into scrub so Habu's floodlights do not paint it. The contact is under the open hood with a light between her teeth, swearing at a belt. Suzumori sits on the running board with her stick of wood crooked in her hair, eyes on the pale concrete block we failed to steal a girl out of.

Suzumori looks up first.

"You smell like a building that died," she says. Not unkind. Exact. "Where is Mei?"

"Mei is not coming," Mira says. She eases me down against the tire before my knees give out. "She took Habu's key and went back for the kids in C. The west guards are dead. Habu is alive, but he cannot talk — Kaito shut his mouth and left him in the glass. A and B are gone. We got out through the cut in the wire."

The contact spits the light into her palm. "That was not the job."

"The job died in the annex," Mira says. "You were not inside. Do not talk to me like I forgot the chalk marks."

"We burned cutters and a night window to pull Mei out," the contact says. She looks at me. "You came back without her, and you look like you spent your whole voice on something else. What did you do in there?"

"What Habu's people deserved," I say. The scraped voice is thin. No weight under it. Just talk. "They thanked kids into a kill room. I killed the ones with rifles."

Suzumori's mouth tightens. "You already knew that mouth of yours cuts both ways. Knowing it and emptying a lobby are not the same thing."

"I am not asking you to like it." I tip my head toward the dark road the truck took. "There was a man in a dark coat on Habu's mezzanine while A and B walked into the vents. He watched the whole thing and did nothing. Then he drove past us with Haruki in the truck bed — kids from C, a girl with a lanyard, somebody with a notebook. Haruki looked straight at me. His nose was bleeding."

Mira's hand finds my shoulder. I shake it off.

"That coat has my friend," I say. "And Habu is still breathing in his glass. I am not sitting in this ditch while either of those things stays true."

The contact laughs once, ugly. "You want to chase a truck on ruined legs after you turned Shizuhara into a story every desk east of here will invent a hold order for. Brilliant. Remnant does not run taxi service for boys who cannot finish an extract without writing a massacre."

"Then do not run it for me," I say. "Point me at the road and get out of the way."

Mira steps between us before the contact can answer with a hand.

"Enough," she says. "Both of you. We are not arguing volume in Habu's scrub. Contact — the extract failed. That is on the night, not on a speech. You still owe us a direction that is not back through the wire. Suzumori — if you have a place that is not a Remnant billet with a clipboard of its own, say it. I am not putting him in another inventory while his mouth is like this."

Suzumori taps the running board with one finger, thinking the way she thinks about keys.

"Ashiro," she says.

The contact's head turns. "That is not our drop."

"It is a city," Suzumori says. "Canals, night markets, boarding houses that take cash. Remnant keeps a thin watch there if you keep your head down. People go when the road behind them is burned and they need a few days somewhere that is not writing hold orders about them." She looks at Mira, then at me. "Your aunt's drivers used the inland ring through Ashiro when the coast got too watched. You know the name even if you have never been. After a job like tonight, that is where I would send a boy who still wants to fight — not to heal him. To keep him from walking straight back into Habu's lobby because he cannot think of anywhere else to go."

"Why would Haruki's truck go there?" I ask.

"I do not know that it did," Suzumori says. "I know coats that move people after buildings catch fire often prefer cities that can swallow a story. I know Mira's road has used Ashiro before as a breath. I know if you stand here inventing sentences for a ditch, Habu's second shift will invent you first."

Mira nods once. Slow. "We go to Ashiro."

The contact slams the hood. "I am not driving you into a reed market so he can hunt a coat. Remnant will want answers for Mei, for the cutters, and for whatever he did to Habu's west side. You take him inland, and that mess follows you."

"Then let it follow," Mira says. "I am not leaving him in this ditch to be the next anomalous line on somebody's sheet. You can come, or you can go tell your table the extract died and the girl stayed. Those are your choices. They are not his."

The contact stares at her. Then she looks at me like I am a broken tool she still has to load.

"I take you to the inland spur," she says. "Not into Ashiro proper. From there you walk or you steal a ride. I am not parking under lanterns with a scraped-voice boy who just made Shizuhara famous. And when the table asks, I tell them Mira chose the loud road again."

"Tell them whatever keeps you breathing," Mira says. "We leave now."

Suzumori stands with a small sound of old joints. She puts her stick of wood back through her hair.

"I am coming," she says. "Not because I like Ashiro. Because somebody on that truck needs a person who has opened bad locks before, and the contact is not going to be that person."

We climb in. The truck bumps off the scrub onto a darker road than Habu's service track. Shizuhara's white smear falls behind the ridge the same way Nagihama's lights once fell away under my boots. Mira sits with her shoulder against mine so I do not tip. Suzumori watches my mouth the way she watched it on the quiet floors — not afraid of blood, afraid of the next sentence.

I do not give her one.

I watch the dark and see Haruki's face in the truck bed, and the coat in the cab glass, and Habu's awake eyes over a sealed mouth, and the armband kids walking into a room that thanked them. The Word offers me clean clauses for all of it. I keep them behind my teeth because Mira is right about distance and trucks, and because if I open my mouth now I am not sure I will stop at one building.

"Ashiro," I say at last, to the window, to anyone who wants it. "I am going to find the coat. I am going to find Haruki. And I am going to find the next man who runs a place like that and make him stop. If your city is on the way, fine. If it is not, I will keep walking."

Mira does not tell me I am wrong.

Suzumori makes that dry sound again. "On your feet, then. Just remember — feet arrive at doors. Mouths decide what happens after."

The inland spur is a black strip between reed fields that smell like wet rope. The contact brakes hard enough that my teeth click.

"Out," she says. "End of the ride. I am not parking under Ashiro lanterns with him in the cab."

We get out. She does not wait for thanks. The truck turns back the way we came, and the taillights shrink until there is only reed smell and dark.

Mira pulls the sack onto her shoulder — the same sack she packed days early in Nagihama.

"Talk now," I say. "I have been waiting since the ridge. I am not walking another hour before you answer."

"You are," Mira says. "If I answer you out here, you will want to turn around and fight somebody before we have walls, and Suzumori cannot run. When we stop somewhere that is not a ditch — a shed, a roof, anything with a door — I will answer you. Not before."

"That is not fair."

"It is not. Can you walk or not?"

"I can walk."

Suzumori taps her stick once against the dirt. "West. Before Habu's people come looking."

Mira starts walking. I fall in beside her.

"How far?" I ask.

"A few hours if we do not stop," Mira says. "Do not start the questions until we have a roof. I mean it."
