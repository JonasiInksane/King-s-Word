# Chapter 11 — Soft Boy

The Ishibashi road does not care that I started walking after dark.

By the time the sky goes gray again, my water is gone and my shoes have stopped pretending they are still school shoes. Trucks pass without slowing. Once a bus goes by with faces in the windows, and I lift a hand like an idiot. Nobody stops. I keep the ditch side and count kilometers the way you count when numbers are the only thing left that still answers.

Ishibashi is supposed to be further inland — old stone bridge, market under the arches, thin FDC paper. Sato said Kuroishi. The shop woman said Ishibashi. Kaito's mouth left a rumor at a well. That is the whole map I own.

Near midday I cut off the main road because a clipboard truck is parked at a bend ahead, and I do not have another stamp to smile through. The cut-through runs behind a scrapyard fence and a row of shuttered tool sheds. Cooler in the shade. Stupid, if anyone is waiting.

Three men are waiting.

Local, not gray-green. One has a pipe. One has hands already on my bag strap before I finish turning around. The third laughs like this is the part of the day he planned.

"Please don't," I say, which is not a plan.

He laughs harder. My hands come up soft. Habit. The apology is already shaping itself — _sorry, I can give you_ —

The strap jerks. I stumble. Someone's knuckles catch my ribs. I hit the fence hard enough that the wire sings, and for a stupid second I think about saying _stop_ the way Kaito said it, and then I remember what finished sentences do under plum trees, and I keep my mouth shut.

"Easy," the one with the pipe says. "He's soft. Soft ones pay quieter."

A fourth set of boots hits the cut-through behind them.

Not quiet. Not careful. Like someone who decided the alley belonged to him for the next thirty seconds.

A hand grabs my collar and hauls me sideways so hard my feet leave the dirt. I go with it because fighting the hand would mean staying with the pipe. I hit a shoulder, then open air, then the scrapyard gate post.

The boy who pulled me is about seventeen, lean-hard, messy dark hair tied back with a scrap of cloth. Cheap work jacket. Scraped knuckles. Crooked front tooth when he grins. He clicks his tongue once, looks at the three men, then at me.

"Soft boy," he says. "You walk like the road owes you manners."

"I—"

"Later." He tips his chin at the pipe. "You three still collecting tolls behind sheds, or did you get bored of the main street?"

The one with the pipe shifts. "This isn't your cut."

"It is now." He doesn't raise his voice. He also doesn't step back. "He's got dust for pockets and a face that apologizes to fences. Take the bag if you want the water bottle. Leave the kid."

"Or what."

He grins wider. The crooked tooth makes it worse. "Or we find out how many of you can run with a busted knee. I'm not picky about which one."

They look at each other long enough for arithmetic. Then the bag man swears, shoves my strap back into my chest, and they go the other way down the cut-through. Not running. Not slow either.

I stay against the gate post until my ribs decide they still work.

"You can breathe," he says. "They left."

"Thank you."

He clicks his tongue once. "Don't. You looked like you were about to apologize them into a second hit."

"I wasn't—"

"You were." He jerks a thumb toward the main road. "You heading Ishibashi, or are you just collecting bruises between towns?"

"Ishibashi." I push off the post. "I'm looking for someone."

"Everyone on this road is looking for someone." He starts walking without checking if I follow. After three steps he glances back. "Well? Soft boy. Legs still attached?"

I follow.

---

We walk the ditch side where the trucks throw less dust. He doesn't offer his name. I don't ask for the first kilometer, because asking feels like I am already owing him something, and I already owe him the collar-haul.

"I'm Haruki," I say eventually.

"Nakahara Riku." He says it like it is not a secret and also not an invitation. "You eat today?"

"No."

"That explains the falling-over walk." He digs in his jacket and tosses me a hard rice cracker that tastes like salt and old paper. "Don't thank me for food either. Makes me itchy."

"Then what am I allowed to say."

"Directions. Facts. Who you're chasing and why I should care if the next shed has more pipes."

"My friend left Kuroishi yesterday. Tall. Scraped voice. Girl with him, dark hair, sack. They were headed Ishibashi — bus if they had fare, walk if they didn't."

Riku chews his own cracker and watches a truck pass. "Loud one and a girl. Yeah. Market talk under the bridge yesterday evening said two like that came through asking about rooms that don't ask questions. FDC paper's thin in Ishibashi, but it still bites if you stand in the wrong doorway."

"Yesterday evening." My stomach drops and climbs at the same time. "So I'm—"

"Late," he says. "Not dead-late. Bridge market turns over rumors faster than tickets. If they stayed the night, you might still catch a door that remembers them. If they moved on at dawn, you're walking again."

"Why help me."

He shrugs. "Bored. And I hate shed tolls. Also you walk like someone is going to use you for spare parts if nobody teaches you where to put your feet."

"I don't need teaching."

"You needed a collar." He glances at my shoes. "Those are city shoes. City shoes die on this road. Next town has a stall that sells boots that have already suffered. If you're smart, you buy before you chase."

"I don't have much money."

"Then you have less after the stall. That's how money works." He kicks a stone off the ditch into the road. "I'm going as far as Ishibashi anyway. Work. Not you. You can walk behind me if you don't slow me down, and if another pipe shows up, you let me talk first. Soft mouths get people killed when they try to be polite mid-swing."

"Okay," I say.

"Okay." He clicks his tongue once, like he has decided something and does not need me to agree twice. "Keep up, soft boy. Bridge before dark if we don't stop for your feelings."

We keep walking.
