# Chapter 23 — Go Home

I hit the pine road running.

The cart is still crooked in the ditch — one wheel sunk, horse stamping, three gray-greens. The contact is on her knees by the horse's head. Riku has his body between Haruki and the nearest rifle, left arm already wrong from the butt-stroke. The rifle that was swinging toward them turns toward me instead when my boots hit gravel.

Mira comes down hard behind me. Suzumori swears from higher up and keeps coming anyway. The contact's breath catches when she sees my face.

"Kaito—" Haruki says.

My name almost stops me. For half a second I am stupid enough to look at him instead of the gun.

The gray-green with the rifle sees a tall boy, scraped voice, bloody bandage — enough of the hospital story to smile wrong.

"On the ground," he says. "All of you. Scraped voice last."

Riku does not get on the ground. He steps into the soldier — shoulder into the barrel, left hand on the sling, right fist short into the gut. The rifle fires into dirt. The horse screams and rears. The contact rolls clear of the hooves.

The second rifle swings toward Riku's back.

I am close enough.

"Drop the rifle. Both hands on your head. Kneel."

It lands. The weight leaves my throat hard enough that the road tips. The second soldier's eyes empty; the rifle hits gravel; he kneels with his hands laced like a child at a shrine. Blood finds my upper lip.

The first soldier is still fighting Riku — my command was for the one with the barrel aimed, not the one already in a clinch. Riku drives him into the cart wheel. The clipboard man scrambles for a sidearm he is too slow to clear. Mira kicks his wrist and the gun skitters under the cart. She does not pick it up. She puts her boot on his shoulder and keeps him down.

"Don't kill him," she tells Riku, sharp. "We need the road empty, not famous."

Riku's answer is a grin with a crooked tooth and another short punch that ends the argument without ending the man. The gray-green slides down the wheel and stays there, breathing, not getting up.

Haruki has not moved except to catch my eyes and hold them.

The kneeling soldier stays down with empty eyes and his hands still laced. I wipe my nose on my wrist and keep moving.

"Up the cut," Mira says. "Now. That rifle went off — the hill will come looking."

Suzumori is already halfway down, stick of wood crooked in her hair, eyes on my mouth like it is a latched door she refuses to praise.

"Boy," she says. "They heard the gun. They will hear what your mouth did next if we stand here."

"I know."

Haruki steps around Riku. Close enough that I can see the dust on his shoes — Haiga dust, city dust, the kind of dust that means he walked farther than he should have on a sentence I never let him finish.

"Kaito," he says again. Full. Careful. The voice that makes people quiet when he does not mean to. "I need you to hear me."

"We don't have time for speeches," Mira says.

"Then he talks while we walk," Riku says. He is already pulling the contact up, already watching the east stretch of road. "Or we stand here until the next pair arrives and I get to be clever with a third rifle. Pick."

Mira looks at me. I look at Haruki. The unfinished thing between us is louder than the horse still stamping in the ditch.

"West," I say. "Off the road. Talk if you have to. Keep up."

---

We take the scrub south of the pine road, parallel to the inland track, far enough that a motor would have to invent a reason to follow. Mira leads with Suzumori. The contact and Riku take turns watching behind. Haruki walks at my shoulder like he has a right to the space, and the worst part is that my body still makes room for him before my anger can stop it.

He does not waste the first hundred meters on small talk. He has been composing this since a gateway. I can see it in the way his hands keep almost lifting and then dropping.

"At the gate," he says. "I started to say it wasn't your fault. I stopped because I'd just watched a man die on the end of your sentence, and I wouldn't give you a lie." He swallows. Dust in his throat, or the old honesty. "I've walked every road between that gate and this one meaning to finish it when it was true enough to say."

I keep walking. Roots. Wet scrub. My shoulder singing.

"It wasn't your fault," Haruki says. Six syllables. The ones he owed me under a vending-machine light outside Kōgetsu. "Not the hand. Not Ryn. Not the roof. You told a soldier to let go of a girl and the world broke wrong around the words. That is not the same as choosing what happened to her." His voice stays level. "I should have said it before the yard. I should have said it in the underpass. I am saying it now, and I am not asking you to pretend the road didn't happen. I am asking you to stop running like the only clean thing left is your mouth."

The words are true. They do not change what I am going to do next.

"You walked all that way for six syllables," I say.

"I walked all that way because you left with my sleeve still in your hand." He almost smiles and fails. "And because nobody else is coming to fix any of this. I figured that out in Takamatsu before I found your shape on a hospital story. If the world is this broken, I am not spending the rest of it one day behind you."

Riku, ahead, clicks his tongue once. "Pretty. Also we have company if we keep making speeches at walking speed."

Mira has stopped at a break in the scrub where the inland track shows through. She points east — dust on the pine road, small as insects from here. Pairs. Moving west. Not running yet.

"They heard the shot," she says. "They will send more."

Suzumori's hand finds my sleeve — not Mira's habit, a different grip, older. "Say what you came to say to him, boy from the clean rooms. Then decide if you are a door or a person. I will not decide it for you."

Haruki faces me fully. The scrub smells like crushed broom. Somewhere a dog barks at the wrong century.

"Come back with me," he says. "Not to Kōgetsu. There is no Kōgetsu. Back to something that isn't this — Mira steering, your mouth as the whole plan. We find another way. We invent one if we have to. Just stop. Let me walk beside you while we still both remember how."

It is a good speech. It is the speech I wanted in the dark outside Kōgetsu, and in the gateway, and in every ditch between Kuroishi and the stationery shop window. It is not enough.

Because Mira is watching me with the face that does not beg. Because Suzumori's dread sits in the air without needing the word *kings*. Because the pairs on the road are closing and my mouth already knows the clean sentence that buys us an exit, and Haruki's careful voice will not make three gray-greens forget a rifle shot.

"You don't get to finish my road with a sentence," I say. "Even a true one."

Haruki's face does something small and broken. "Then what do I get?"

"You get to go home."

I do not mean Nagihama. I do not mean Sugi's door. I mean off this scrub, off Mira's fork, off the hunt that ends with the Listener collecting mouths. I mean away from me before I use him the way I used a clerk and a soldier and a kneeling man on gravel.

He hears the ordinary meaning first. Then he hears the weight gathering under it — I see it in his eyes, the same recognition he had for a door that opened because a mouth asked.

"Kaito. Don't."

Mira's hand is on my back. Not stopping me. Steadying the body that is about to pay.

Riku starts forward. "Hey—"

The pairs' voices carry from the pine road, closer — questions about a scraped voice, about the hospital path. The contact goes still.

I look at Haruki — careful hands, the face that would not lie to me even when a lie would have been kinder — and I put the weight under the only exit I can still close.

"Stop following me. Go home."

Copper. Hard. The scrub goes gray at the edges. My knees want the ground and find Mira's shoulder instead.

Haruki's face empties — and then something in him fights it, a resistance I have never watched from the outside. I do not get to see which one wins. The weight has already left my throat. Blood is already on my lip.

"Kaito—" Mira says.

Riku grabs for Haruki's arm. Haruki's feet are already turning toward the east, toward Takamatsu, toward whatever *home* means when a scraped voice defines it for you. His mouth opens around my name again and nothing comes out that has force in it.

Suzumori makes a sound like a door latch failing.

I do not watch him take the first steps. I let Mira pull me west into thicker scrub, away from the pine road, away from the pairs, away from the boy who walked a country to close six syllables and got two sentences back instead.

Behind us Riku swears once — ordinary, furious — and his boots go east after Haruki. The contact stays on Mira's heel. Suzumori comes west with us, stick of wood crooked in her hair, eyes on the road we left like she expects the gray suit to step out of it.

Needles scrape my bandage. Mira does not tell me I did the right thing. She does not tell me I did the wrong one. She puts my arm over her shoulders and keeps me moving west until the pine road's voices thin to nothing.

"Keep walking," she says.

I keep walking.
