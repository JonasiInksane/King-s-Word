# Chapter 8 — Toward Kuroishi

They hose the lane before noon. The stones under the plum stay dark. FDC puts up a cone line by the dried-fish shop and asks every gate the same questions — irregular activity, unregistered travelers, unusual orders.

I stand near Sugi's gate with her grip still aching in my wrist. A soldier asks if I saw anything last night. I say no, quiet enough that it sounds ordinary. He asks again, slower. Same answer. They look at me, decide I am not useful, and move on.

Kaito already left with Mira and Jun's papers are gone. Someone picked them up with the rest.

Sato finds me at the cone line with stamps in her good hand and a white sling. She does not bother with a greeting.

"You look like you're deciding whether to fall over," she says.

"I'm fine."

"You're not." She glances past me at the cone line, then presses a stamped paper into my palm. "One checkpoint. One day. Don't waste it standing here looking guilty."

"Where did they go?"

"Inland. Before night. Toward Kuroishi — that's what the loud one said when he thought nobody was listening to directions." She shifts the sling and winces. "If you go after them, stop him if you can."

"I can't. Not with this. Not after last night."

"Then don't promise me you will. Just don't lose the paper." She starts to turn, then stops. "And if you catch him — tell him the alley wasn't nothing. Tell him I can still use the arm. Mostly."

"Sato—"

"Go, or don't. I'm not your mother." She leaves. The stamp ink is still faintly warm.

---

Hanae comes at dusk along the same wet stones. Dust in her hair. Hands shaking before she speaks.

"Did he suffer?"

I can't tell her the true sentence — the Stop that muddied, the trust-look, what Kaito said after. She wants something she can live with.

"It was quick," I say.

She nods once, then shakes her head like the nod wasn't enough. "He kept talking about papers. Right up until — I heard him. He thought papers still opened doors."

"I know."

"They don't." She wipes her face with the back of her wrist. "If you see the girl who told us about the depot, tell her I said thank you. Or don't. I don't care. I just needed someone to say it was quick."

She walks away before I can invent anything kinder.

Sugi is waiting at the gate when I turn back. She looks at my empty hands and at the road Mira took.

"You can't hold both of them," she says. "One left. One's dead. Decide what you're going to do."

"I should have stopped him at the gate."

"Should doesn't help. You stay, or you go. Choose."

"If I go—"

"Then you go," she says. "I'm not locking you in. I'm also not pretending Kuroishi is a holiday."

---

That night she packs onigiri without asking if I am leaving — rice, a scrap of pickled plum in the center, cloth wrap.

"You're going to ask me not to," I say.

"I'm not asking you anything." She ties the cloth. "The cat stays here. And if you find him, don't make me mourn three of you."

"I'll try."

"Trying is what got us the plum tree conversation." She holds my wrist hard. Then she lets go. "Eat on the road. Don't talk to gray-green unless they make you. If they do, keep your voice quiet and boring."

I leave the arcade cat on the shelf. Sato's paper goes in my inner pocket. Onigiri in the cloth. I put my shoes on and step into the entryway sand.

---

Dawn on the inland road is dust.

The first kilometer still feels damp from the harbor. By the third, it doesn't. I eat half an onigiri without tasting it and wrap the rest. Behind me is Nagihama. Ahead is Kuroishi — the direction Sato gave me.

A checkpoint waits where the road narrows. I hand the paper over. The soldier checks the stamp and waves me through.
