# Chapter 39 — What She Packed

We stop because Suzumori's ankle will not take another hour on the spur.

Her boot comes off with a wet sound. She sits with her back to a crate and rolls the sock down slow — not showing off the pain, just letting it be there. Mira finds a strip of clean cloth from the sack and wraps the swelling without asking. I stand opposite the shed door so I can see anyone coming down the canal path.

"You said when we stopped somewhere that was not a ditch," I say. "This is not a ditch. Why did you pack that sack days before the shrine yard in Nagihama? Why did you already know the ridge track? Why does Remnant hand you chalk marks and cutters like you are on a payroll? And why the hell did you walk me into Shizuhara for a girl and let me empty a lobby while you held my belt?"

Mira does not flinch. She sets the water bottle between us and rolls her left sleeve up to the elbow.

The burn on the inside of her forearm is old — pale, tight, skin that healed wrong. She does not hide it. She also does not wave it around.

"When I was fourteen," she says, "Federation people burned a warehouse on the coast where my aunt's drivers stored crates they were not supposed to have. Food. Medicine. Paper that helped people who did not have the right stamps. I was inside counting boxes because my aunt trusted me with numbers. The fire started at the loading doors. I got out because a woman I had never met dragged me through a side hatch and put her coat over my face so I would not breathe the smoke."

She stops. The canal lock drips outside. Suzumori's eyes stay on Mira's arm.

"My aunt did not get out," Mira says. "Two of the drivers did not get out. The woman who pulled me told me her name once, then said names mattered less than getting people out. She was Remnant. She still is. She is the reason I am not ash in a warehouse the news called an electrical fault."

Suzumori clicks her tongue. "Okina does not like being thanked in rooms with thin walls. Keep going."

"Okina," Mira says. Just the name. "She got me out. She put me with my aunt's remaining drivers when the coast got too watched for their old routes. Those drivers taught me which gray suits listen instead of stamp, which ridge tracks still take a bicycle, which cities you can hide in for a few days if you keep your head down. That is how I knew about the man in the gray suit before you saw him cover his ears in Nagihama. That is how I had a sack packed. Not magic. Homework. Ugly homework."

I look at the scar until she rolls the sleeve down.

"So you were always Remnant."

"I was always a girl who owed a woman her life," Mira says. "Remnant is the road that woman walks. I walk it because asking nice did not save the people in that warehouse, and the news lie did not either. Habu's building is the same kind of place — soft voices thanking kids into a kill room. You can make people do what you say when you put weight behind it. I have watched it work. I am not going to pretend that is a party trick while places like that still have the lights on."

Suzumori leans forward. "Careful, girl. That is starting to sound like you own him."

"I do not own him," Mira says. She looks at me, not at Suzumori. "I steered you because you were already going to throw yourself at the next person held over a drop. I would rather that be pointed at the next warehouse instead of at a random fight you cannot win. That is not a leash. If you want to walk away from me in Ashiro, walk. I will not Word you. I cannot."

"Remnant wanted Mei," I say. "Not a massacre. The contact made that clear."

"Remnant wanted a clean extract," Mira says. "Next time they will want answers — for Mei staying, for the cutters, for what you did in the west. Ashiro is not the end of my road either. Okina has people somewhere who still think I report to them. I am telling you that now so you do not invent a nicer version later."

Suzumori ties her boot back on with hands that know how to work through pain. "I am walking with you because a boy with a mouth like yours scares me when he is alone. I have kept packages for people Mira's aunt's road owed. I have sat on quiet floors while men with nice watches decided when I would be transferred east. Your voice is loud even when you whisper, Kurosawa. I will not turn that into a speech tonight. I will tell you this: if Mira uses you only as a hammer, I will say so to her face. If you use yourself only as a hammer, I will say so to yours."

"Two mouths like that in one world," I say, because the phrase has been stuck in my head since the pine road. "You said that once."

"I said I was afraid of the thought," Suzumori says. "Do not turn it into a plan. Not tonight."

Nobody talks for a bit. Outside, something moves on the canal — a night fisherman, maybe, or a patrol that does not care about a broken padlock on a tool shed. My throat still tastes like the lobby. Haruki's face in the truck bed will not leave. I see it every time I blink: blood at his nose, C shirt, the coat man's cab between us.

"You watched me tell him to go home," I say to Mira. "On the pine road. You did not stop me."

"No," she says. "I did not. I thought if he stayed he would die next to you. I may have been wrong. I may have been right in a way that still makes me sick. I am not going to polish it for you."

"And Shizuhara?"

"I came for Mei. You stayed for the kids. I held you when the lobby was done because if you kept going there would be nothing left of you by morning." Her voice stays level. "I am not sorry I held you. I am sorry A and B are dead. Those can both be true."

I want to say something that cuts her. I want to crack Habu's glass from here. I want Haruki in front of me without a truck between us. None of that helps, so I keep my mouth shut.

The vents come back anyway — white room, hose between groups, a girl my age saying thank you like it was a password. Something ugly comes up my throat and I swallow it down. Mira watches my jaw work.

"You are doing that thing," she says. "Where you line up words and do not say them."

"If I say them, somebody dies," I tell her. "That used to feel like a rule. Now it just feels like the only way I know how to stand still."

"Good," Suzumori says. "Keep your mouth shut until you know who you are pointing it at."

I look at her. "You think I do not know?"

"I think grief makes boys sure of themselves," she says. "I have seen it on the quiet floors. Your mouth is louder than theirs. That does not make you wiser. It only makes the mistakes bigger."

Mira passes me water. I drink because my body needs it, not because anyone offered kindness.

"Why stick with me at all?" I ask her. "If Okina taught you numbers and ridge tracks and chalk marks, why stay next to a boy who just killed a room full of people?"

"Because after Ryn, you were going to keep throwing yourself at the next bad thing until something stopped you," Mira says. "I did not make you that. I only decided I would rather be standing next to you when it happens than hear about it later. That is selfish. I am not pretending it is noble."

"Selfish is honest," Suzumori says. "Noble is what people say later when the bodies are out of sight."

The shed creaks when the wind shifts. My chin still has dried blood on it from Habu's night. I touch the scab and feel how ordinary the skin is underneath — how the lobby did not burn me, only the people in the vents.

"The coat," I say. "Haruki's truck. You think Ashiro?"

"I think cities where a story can get lost," Mira says. "My aunt's drivers used Ashiro when the coast got watched. If that truck is anywhere we can reach on foot, it is more likely under Ashiro lanterns than behind another fence. I do not know that. I will not pretend I do just because your hands are shaking."

"I am not shaking."

"Your hands are," she says.

I look down. She is right. I make fists until the knuckles go white, then open them because Suzumori is watching and I am tired of being read.

"Ashiro," I say. "We find the coat. We find Haruki. We find the next man who runs a place like Habu's. After that I decide whether your road and mine are still the same."

Mira nods once. "That is fair."

Suzumori finishes with her boot. "Fair changes the minute a city gives you a worse choice. Drink the water. Lie down for an hour. Ashiro is closer than it looks, and neither of you will get there sharper by talking yourselves empty."

"I am not sleeping," I say.

"You are lying down with your eyes closed," she says. "That is what I mean by sleep. Do not argue about words at two in the morning."

Mira almost smiles. It does not reach her eyes. "She is older than both of us. Listen to her anyway."

I lie down on the shed floor with my eyes open. The boards smell like oil and old rope. Suzumori takes the spot by the door. Mira sits against the crate with the sack in her lap, awake the way she is when she still thinks somebody might come down the path.

When the hour is up, Suzumori stands and checks her ankle. Mira repacks the water. We leave without another speech about roads.

By the time the reed thins, Ashiro is lanterns over black water and a bridge with a sleepy clerk who wants two coins or a reason.

Mira pays before I open my mouth.

The clerk waves us through. On the far side, under the first real streetlamp, I stop.

"Where do we start?" I ask. "Boarding houses? Drivers? The coat will not wait for us to be careful."

"Boarding houses that take cash," Mira says. "Drivers who talk when they drink. Not desks. And not your voice on the first man who smells wrong."

Suzumori leans on her stick. "If you Word someone in this market before breakfast, the city will write a hold order with your face on it. Ask with coins and eyes. Save the mouth for when you are sure."

"Haruki first," I say. "Then the coat. Then I decide about your road."

Mira nods once. "Haruki first. That much we agree on."
