# Chapter 5 — The Pier

Tuesday comes in gray, and I go to the pier anyway.

Haru thinks I'm stacking wood behind the house. Sugi thinks I'm at the well. Mira waved at me from her aunt's gate when I passed and asked if I wanted company to the depot cart.

"No," I said. Voice still scraped.

She frowned the way she does when my color's wrong. "You look like you're about to do something stupid."

"I'm getting air."

"That's what people say right before they do something stupid." She shifted the towel sack on her shoulder. "If you're not back by noon, I'm telling Sugi-san."

"I'll be back."

---

The truck is already there.

Same gray-green. Same clipboard lieutenant with sweat in the wrong places. Two soldiers peeling people out of the shrine grounds by the arm — not cruel yet, just herding. Assessment. Refugee flow. Temporary. I hang back by the net-drying frames and tell myself I only came to look.

A boy about our age won't kneel when they tell him to. Jun — I've heard the name in Sugi's kitchen, Hanae's brother, Haiga dust still in the seams of his shoes. The kind of kid who grins at umeboshi like it's a festival and then walks straight into a rifle because someone said *ground* and he still believes *no* means something. He's got papers half in his fist. He's talking too fast about his sister, about a satchel, about a soldier who tore something off an old net-hauler near the plum tree.

"On the ground," the soldier says. Bored. Same voice the roof had. "Last time."

Jun doesn't go down.

My throat tightens the way it does before I speak now — raw, like the words have to push through sand. I came here because sitting in Sugi's house was going to split me open. I step out from the frames far enough that the wind hits my teeth.

"Hey," I say. Not loud. Careful. "Enough. Leave him."

The soldier's grip on Jun's shoulder loosens. Just for a second. Jun slips sideways. The soldier looks at his own empty hand, then at me, then shrugs it off as sun and overtime and grabs for Jun again.

"Stop," I try, clearer, and my nose goes hot. I taste metal. "Stop moving. Put the—"

Jun twists. The soldier swears. The sentence dies unfinished because unfinished is what I do now when I'm scared of my own tongue. The barrel comes up — warning distance, not kill distance yet — and Jun is still standing with papers in his fist, and I hear the roof in my skull, and I say it anyway, desperate and half-built:

"Drop it. Get away from him—"

The rifle dips. The soldier's shoulders hitch. For half a second the world leans the way I want it to. Then his head shakes once, hard, and the barrel comes back up clean. Jun is still standing in the wrong place. I have blood on my upper lip and nothing finished.

"Last chance," the soldier says, almost bored, and steps in on Jun instead of away.

I start moving forward. Someone's hand catches my jacket from the side.

Mira.

She's breathing hard, like she ran the last lane. Towel sack over one shoulder. Her eyes go from my face to the blood to the soldier to Jun, and for the first time since she walked into Sugi's kitchen with clean towels she doesn't look merely worried. She looks like she's seen something that should not move that way.

"Kaito," she says, low. "Don't. You'll get him shot. You'll get *you* shot. Come—"

"He's going to—"

"I know what it looks like. Come."

The soldier shoves Jun to his knees. Jun's temple hits the pier plank. Papers scatter. Hanae's voice cuts from the shrine path — a raw *no* — and then there are more hands, more gray-green, and the lieutenant with the clipboard is writing.

Mira hauls me back behind the net frames while they load Jun into the truck with two others — dazed, temple bleeding.

Mira presses the edge of her own sleeve to my lip without asking. "You're bleeding."

"Dry air."

"Don't." She keeps the sleeve there. "What did you do back there?"

"Nothing."

"His hand let go of the boy when you talked. Then the rifle dipped when you talked again." She shakes her head once. "I don't know what I saw. But it wasn't nothing, and it wasn't dry air."

"Jun—"

"I'll tell Hanae which truck. I know which depot they use on Tuesdays." She's already shifting into helpful again, but her eyes keep flicking back to my mouth. "Go home. Before Sugi-san counts heads. And don't practice whatever that was in the lane where people can hear you."

"I wasn't practicing."

"Good," she says. "Don't start."

---

Haru is mending nets when I come through the gate. He looks up, sees the blood, sees my face, and sets the net down carefully.

"You said inside," he says.

"I said sure." My voice is worse than this morning. "They took Jun at the Pier. I tried to stop them and I couldn't."

"Are you hurt?"

"No. Just my nose. It's nothing."

"Did the soldiers see you?"

"I don't think so. Mira did. She pulled me back. She's telling Hanae about the depot."

Haru is quiet for a second. "Mira was there."

"She brought towels to the shrine gym. She saw the truck. She's won't tell, Haru."

"I didn't say she was." He stands. "Come inside. I'll get water for your face. And you're going to tell me what 'tried' means, because your lip doesn't bleed for nothing."

"Later."

"Now," he says, and then softer: "Or tonight. But not never."
