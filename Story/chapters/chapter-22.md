# Chapter 22 — Tight Road

We reach the pine fork before the two sets of boots do.

The shed is half a roof and three walls of warped board, set back from the track where a cart could hide without inventing a story. Mira stops us short of the clearing and whistles once — two notes, not a tune anyone would hum for fun.

A dark jacket answers from the shed door. Hair pinned flat. The contact from the laundry court. She looks at my bandage, then at Mira, then past us into the trees as if counting shadows.

"You're late," she says. "And you're loud for people who were supposed to take the long cut."

"Marks got interrupted," Mira says. "We had to cut wider. Is she here?"

"She's inside, awake, and asking questions I don't answer." The contact tips her chin at me. "Keep that mouth the wage length. She's already decided what she thinks of it."

The shed smells like dry pine and old oil. Suzumori sits on a crate with a blanket over her shoulders that is not the quiet-floor blanket. She looks smaller in daylight and somehow harder. Her eyes find my scraped voice before they find my face.

"Saeki," she says. "You brought him."

Mira is already looking past her at the track. "He opened the door. Paper wouldn't have. We can fight about whether that was smart when we aren't standing in a shed the hill can see."

"I'm fighting about it now." Suzumori's gaze does not leave my mouth. "I told him in that room to be careful where he points it. Looking at the blood on his wrist, careful didn't take."

I stay by the wall. My shoulder throbs in time with my pulse. I do not offer her a name. She does not ask for one.

"Who's behind us?" the contact asks Mira.

"Gray suit on the hill road yesterday afternoon. Pairs. And two people on the service path at dusk who weren't wearing gray-green." Mira says it flat. "They took the open pine stretch. We came around."

Suzumori's mouth tightens. "Scraped-voice hunt, or a boy who knows the shape?"

"Both, if we're unlucky." Mira crouches and redraws the fork in dust on the shed floor — track, road, a third line south toward scrub. "We don't sleep here. Truck's gone. Next billet is the farm lean-to past the ridge cut."

"No." The contact says it before the dust settles. "Split it. I take her south through the scrub tonight — two women and a cart story, nobody's hunting that. You two are the description. You draw the road north, shed whoever follows, and we meet past the fork in three days."

"Three days is three more nights of pairs learning the fringe," Mira says. "And she doesn't have three days of scrub in her legs, whatever she's told you."

"I can hear both of you," Suzumori says.

"Then settle it," the contact says. "It's your neck."

Suzumori looks at me — at the bandage, at the mouth she has already made up her mind about — long enough that I understand the argument is not about routes. It is about whether the latch travels with her or away from her.

"I stay with the loud one," she says finally. "If the wrong people find two women and a cart, a story saves nobody. A door might save somebody." She pulls the blanket tighter. "And I walk. I have been sitting in a clean room long enough."

The contact takes losing the way professionals take it — one breath, then the next problem. "Lean-to, then. You ride if you have to," she tells Suzumori. "He walks."

---

We move before the light gets kind.

The contact takes point with a sack that looks like market work. Mira stays with Suzumori. I take the rear because the rear is where mouths get used when something comes up from behind, and Mira would rather see my face when the hunger starts than hear it happen at her back.

The inland track after the fork is narrower. Pine gives way to mixed scrub — broom, a few stubborn plum trees gone wild, stone walls that used to mean somebody's field. Once we hear a motor on the open road and freeze until it passes. Once a dog barks from a farm.

Suzumori talks while she walks. Not loud. Not soft enough to ignore.

"You pointed him at a latch," she says to Mira. "Paper would have failed. I know. That does not make a boy's throat a tool you spend without asking what it costs him later."

"I asked," Mira says. "He chose."

"He chose angry." Suzumori glances back at me. "Anger opens doors clean. It also opens the wrong ones."

I could tell her about Jun. About a rifle under a man's own jaw. I do not. She has not earned those names, and I am out of the habit of offering them.

"I can hear you deciding not to answer," Suzumori says. "Good. Keep deciding that when someone asks you for a shortcut."

Mira's jaw works once. "We're not having that argument on an open track."

"I'm not naming what he is." Suzumori's stick of wood shifts in her hair as she ducks a branch. "I'm telling you that two loud mouths in one generation is a problem people like your gray suit will solve by putting both of them in the same locked room. I would rather not watch you help him do it."

Two mouths like that in one world. She said it under the linen, and she is saying the shape of it again now. I do not ask. The questions I do not understand stay behind my teeth.

---

The lean-to sits against a stone wall on the far side of the ridge cut.

It is empty when we arrive, which Mira treats as good news and the contact treats as a question. Inside: straw, a water jug, a folded tarp. Outside: a view of the open pine road for anyone willing to crawl to the wall's broken end.

The contact crawls. She comes back with dust on her chin.

"Pairs on the road," she says. "Not sweeping. Asking. I saw a clipboard."

"Asking what?" Mira says.

"Directions to a boy whose voice sounds hurt. They have the hospital story now — door, empty room, clerk who cries when you make him repeat it." The contact looks at me. "Your face is becoming a description."

The answer comes up ready — send them back to the hill, make them forget the scraped voice — and I stop it the same way I stopped it on the fringe: jaw locked, nothing under the ordinary air. Mira watches my mouth and nods once, small, like she saw how close I came.

Suzumori sits on the straw. "How long do we have?"

"Until someone smart connects the chalk marks to this wall," Mira says. "Or until the two from the service path walk into the same questions and point inland for free."

"They won't point," I say.

The contact looks over. Mira doesn't. She is suddenly very still, the way she gets when I am about to make a problem into a person.

"You sound sure," she says.

"I am. The tall one will lie to a clipboard and walk. The other one will keep asking shopkeepers and clerks until somebody tells him which way we went. They won't sit down in an office and draw a map for free — but they also won't turn around and go home. They'll stay on the marks."

"So, worse," the contact says.

"That's why we don't leave them marks," Mira says. She draws a line through the fork in the dust with her boot. "Dawn. South scrub. You go ahead and warn the next hand. We stay off the open road."

Suzumori watches me over the water jug. "You sound like you know those two."

"I know the type," I say, and drink before anyone asks which type.

---

Night hardens.

The contact leaves with the tarp rolled under one arm and a promise to whistle from the scrub line before first light if the next billet is clean. Mira takes first watch at the wall break. Suzumori sleeps like someone who has practiced sleeping in rooms that were not hers.

I do not sleep. My shoulder has found a new way to hurt. Every time the wind moves the pines I hear boots that are not there. Once they are there — real, two pairs, on the open road below the ridge — and Mira's hand finds my ankle in the dark and squeezes until they pass.

Voices carry up the cut, thin as wire.

"—hospital service path. Wet prints. Two, maybe three."

"Scraped voice?"

"Clerk said he never saw faces. Captain says heat. The gray suit says mouths."

A third voice, quieter, unused to shouting: "Anyone ask at Minato?"

"Tomorrow. He wants the bars that feed hands. Tonight he wants the pine road walked twice."

They move on. Mira's grip eases.

"Listener," she whispers. "He's tying your well story to the quiet floors. We are out of Takamatsu's patience."

"Haru—"

This time I say the half-name. Mira does not pretend she misheard.

"If he's on that road," she says, "he's walking into the same questions those pairs are asking. That is not a reason to go find him."

"I wasn't going to go find him."

"You were going to stand up." She is right. My legs had already decided. "Sit. Dawn is six hours. You can hate me for all six, but you still sit."

Suzumori's eyes are open in the dark. She does not speak. She does not need to. The dread she named under the linen is in the lean-to with us now — not as a lecture, as a fact about how many people are hunting the same scraped voice from different directions.

---

First light comes wrong.

The contact's whistle does not come from the scrub line. It comes from lower, toward the open road, short and cut off. Mira is on her feet before the echo dies. She pulls me to the wall break.

Below, on the pine road where the ridge cut spills gravel, three gray-greens have stopped a cart. The contact's dark jacket is among them — hands out, talking fast. Beside the cart, two civilians who are not civilians enough: a tall boy with his shoulders set wrong for surrender, and a shorter one who stands like the road owes him an argument.

I know the shorter one's stance before I know his face. Ordinary voice. Ordinary hands. The unfinished sentence living in his mouth whether he has said it yet or not.

Haruki.

The tall one has the tongue-click shape from the path — body half a step ahead of the boy he is walking with. A gray-green reaches for Haruki's sleeve. The tall one knocks the hand away. The cart horse startles. Someone shouts for them to kneel.

"Riku—" Haruki says, sharp, and the tall one answers by moving, so the name sticks whether I want it or not.

Mira's fingers dig into my arm hard enough to bruise.

"We go south," she says. "Now. Through the scrub. We do not go down there."

Suzumori is already beside us, blanket discarded. "If you leave him to that road, boy, you will spend the rest of your anger inventing a cleaner version of this morning."

"I'm not leaving him," I say, and the truth of it shocks me more than the lie would have. "I'm not walking into a clipboard either."

On the road Riku takes a swing at the nearest gray-green. It connects. The second soldier brings a rifle butt around; Riku catches it on his forearm and swears loud enough to carry. Haruki's voice rises — not scraped, not weighted the way mine weights, but the kind of careful talk that makes people pause when they should not pause. One gray-green's grip loosens. The third one's does not.

Mira looks at me. Really looks — the pier look, the tide-chart look, the look that asks me to choose before the world chooses for me.

"If you go down there," she says, "that rifle finishes its swing before you finish a hello. So either you do something, or we watch. I'm not choosing it for you."

Suzumori's hand finds the wall. "Decide, boy. Standing here is also a choice."

Below us Haruki turns his head as if he heard something on the ridge. For half a breath his face is aimed at our wall, at the lean-to, at me — not seeing, not missing. Just hunting the shape he walked a city to find.

I stand up into the open enough that if he looks harder he will find more than a shape.

Mira swears. Suzumori does not.

On the road a gray-green raises the rifle properly this time, not for a butt-stroke — for the shot that ends arguments. Riku moves to put himself in front of Haruki. Haruki's mouth is already forming a word.

I go down the ridge cut with my mouth already open.
