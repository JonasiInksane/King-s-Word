# Chapter 7 — Kill Yourself

Morning light through Sugi's kitchen window. Kettle on. Rice cooling. I sit at the table practicing the shape that worked in the alley — quiet, aimed, finished — when I think nobody can hear.

"You. Drop it. Three steps back."

My throat burns on every clause. Haru is in the doorway with a cup he isn't drinking.

"Louder doesn't make it cleaner," he says.

"I know."

"Then stop practicing where Sugi can hear the kettle and think you're talking to her."

"I'm not—" I cut myself off. "Help me check the aim. That's all. If the next one goes wrong, I'd rather it go wrong on a cup."

He sets the cup on the table between us. "Don't tell the cup to kill itself."

"Not funny."

"Wasn't trying to be."

Mira comes to the gate before the second pour cools. No towel sack. Hair stuck to her temple like she ran. She doesn't come inside.

"They're moving boys from the Tuesday truck," she says. "Verification through the lanes, then west. Hanae's at the bend already. If Jun's on that line, she'll see him."

Haru sets his hands flat on the table. "We're going."

"You're staying," Sugi says from the sink.

"Sugi—"

"I said—"

Engines fill the lane. Staying stops being a choice.

Boots. A loudspeaker telling residents to remain indoors while the Federation Defense Command conducts a security verification. Gray-green at the bend under the plum. People pressed into doorways. Mira's hand finds the gate latch and holds it without opening it.

I see Hanae first. Sunken eyes from yesterday's kitchen, fixed on the line of boys being walked past the dried-fish shop.

Jun.

He isn't free. He isn't home. He's in the middle of their line with the pier still written on the side of his head, and when he sees Hanae he tries to step toward her anyway.

"On the ground," a soldier shouts.

Jun shakes his head. "I have papers — my sister — I can show—"

Hanae's voice cuts raw across the lane. He keeps standing. The soldier's rifle comes up the way it did on the pier, warning first, and something in my chest decides before my head does.

I am already moving. Haru grabs for my sleeve and misses. Mira says my name once, sharp, and I am past the gate with the alley's clean grammar stacked behind my teeth.

"You. With the rifle." A breath. Blood already threatening. "Stop. Put it down. Step away from him."

The soldier's shoulders hitch. The barrel dips.

For half a second I think clearer is enough. Jun's eyes find mine — trust, clean and stupid — and I almost believe we get him.

Then the soldier's head shakes once, hard, and the barrel comes back up. He shoves Jun toward the stones with the butt of the rifle. Jun staggers, doesn't go down. Papers scatter; he snatches them back like the paper is the only door left.

"I said on the ground." Not loud. Bored in a way that is worse than shouting. "Last warning. You kneel, or this ends."

Hanae screams his name. Jun looks at her, then at me, and still doesn't kneel.

"Stop," I say again, louder. "Stop aiming at him. You — stop moving—"

Too many jobs. The soldier blinks, steps in instead of away.

"Don't—" My voice cracks. "Don't touch him—"

"Last chance," the soldier says, and for one stupid second I think he means it the pier way — warning distance, more time, another almost.

He doesn't.

The shot is small. Practical. The trust-look still on his face for a quarter-second longer than his body can use it.

"**No—!**"

The soldier turns. Rifle still up. Eyes on me now instead of the boy on the stones.

"Shut up," he says. "Hands where I can see them. Shut your—"

"Why don't you shut up," I hear myself say, raw and too loud, "or better — why don't you just kill yourself."

The last of it lands clean whether I meant it to or not.

The soldier does.

The lane holds the sound, then the silence after, except someone's radio still muttering procedures and Hanae's voice breaking open on a name.

Mira's hand finds my shoulder hard enough to turn me.

Haru is there somehow. He looks at Jun. He looks at me. His mouth opens. Nothing comes out.

Sugi's hand catches his wrist when he steps toward me and says one word, soft as a shut door:

"No."

More boots further down. The loudspeaker keeps talking to a street that has already failed the instruction.

"Kaito." Mira's voice is low, urgent. "You can't stay. If they find the mouth that did that, you die here before noon."

"Haru—" I try.

He stands on the other side of Sugi's arm. He still doesn't say anything I can use.

"Come with me," Mira says. "Inland. Kuroishi. I know people who can hide a boy the Federation wants to write down."

Haru's eyes find mine over Sugi's shoulder. My nose runs warm down my lip.

I leave with Mira.

We cut between sheds, then up the inland track. Mira does not praise me. Once, when my legs threaten to stop, she says, "Keep moving. Looking back won't put him on his feet."
