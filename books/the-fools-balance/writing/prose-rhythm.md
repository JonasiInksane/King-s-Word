# Prose Rhythm — Quiet Confidence

**Target:** narration that calmly tells what happened — not a performance of the story.

Readers should stop noticing the writing and live inside the scene.

Touchstones for the middle ground: Frieren, Dungeon Meshi, good literary fantasy — flowing, not poetic, not clipped.

---

## The three extremes

### 1. Overwritten (drift — cut this)

Every sentence asks to be admired. Readers become aware of the writing.

Bad:

> The road into Ashvale should have smelled of river mud and cooking smoke.  
> It smelled of wet ash and something sweet gone wrong.  
> The gate hung like a tooth.  
> Pride lay broken.  
> The fire had somehow lost interest.

Stack of metaphors / thesis lines / personified places. Cleverness as default.

### 2. Underwritten (also wrong)

Clean, but no rhythm. Reads like stage directions.

Bad:

> The road smelled of ash.  
> Renji stopped.  
> A toy lay there.  
> One wheel turned.

### 3. Middle ground (locked target)

Not poetic. Not clipped. Flowing. Varied length. Precise observation. Almost no narrator commentary.

Good:

> The road into Ashvale should have smelled of river mud and cooking smoke. Instead it smelled of wet ash.  
>  
> Renji slowed his horse.  
>  
> The gate leaned sideways, one hinge torn loose. Beyond it, roofs had collapsed into the street. A child's wooden horse lay in the dust with one wheel still turning.  
>  
> No voices.  
>  
> Just a bell.

---

## Rhythm (think music)

Do **not** default to:

- Short. Short. Short. Short.
- Long metaphor. Long metaphor. Long metaphor.

Prefer patterns like:

- Long → medium → long → short
- Long → short → dialogue
- Medium → short → medium

Rhythm matters more than any single sentence’s length.

---

## Observation, never interpretation

Write what can be seen, heard, touched, done. Do **not** explain the feeling for the reader.

| Observation (do) | Interpretation (cut) |
|---|---|
| The bells answered together. | The meadow poured its music through her ribs. |
| Bram opened his mouth, then closed it again. | Bram was overwhelmed. |
| Mira sat before she realized she'd stopped walking. | The meadow invited her to rest. |
| The stew was hot enough to sting Mira's tongue. She reached for another spoonful anyway. | The forest had decided to be kind for an evening. / The stew was warm. |

Nobody narrates “the meadow was magical” or “they were in awe.” The rhythm and details create the feeling.

---

## Quiet confidence checklist

Narration should have:

- [ ] flowing paragraphs (not telegram stacks, not metaphor stacks)
- [ ] varied sentence lengths
- [ ] precise observations
- [ ] strong nouns and verbs
- [ ] almost no narrator commentary

Then let:

- **dialogue** create personality
- **actions** create emotion
- **the world** create wonder

If a line is trying to impress, cut it. Trust the scene.

---

## Bellvine test (rhythm without labeling wonder)

Overwritten: *The meadow poured its music through her ribs.*  
Too plain: *Mira sat down.*  

Balance:

> Wind moved through the Bellvines.  
> The bells answered together.  
> Not loud.  
> Just enough that nobody spoke.  
>  
> Bram folded his arms.  
> Nia wandered into the flowers after a Crystal Bee.  
> Mira watched them for a moment, then followed.

---

## Ship rule

Before calling a chapter done: read one page aloud. If you hear admiration-hunting or stage-direction stutter, rewrite toward observation + rhythm.
