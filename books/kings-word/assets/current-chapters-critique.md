# Current Chapters Critique — Ch 1–23 vs the Example Standard

**Writer-only.** Companion to `assets/example-style-guide.md`. That file says what the examples do; this file says where our live chapters fall short of it, ranked by damage, then chapter by chapter.

Scope note: the hard rules in `.cursor/rules/story-writing.mdc` still bind. Nothing here licenses reintroducing banned devices (ledger voice, personified places, telegram dialogue). This critique is about the *next* level of craft — the gap between "clean" and "as good as the examples."

---

## Part 1 — Global recurring faults (ranked by damage)

### 1. Dialogue as relay, not transaction — the world is too cooperative

**The biggest gap.** In the examples, every conversation is a trade: both sides want something, both pay something, and information arrives *bent* by the teller's interest (Lucia has to be bribed and still shorts the answer; the potato scene makes Tristan peel for an hour to earn three sentences; Fortuna gives help wrapped in vanity that costs Angharad patience). In our chapters, NPCs frequently exist to hand the protagonist exactly what the plot needs, priced at one token gruffness up front.

- Ch 13: Haruki works Kuroishi and the shop woman, the well neighbor, the crank man, the croquette seller, and a delivery boy hand over the whole trail between them. Each is individually fine; in sequence they are a chain of info dispensers. Nobody misleads him, sandbags him, or wants something back.
- Ch 15: the tea seller delivers the hunting thesis ("stop chasing where he's been; stand where he has to keep going back to") complete and correct, then *labels it free*. Gorō (Ch 15, 17) narrates himself as a free map. The examples never let wisdom announce itself as a gift; it leaks out sideways from self-interest.
- Ch 22: the contact and Suzumori both align with Mira within a page. Three adults with different stakes produce one plan with no real counter-proposal.

**Fix pattern:** before an NPC yields anything, ask: what do they want out of this exchange, what wrong or partial version do they give first, and what does the asker pay (work, coin, embarrassment, a favor owed, a lie they have to maintain later)? One refusal that *stays* refused per arc would do more for the world's texture than five new helpful strangers.

### 2. Scene middles compressed to summary — goal → result, skipping the complication

The examples dramatize the middle: the plan meets friction on-page and the friction is where character shows (the dice game, the murder-frame morning, the docks standoff). Our chapters often narrate the middle instead — montage of work, days passing, roads walked — and the actual turn arrives in half a page.

- Ch 12 is almost entirely connective logistics (haul montage, bus, market-work montage). No scene in it changes the situation; it moves bodies between cities.
- Ch 16–17 compress weeks of Takamatsu into iterated routine ("I run the same seven streets… On the fourth day…"). The *notice* beat and Tanabe's firing are strong; the tissue between them is summary the examples would have either cut harder or dramatized as one full scene.
- Ch 21's middle (chalk marks, walking, rice balls) iterates the same beat — check mark, walk, worry — three times where one dramatized complication (a mark that's wrong, a stranger on the track who must be talked past) would carry more.

**Fix pattern:** per chapter, budget one *full* scene for the complication — a plan visibly failing and being improvised around on-page — rather than three half-scenes of transit. Summary is for time, not for conflict.

### 3. Thesis and motif restatement across chapters — re-teaching at the theme level

The rules ban re-teaching beats; the same discipline has to apply to *lines and ideas*. The examples repeat motifs (the List, the Law of Rats) but every recurrence adds new information or lands in a new context. Ours restate:

- "Nobody is coming to fix any of this" — Ch 10, again Ch 17 ("that version still won't stand"), again Ch 18 (verbatim, to Riku), again Ch 23 (Haruki repeats it to Kaito). Powerful once. By the fourth appearance it is a slogan.
- The six-syllable unfinished sentence ("It wasn't your fault") — carried in narration in Ch 10, 13, 14, 15, and paid in Ch 23. The *payment* is right; the reminders en route are too many and too explicit. The examples would trust the reader after one reminder per block.
- "A machine for not knowing people" — Ch 15 and 16.
- "Boring is the whole uniform" / boring-boy act — earned Ch 8–10, then echoed as a label in 13 and 15.
- Kaito's cost tell runs the same sequence nearly verbatim each use: copper/weight → "blood finds my upper lip" → "the weight leaves my throat hard enough that the [X] tips" (Ch 6, 9, 11, 20, 23). Rationed per chapter, yes — but the *phrasing* is a stamp. Vary the order, the body part, the aftermath.
- Signature gestures over-fire: Mira's "tide-chart" face (Ch 6, 8, 11, 19, 22, 23), Riku's tongue-click (near-every appearance), Kaito's "my hands want a crate" (Ch 19, 20, 22).

**Fix pattern:** one appearance per motif per POV block, and each recurrence must *do* something new (recontextualize, invert, get contradicted) or be cut.

### 4. The protagonists are never wrong — partial and false knowledge underused

Example POV characters misread people constantly, and the misreads drive plot (Angharad's blind spots about Isabel and Cozme; Tristan under-reading the twins). Our leads' reads are almost always correct: Haruki's people-reads land, Mira's calls are always right, rumors the boys act on turn out true. The one real misread — the wrong boy at the docks (Ch 16) — is among the best beats in the Takamatsu block precisely because it costs Haruki something.

Related: cross-camera dramatic irony is underexploited. The Haruki block (13–18) and Kaito block (19–23) run parallel but barely recontextualize each other until the near-miss in Ch 21. The examples make alternating POV pay every pair of chapters — each camera holds a piece the other misreads. Ours mostly just take turns.

**Fix pattern:** per block, give the POV one confident read that is *wrong* and let the error bill arrive on-page. Feed each camera a detail the other camera's reader knows is false or incomplete.

### 5. Helper-NPC archetype repetition — the gruff feeding auntie ×6

Sugi, Orihara, Orii, Machi, the tea seller, the squid woman: brusque older woman, tests the boy, feeds him, hides the kindness, deflects thanks. Each is individually good — together they reveal a default. (Sato escapes it by being institutional; Hanae by being broken.) The examples spread their side registers wide: vain, silky, brittle, falsely sweet, cheerfully corrupt. Our male NPCs (Gorō, Jun, Riku, Tanabe) show more variety than our women do.

**Fix pattern:** for the next helper NPC, pick a register off the used shelf — someone who helps out of vanity, out of boredom, for gossip value, or while being genuinely unpleasant. Let one helper be someone the boys *dislike* and still owe.

### 6. Close-type monotony — the walking button

Openings are genuinely strong across the book (most chapters start in motion, mid-task, no throat-clearing — this matches the examples). Closes cluster on one type: quiet resumption of movement/silence.

- Ch 11 "I keep my mouth shut until morning, the way she asked." Ch 12 "…join the line of boys waiting for the afternoon load." Ch 15 "Tomorrow I start standing near the places that hide." Ch 19 "I don't look back at the stationery shop." Ch 20 "I keep my mouth shut and follow her." Ch 21 "…the only signal left is the one she trusts more than chalk." Ch 23 "I keep walking."

Individually each obeys the rules (concrete, no poetry). In sequence they are the same chord. The examples rotate closes: decision, reveal, threat, arrival of a new person, a question that changes the goal. Ours also sit on the quiet-exit chord for chapters that *end volumes* (23 earns it; 21 and 20 back-to-back don't both need it).

**Fix pattern:** track the last two chapter closes before writing a new one; if both were quiet-movement, this one must end on a reveal, a decision spoken aloud, or a new arrival.

### 7. Mira is infallible and want-less on-page

Mira never miscalls a risk, never pays a personal price, and her motive arrives late and thin ("I knew her," Ch 19). She functions as a competence dispenser the way the auntie NPCs function as food dispensers. The examples give every recurring ally visible self-interest and at least one on-page failure (even Fortuna, even Yong). Mira's steering would read *stronger*, not weaker, if one of her calls burned them — it would prove the danger is real and make Kaito's compliance a choice rather than physics.

**Fix pattern (Vol 3):** one Mira mistake with a cost that persists; one scene where what *she* wants (aunt's road, debts, Suzumori's network) visibly conflicts with what is safe for Kaito, and she picks her side.

### 8. Haruki's own power stalls between Ch 10 and Ch 23

The rules already flag this at the plot level; it is also a craft-standard gap. The examples escalate every power with usage and mounting cost. Haruki's too-useful voice gets one strong dramatization (Sato's office, Ch 10; echoed nicely at Tanabe's, Ch 16) and then rides as flavor — "the kind of careful talk that makes people pause when they should not pause" (Ch 22–23) — without Haruki noticing, testing, or paying for it. His block (13–18) runs almost entirely on *Kaito's* mystery. Chapters on his camera need his problem advancing, not just the trail.

**Fix pattern (Vol 3):** each Haruki chapter should move his own thread one notch — a moment where his ask works *too* well and someone gets hurt by complying, or where he catches himself about to lean on it and doesn't know what "it" is.

## Part 3 — What already meets standard (do not "fix" these in rewrites)

- **Voice separation.** Haruki and Kaito pass the one-paragraph test almost everywhere. Do not homogenize while smoothing.
- **Openings.** In motion, mid-task, no throat-clearing — book-wide strength.
- **Found family shown, never named.** Holds throughout; Riku and Machi beats are exemplary.
- **Magic cost on-page.** Every Word use pays visibly. Only the *phrasing* of the cost needs variety (fault #3), never the presence.
- **Public-vs-private tells.** Knowledge hygiene around the scraped voice / metal taste is clean across 23 chapters.
- **Model chapters for any rewrite pass:** Ch 3, 7, 9, 14, 20 — plus the user-edited 5–10 pass as canon voice.
