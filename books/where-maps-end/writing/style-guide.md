# Style guide — Where Maps End

## Voice

Simple light-novel prose. Short sentences. Paragraphs of 1–4 sentences. Lots of white space. No purple canopy every paragraph.

**Bad:** The verdant arboreal canopy shimmered beneath the ethereal luminescence of crystalline pollen.

**Good:** Crystal pollen drifted through the trees. The whole forest glittered. Nobody spoke for a while.

## Never describe what the reader can infer

**Bad:** Niko was nervous.  
**Good:** Niko checked the map for the fourth time.

**Bad:** Marta was scared.  
**Good:** Marta stopped walking. She had never stopped walking before.

## Dialogue-heavy

Aim for ~60–70% of scenes carried by talk. Personality through exchange, not labels.

### Dialogue is the scene

**Hard rule (updated):** When the beat is relationship, comfort, friction, naming, worry, or “who are we together,” **the dialogue is the scene.** People can stop packing. Hands can go idle. The talk *is* the event.

Do not demote every conversation to seasoning on choreography. Action-during-talk is still useful texture—not a requirement that starves the exchange.

Still not TBATE first-person: no joke tags, no self-narration (“I’m such a rebel”), no lore lectures dressed as chat. Stay 3rd-limited, underwritten voices. Warmth and length come from *people talking to each other*, not from the author explaining feelings.

**Permission:** let someone be soft, dumb, stubborn, or fond out loud for a page. Then resume the road.

### Breathing room

Not everyone is interesting all the time.

Sometimes Bram simply walks. No pot, no salt, no lesson. Sometimes Lumi just eats. Conversations end without resolution (“That’s a philosophy, not lunch.” — then silence).

**Hard rule:** if every appearance teaches a quirk, cut until someone can exist without performing.

Pointless tags: “Shame.” / “Close enough.” No sitcom last lines.

### Less choreography

Not every realistic micro-action needs the page.

Bad: She opened the pouch. Checked. Closed it. Opened it. Checked again.  
Good: Marta checked the packs one last time.

Readers fill in the rest. Prefer one clean beat over five “written” ones.

### Sentence rhythm

Don’t stack identical beats forever: *He looked. She walked. Bram nodded. Niko followed.*

Occasionally combine movement into one fluid line:

Marta was already halfway across the square before Bram stopped arguing long enough to follow. Niko grabbed the pot and hurried after them.

Still short. More alive.

### Travel memories

Every chapter needs one memory that isn’t a plot point—something readers miss five volumes later. Mossgate: Marta paying for four buns; flour on Lumi’s book. Mystery hooks are not enough.

### Wonder (not foreshadowing)

At least sometimes, put one beautiful thing on the page that exists only because the dungeon is beautiful. Crystal butterflies on a pot. Resin light between trunks. Nobody explains. Nobody turns it into a clue. Bram waits; they leave; he cooks.

### Observation lenses

Characters notice different things in the same place:

- Niko — maps, absences, tracks that quit  
- Lumi — season, specimens, charts  
- Bram — food, what shouldn’t grow here  
- Marta — light, distance, when to camp  

Do not make everyone comment on Niko’s notice. Let four worlds overlap.

### Bonding habits

Party talk *can* interrupt, finish sentences, steal food. Curiosity *can* be shared. Don’t always end on Marta’s shut-down.

**Don’t formula it.** Not every chapter is finish→twist→button. Let lines hang. Let answers arrive late or never.

### Useless minutes

Kettle rattles for two minutes. Sock wedge fails. Never matters again. People, not story functions.

### No craft on the page

Never narrate the rule. Just write the behavior.

Bad: *Nobody asked. It never came up again.* / *without making a speech of it* / *It wasn’t awkward.*
Good: the kettle rattles into the dark; Marta pockets the glove; a lizard on a warm stone.

If a sentence exists to reassure the reader you’re obeying a craft note (silence, useless minutes, “not a performance”), cut it.

### Scene length and scale

Japanese LNs let scenes breathe — and they **change scale** between chapters.

Ch 1 can live in Mossgate. Later chapters should not all be town-sized:

- leave town  
- already deep in forest  
- wake in camp  
- already halfway through the expedition  

Vary how much time a chapter spends in one place. Breathing ≠ every chapter is 600 lines of packing.

### Characters shouldn’t sound like writers

**Hard rule:** If a line would make a great quote on social media, rewrite it.

Not because good lines are bad. Because if everyone speaks in quotable lines, nobody sounds real. Reserve those moments almost exclusively for the Jester—and even then, make them accidental.

Western witty ≠ Frieren / Dungeon Meshi / Witch Hat — but those series also let talk *be* the scene (campfire, naming, worry). Copy that permission, not TBATE’s narrator voice.

Prefer underwritten talk that is still distinctive: awkward, repetitive, interrupted, uncertain, matter-of-fact—not bland. Personality from rhythm and what they care about—not polished wording.

Simple ≠ neutral. Fern says “You’re late,” not “Hello.” Senshi says “Don’t throw that away,” not “Hello.”

### Camera follows the party

Write as if the camera is following the party. Between dialogue scenes, people keep living: walking, cooking, packing, pinning maps.

When a talk scene starts, **stay with the talk.** Don’t cut away every two lines to prove someone is tying a knot.

### Messy talk

Groups are slightly chaotic. People interrupt, ignore questions, answer the wrong thing, talk over each other, misunderstand.

**Dialogue should rarely answer the exact question asked** — but stay in character. Marta answers sideways with *reality*, not filler weather:

“Do you think the maps are wrong?” → “They’re paper.” / “We’ll look at the real thing.”

Messy ≠ telegram. Same idea, more breath: character beats between lines, and sometimes a whole stretch where they just… keep talking.

**Cut empty bridge dialogue.** Not every “Good.” needs “For what?” — but do allow real back-and-forth when the relationship needs it.

**Never use an ellipsis (`…` / `...`) if a character can do something instead** — except rare mid-line revision (“Wrong mushroom.”). Prefer action or the next spoken line.

Silence comes from what people don’t say, from interruptions, from changing attention. Reality can interrupt — it doesn’t have to every paragraph.

Manga-panel rhythm still works for *action* beats:

Marta took the rope.  
Pulled once.  
Knotted it.  
“Books.”

**Allow thirty useless seconds.** A dog follows for thirty paces, then leaves. Bram sneezes; nobody reacts. Also allow thirty seconds of *useless talk* — nothing advances the map; someone just complains about bread.

**Trust the action.** If Bram uses a pot lid as a windbreak, stop. Don’t add a sentence proving he’s thoughtful.

**Characters aren’t finished yet.** Early Marta / Bram / Lumi should feel like people who will become themselves over dozens of chapters—not fully formed anime cast sheets on day one. Leave gaps. Let them be wrong, blunt, unfinished.

Narration should speed up and slow down. Let Niko’s hesitation show in tiny beats (“Probably.” after a frown). Thought-like narration > author description.

**Bad:** “Maps eat time. Time eats people.” / “Proof is expensive. Answers are cheaper.” / “Contradictions like doors.”  
**Good:** “Did you pack enough salt?” / “If you can prove it, great.” / “That doesn’t make sense.”

**Thinking speeds**

| Character | Speaks like |
|---|---|
| **Niko** | Observes. Asks. Rarely concludes. “Did you notice—” |
| **Marta** | Short. Practical. No metaphors. “Pack.” “Move.” “We’ll see.” |
| **Bram** | Talks while doing things. Always food/practical. “Don’t touch that.” “Needs more salt.” |
| **Lumi** | Starts explaining. Stops. Corrects herself. “Actually—” “No, wait.” |
| **Jester** | Only one allowed to sound strange—that’s his identity. |

If everyone already speaks in memorable quotes, the Jester loses uniqueness.

Example rhythm:

"Are you sure this is the right path?"  
Marta kept walking.  
"No."  
"You sound calm."  
She adjusted her pack.  
"I've accepted it."

## Every scene needs

At least one of: discovery · joke · emotion · mystery · ecological observation.

**And** every scene must **change something**: a relationship, a theory, the route, the ecosystem, a clue, or a decision. Never pure travel. Never static.

## Exposition

Hide it. Never lecture.

**Bad:** "The Crystal Deer are herbivores that eat crystal moss."

**Good:** Bram watches one eat crystal moss. "So that's why their antlers sparkle."

## Comedy

Dry. Almost British. The Jester says ridiculous things like they're obvious.

"Can we eat it?"  
"You can eat almost anything once."  
"..."  
"Twice is harder."

## Action

Very visual. Clear. Short. No paragraph-long attacks. Dialogue can run in motion.

Ecology-first combat is welcome: small road fights and monster scrapes between wonder beats — not boss rooms, not a Demon King ladder, not passive tourism. Ordinary threats (glass wolves, beetle swarms, desperate scavengers, bad trail encounters) get fought, driven off, or slain. Apex pressure is survived by reading signs. Party roles differ in a scrape: Marta leads steel and exits; Bram uses pot, lid, noise, environment; Lumi magic or practical light/tools; Niko notices flanks and timing. Never narrate the combat ethos on-page.

The stag lowered its head.  
Nobody moved.  
Leaves drifted from its antlers.  
Marta whispered, "Run."  
Nobody argued.

Also fine:

The wolf came low for the packs.  
Marta met it with the flat of her blade.  
Bram’s lid rang off its skull.  
“Left—” Niko said.  
Lumi’s spark cracked the air.  
It left bleeding.

## Emotion

Quiet. Campfire. Cooking. Repairing gear. Watching spores float. No melodrama.

## Chapter endings

Every chapter ends with a reason to turn the page.

Not necessarily a cliffhanger. Just curiosity.

Chapter 1 success test: the reader thinks *I want to go with them.*

## Tone pulse

Cozy camp → wonder → mapping/cooking/magic work → sudden prey-terror → aftermath that remembers the signs were there.

## What to avoid

- Boss-room video-game fights
- Jester cool mode or exposition dumps
- Niko sudden inspiration / “proving” lore dumps
- Saying “First Conservatory” on-page in Vol 1
- Closing cosmic mysteries early
- Misery tourism without leisure and beauty

## Home arc

Campfires brush belonging without speeches. Food shared, rope counted, jokes that land, maps redrawn together.

## Names and culture

Map Wall, guild songs, shrines, crystal kids — sprinkle, don’t dump.
