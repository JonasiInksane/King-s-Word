# AI Writing Prompt — Where Maps End (all chapters)

You are writing a modern Japanese light novel inspired by Frieren, Dungeon Meshi, and Witch Hat Atelier, while keeping this series’ own identity.

**The goal is NOT prettier prose.**  
**The goal is characters who feel like people travelling together.**

After a chapter, the reader should think: *I want to travel with this party* — not *I understand the plot.*

---

## Before writing any chapter

1. `PROGRESS.md`
2. `tracking/chapter-log.md` + `continuity.md` + `anti-leakage.md`
3. This prompt + `writing/style-guide.md` + `writing/dialogue-guide.md`
4. `lore/plot-outline.md` beat for this chapter
5. Relevant ecology/regions + character sheets
6. Previous chapter

Do not invent plot against the outline. Do not leak other books’ cameras or cast (`anti-leakage.md`).

---

## General style

- 3rd person limited; stay inside the scene’s POV (default **Niko**); past tense
- Easy, natural English
- Short paragraphs (1–4 sentences); white space matters
- Never write beautifully for the sake of sounding beautiful
- Every description = something the POV actually notices
- Narration is not the author describing the world — we stand beside the POV
- Never describe what the reader can infer from action
- Target ~2,000–3,500 words unless the beat is intentionally short

---

## Dialogue (highest priority)

**Dialogue is often the scene.** Relationship, friction, comfort, worry, naming — stay with the talk. Hands can go idle. Do not demote every exchange to seasoning on choreography.

**Let scenes breathe when the place can hold it — and vary scale.** Not every chapter is Mossgate-length. Idle time. Pointless lines. People exist without teaching. Less choreography (one pack-check beats five pouch opens). Conversations may end unresolved. Aim for one shared party moment early (a laugh that includes everyone).

Do **not** make characters speak in memorable quotes.

If a line would make a great social-media quote because it is clever — **rewrite it**.

Memorable characters > memorable lines.

Still not first-person webnovel: no joke tags, no self-narration, no lore lectures as chat. Warmth = people talking to each other.

### Filters

**Niko** — notices; **starts** questions he can’t let go (not only replies); rarely concludes; curiosity first.

**Marta** — practical; action before theory; rarely answers the exact wording; redirects toward doing something; brief. Indirect is fine. Disconnected is not. Can hold a longer plain conversation about the road.

Bad: “You think the maps are wrong?” / “I think it’s going to rain.”  
Better: “They’re paper. We’ll look at the real thing.” — and then let Niko keep asking for a beat.

**Bram** — thinks through food; touching/smelling/carrying/cooking; assumes others know what he knows; not intentionally funny; naturally funny. Let him argue out loud (bread, salt, mushrooms).

**Lumi** — starts before finishing; corrects herself; loses track; awkward but intelligent; her half-explanations *are* scenes.

**Jester** — only character allowed to naturally sound strange. Nobody else should accidentally sound like him.

---

## Conversation rhythm

Rarely: Question → Answer → Question → Answer → end.

Instead: drift. Interruptions. Subject changes. Someone ignores. Someone answers the wrong thing. Sometimes they just keep talking because they’re people.

**Dialogue should rarely answer the exact question asked** — but allow real back-and-forth when the relationship needs air.

---

## Action during dialogue

Activity is **texture**, not a leash.

Talk while packing is fine. Stopping to talk is fine when the talk *is* the beat.

Never use an ellipsis (`…` / `...`) if a character can do something instead — or say the next line.

Between dialogue scenes, life continues. During a talk scene, stay with the talk.

---

## Scene flow

Every scene needs ≥1 of: discovery · relationship · comedy · ecological observation · mystery · decision.

Scenes can move by **conversation changing a relationship** — not only by walking to a new street.

Scenes end because something happens — not because the topic is exhausted.

---

## Group dynamics

Parties assemble and bond through small interactions, not exposition. Arrive mid-conversation when you can — history already running.

Examples: Bram rearranges someone’s pack without asking; Marta fixes gear; Lumi gets distracted; Bram buys/handles supplies; Marta keeps everyone moving; Niko notices what others ignore.

Show personality through repeated behaviour — never explain it; never reinforce the same trait twice in a row. Let scenes wander thirty seconds (dog, sneeze, glove) with no plot job. Early cast is unfinished—room to become themselves.

Readers should begin to recognize speakers without tags.

---

## Series rules (still bind)

- Wonder + cozy + quiet melancholy; prey-terror fair and foreshadowed
- Exploration over boss clearing; ecology before loot
- Volume scientific question advances; answers open two new questions
- Leave one mystery unexplored per expedition
- No Demon King / power ladder framing
- Cosmic ending truths unset
- Camera follows the party; dialogue often *is* the scene (relationship beats)
- Chapter ending: reason to turn the page (curiosity, not always cliffhanger)
