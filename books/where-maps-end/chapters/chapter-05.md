# Chapter 5 — Root Camp

The spur marks held.

East of the Coil, the trees thinned by degrees—first more light, then wheel ruts again, then the smell of ink boiled too long. Canvas roofs. Sample tables. A kettle that was not Bram’s. A wooden sign leaned on a stake like it had argued with wind and lost.

*Root Camp — Academy Station*  
*Wipe your boots. Label your jars. Do not argue with the ground.*

Lumi made a noise that was almost home.

“I left a sock here,” she said. “Possibly two. Don’t ask.”

“I wasn’t going to,” Bram said.

“Good. Because I don’t know where they are.”

A Scholar in a stained coat looked up from a tray of moss plugs, saw Marta, and raised two fingers. Marta returned them. The Scholar’s eyes moved to Lumi and warmed.

“You’re back.”

“I’m passing through,” Lumi said. “We’re surveying. Independent. Guild. The disagreeing maps. You know the ones.”

“Everyone knows the ones,” the Scholar said. “Nobody likes them.”

Niko held up the rolls anyway, because that was his job.

The Scholar glanced, then went back to the plugs.

“Wipe your boots,” they said. “The ground here keeps score.”

---

Root Camp was smaller than Mossgate and louder in a different way. People argued about spore counts. Someone cried quietly over a ruined sample and was handed tea. A board listed open questions in chalk, half erased, half rewritten. A dog of no clear ownership followed them for thirty paces, sniffed Bram’s pot, and left.

Lumi drifted toward a stack of textbooks under a tarp. Marta caught her elbow.

“We eat first.”

“I was only—”

“Looking. I know.”

Bram had already found the camp cook’s fire. Niko heard negotiation before he saw it—Bram’s voice low, the cook’s higher, the word *onion* repeated like a treaty clause.

“Three,” Bram said when he returned, holding them like medals.

“How?” Marta asked.

“I explained hunger.”

“That’s not an explanation.”

“It worked.”

They ate sitting on a log that had been a log for other expeditions. Warm food. Real salt. Someone else’s stew base improved by Bram’s onions and a pinch of his own. Lumi talked with her mouth full about people Niko didn’t know—who stole jars, who slept through spore alarms, who cried over charts—then stopped mid-name when she realized none of them were here.

“Sorry,” she said. “I forget who I’m with.”

“You’re with us,” Marta said.

Lumi looked at her bowl.

“Yeah.”

Bram stole a piece from her bowl while she was looking at the tarp. She stole one back without looking at him. Niko watched the exchange and decided not to comment. Marta’s hand was already in his bowl for a potato.

“Hey—”

“You hesitate,” Marta said. “Food doesn’t.”

Across the clearing, two Scholars argued about whether a jar lid was “academy standard” or “field standard.” The argument ended when someone put both lids on the same jar. Nobody won. Everyone ate.

---

After food, Lumi took Niko to the sample tables. Bram followed because the cook fire had onions and he had opinions. Marta came because she didn’t leave people unsupervised near textbooks.

“Look. Crystal moss from three ridges. Same species. Different sparkle.” Lumi flipped a heavy book open to a diagram, triumphant. “The textbook says sparkle tracks mana density. See? If the moss is dull, mana is low. If it’s bright—”

She held a plug from the tray beside the page.

The plug was bright.

The diagram’s “bright” example was duller.

Lumi frowned. Turned the page. Turned it back. Checked the caption. Checked the tray label. Checked the date on the book’s inside cover.

“That’s… wrong.”

“The moss?” Niko asked.

“The book.” She said it like she was accusing a friend. “This edition’s chart says brightness scales with density. This sample is brighter than the chart’s maximum and the field meter reads—” She tapped a crystal needle stuck in the plug. “—normal. Mid. Not high.”

Bram peered over her shoulder.

“So the book’s hungry.”

“Books don’t get hungry.”

“Yours do,” Bram said. “They eat margins.”

A Scholar at the next table snorted without looking up.

“If you’re going to argue with editions, take it outside. Some of us are trying to fail quietly.”

Lumi sat down hard on a stool.

“It’s a small thing,” she said. “A calibration. A printing error. Or the moss changed. Or the meter’s lying. Or I am.”

“You’re not,” Niko said.

“You don’t know that.”

“I know you check twice.”

Marta, from the edge of the tarp: “Write what you see. Leave the book its pride.”

Lumi wrote. Her hand shook once, then steadied. The note in her margin was smaller than usual: *chart fails sample RC-12 — brightness ≠ meter. Don’t trust edition.*

Niko watched her cross out *Don’t trust edition* and write *Don’t trust this page.*

Better. Smaller. True.

She closed the book gently, as if it might apologize if given privacy.

“I liked this edition,” she said.

“You can like it and still correct it,” Niko said.

“Academy doesn’t always agree.”

“We’re not academy today,” Marta said. “We’re boots.”

Bram poked the bright plug with a careful finger and wiped the glitter on his trousers.

“Still smells like moss,” he reported. “Not like lying.”

“Moss doesn’t lie,” Lumi said. “People print.”

---

They stayed the afternoon because Marta allowed it and because Root Camp had water that didn’t need tasting to be trusted.

Niko sat at a borrowed table and copied two local sketches onto scrap—spur trail to quarry cut, lake road hours beyond. The sketches were messy. Useful. Someone had drawn a kettle in the margin of the quarry scrap for no reason he could invent. He left the kettle there. It felt like company.

A Scholar asked if he wanted tea. He said yes. The tea tasted like boiled patience and chalk dust. He drank it anyway. Across the clearing, Bram taught nobody a lesson about onions and somehow still looked instructional. Lumi reorganized three books that did not need reorganizing and then reorganized them back.

Lumi wandered the tarp stacks and came back with flour on one cuff, unexplained. Bram negotiated a second pinch of salt and lost a third onion argument with dignity. Marta repaired a strap on Lumi’s crate while Lumi pretended not to notice and then noticed too hard.

“You don’t have to—”

“I already did,” Marta said.

“Thank you.”

“Don’t make it a speech.”

“It wasn’t.”

“Good.”

A Researcher tried to sell Niko certainty about the three Guild maps.

“Human error,” the Researcher said, tapping the locus ovals. “Always is. Different surveyors. Different pride. Same dirt.”

“What if it isn’t?” Niko asked.

The Researcher smiled the smile of someone who had graded papers.

“Then the basin would be a worse place to work.”

“Worse how?”

“Unpredictable. Hard to publish. Hard to sleep.” The Researcher shrugged. “Take the quarry road if you want stone. Stone stays still. Mostly.”

“Mostly,” Niko repeated.

“Don’t sleep in the smooth holes,” the Researcher added, almost as an afterthought. “Cutter will tell you the same. Everyone tells you the same. Kids still dare each other.”

Niko did not buy the certainty. He bought a spare chalk stub from a jar that claimed to be free and was not free after the second stub.

---

Before dusk, Marta made them walk the camp’s edge once—boots, water trough, the board of open questions. Niko read a chalk line that said *why do brightness charts lie?* and almost pointed it out to Lumi. She had already seen it. She looked away first.

“Someone else failed a page,” she said.

“Good,” Niko said. “Means you’re not alone.”

“Alone is easier sometimes.”

“No,” Marta said, passing them. “Alone is quieter. Not easier.”

Bram returned from the cook fire with a heel of bread he claimed was a gift and Marta claimed was theft. The argument ended when Lumi ate half of it.

Near dusk, Lumi found her old cot tent by walking until a flap looked familiar. Inside: room for packs and wet boots, a crate that still had her scratched initials, a smell of dust and old ink. Bram hung the pot where he could see it. Marta checked the flap ties twice. Lumi put her failed textbook under her crate like a scolded dog.

“You’ll sleep?” Marta asked her.

“I’ll try. Root Camp nights are loud in a soft way.”

“Good. Loud soft is better than quiet wrong.”

Niko lay listening to Scholars argue softly about jars. Someone outside laughed. Someone cursed a spilled sample. The forest pressed its shoulder to the clearing and did not enter.

Bram said, to no one in particular, “We should steal their salt recipe.”

“We are guests,” Marta said.

“Guests notice recipes.”

Lumi snorted into her blanket.

“Tomorrow quarry?” Niko asked the dark.

“Tomorrow stone,” Marta said. “Then lake if the road holds. No inventing monsters out of soft words.”

“I wasn’t—”

“You were arranging them,” Marta said. “Different. Still gets people lost.”

Outside, a kettle that was not Bram’s rattled for two full minutes and then went quiet. Nobody investigated. The sound was enough.

Niko dreamed of maps that changed while he held them, and woke to Bram saying “Morning” to a sky that agreed.
