# Chapter 1 — The Map Wall

The wall was taller than the guildhall.

Maps covered every plank.

Fresh ones.

Yellowed ones.

Some had faded until only rivers remained.

Others were so crowded with notes that the paper had disappeared beneath the ink.

People stopped in front of them the way others stopped in front of graves.

Niko did too.

---

Mossgate’s square smelled like stew grease and wet rope.

Cart wheels rattled.

A child scraped spent mana crystals from a gutter and sorted them by color into a tin. Nobody told her to stop. In Mossgate, that counted as a job.

Niko stood close enough to read the nearest sheets without blocking the path.

Returned maps stayed. Unreturned maps faded. Everyone in town knew that.

He had known it since he was small enough to think the Wall was the edge of the world.

He tracked a line of ink with one finger, careful not to touch.

*Bridge out. Go left. Left is worse.*

The note ended there. No signature. The paper around it had gone the color of old tea.

A woman two steps down sighed and moved on.

Niko moved with the crowd.

He passed a chart covered in corrections. Someone had drawn a deer in the margin. Someone else had crossed it out and written, *Not a deer.*

He almost smiled.

Then he found a map with no drawing.

Just one sentence, centered.

*Don’t go east.*

No signature.

No explanation.

Nobody commented on it.

A man selling grilled skewers glanced at it, shrugged, and went back to turning meat.

Niko checked the maps pinned around it.

Rivers. Forests. Notes. Arrows.

None of them pointed that way either.

He frowned at the empty paper, then at the chalk stub in his pocket.

A boy nearby tugged his mother’s sleeve.

“Why is everyone looking?”

“It’s the Wall,” she said, and pulled him away toward the skewers.

Niko found one more map that stopped halfway down a cliff. The last note said *heard singing* and nothing else.

The Guild clock rang.

Niko stepped back and started walking before he could stare again.

---

The Cartographers’ Guild smelled like wax and damp wool.

A line of two stood ahead of Niko. The man in front complained about a missing stamp. The clerk stamped something else entirely and waved him aside.

“Next.”

Niko stepped up. She slid three sheets across the desk without looking up. Her other hand kept working a stack of travel slips.

“Independent survey. Same place. Three maps.”

Stamp.

“Don’t make a fourth. Just tell us which one we can use.”

Behind Niko, someone cleared their throat.

“Those are the basin ones,” said a cartographer with ink on both cuffs. She was waiting for a different desk and had drifted closer. “I filed a copy last year. Waste of pins.”

“They’re hiring again,” the clerk said.

“They’re always hiring again.”

Stamp.

Niko smoothed the edges.

Guild Map, Year 1712: a river.

Guild Map, Year 1839: forest.

Guild Map, Year 1987: grassland. A road through the middle.

Same stamps. Same mark in the corner.

He laid them side by side and just looked for a while. The clerk kept stamping. The other cartographer wandered off again.

“Probably a mistake,” the clerk said. “Happens.”

Same place.

The fold lines didn’t match. Someone had stored them separately for a long time, then forced them into one stack.

He didn’t mention it.

“What if they weren’t wrong?” Niko asked.

The clerk looked up, then back down.

“If you can prove it, great. Bring back something useful. Sign.”

Niko took the pen.

“There are copies on the Wall.”

“Everyone knows. Doesn’t stop anyone staring.” The clerk pointed with the stamp, still not looking up. “Your lead already signed. She’s outside. Next.”

---

Outside, cookware was stacked too high.

Marta stood in the middle of it while a man with a cord in his teeth argued with a Harvester apprentice.

“No. Not those.”

“They’re mushrooms.”

“They’re leftover mushrooms,” the man said around the cord. “These ones are fine.”

He shoved the apprentice’s bag back.

Marta saw Niko.

“You’re late.”

“I know.”

“You said noon.”

“It isn’t noon yet.”

“It is for me.”

The man spit the cord into his palm and squinted at Niko.

“Who’s this?”

“Our cartographer,” Marta said.

“Hm.”

He held out a pot.

“Carry this.”

“What?”

“The pot.”

Niko took it.

“Niko,” Niko said.

“Bram.” He pulled the maps from under Niko’s arm, tried to fold the top one, made it worse, and pushed them back. “You draw. I cook. I’d get us lost anyway.”

Niko held the three maps out to Marta.

“You saw these?”

“Yeah.” She took one, squinted, and handed it back. “I didn’t like them.”

“One says river. One says forest. One says grass. Same place. How do three surveys disagree and all stay on file?”

“I can read,” Marta said.

“That’s not what I—”

“We’ll find out.”

“Leave the maps,” Bram said, stacking pans. “Morning, by the way.”

It was nearly noon.

“Morning,” Niko said.

Bram kept stacking.

“You think they’re wrong?” Niko asked Marta anyway.

She folded the top map once and pushed it against his chest.

“They’re paper.”

“That’s not an answer.”

“It’s the only answer until we walk there. We’ll look at the real thing.”

“And if it doesn’t match any of them?”

“Then you draw a fourth. That’s your job. Mine is getting you there.”

Marta checked the packs one last time and was already halfway toward the stalls before Bram finished tying a lid. Niko grabbed the pot and followed.

“We leave within the hour,” she said over her shoulder.

Bram bought salt. Marta bought rope. Niko stood between them with the pot and watched Bram sort mushrooms into two piles.

“Why’d you keep that one?” Niko asked.

“It smells right.”

“You can smell that?”

“No,” Marta said. “Leave him.”

“I’m answering,” Bram said.

“I didn’t say stop,” Niko said.

Bram dropped the chosen one into his belt pouch and tied it shut, sure of himself. He sneezed once and kept walking.

Marta rejoined them with the rope.

“One more person.”

A wagon rattled into the square.

---

The Root Camp wagon stopped hard.

A girl jumped down with too many books.

She made it three steps.

The books did not.

Paper spilled across the stones.

“Ah—sorry—wait—don’t—”

Lumi dropped to her knees and gathered pages. Ink on one cuff. Hair coming loose. She stuffed one book under her arm and chased another with her free hand.

Niko set the pot down carefully and picked up a book before it reached the gutter.

“Lumi?”

She looked up, still grabbing pages.

“Niko. Right. You’re—”

She blinked, shoved a diagram into a cover wrong-side out, fixed it, made it worse.

“You write in margins. I saw. On the loan charts.”

She stopped with a book halfway into the stack.

“That wasn’t— I didn’t mean—”

“It’s fine.”

“I’m from Root Camp. I mean the academy. Root Camp is the station and the station belongs to the academy, so it’s basically the same thing, except the academy says it isn’t, which—” The stack leaned. She caught it with her chin. “Anyway. They wanted samples. I came. The samples were boring.”

Niko pointed at a brown smear on one cover.

“Is that mud or ink?”

“Both,” Lumi said. “Mostly ink. The mud was an accident. The ink was also an accident.”

“How do you accident ink twice?”

“I write while walking.”

“Don’t,” Marta said.

Lumi’s hands froze on a book.

“Oh. Right. Hello. I’m Lumi. You’re Marta. I know. They told me. I read your—”

“Don’t.”

“—file,” Lumi finished, quieter. “Sorry.”

She nodded at the rolls under Niko’s arm.

“Is that them? The disagreeing ones?”

“Yeah.”

“Can I—”

“Later,” Marta said, at the same time Bram said, “Can we eat this?” holding up a page.

“No,” Lumi said.

“Can we cook it?”

“No.”

Bram looked at the page again.

“Shame.”

He handed it back.

Marta roped the books into a crate while Lumi was still apologizing to nobody in particular.

“Water,” Marta said. “Then the Wall.”

At the pump Bram filled a kettle, tasted the water, and tied it on.

“Good.”

Niko never found out what that meant.

Lumi got a sleeve wet. Marta finished filling the waterskins and pocketed a lost glove from the mud.

Bram bent for the pot.

The ladle slid off the stack.

Marta caught it without looking.

Lumi laughed—short, surprised, too loud for the square. Marta’s mouth almost went with it.

Niko noticed.

“Now,” Marta said.

---

They were halfway to the Wall before Bram stopped arguing with no one about the kettle strap. A dog followed for a while, then left.

Bram opened his belt pouch mid-step, sniffed, and held the mushroom up.

“Wrong mushroom.”

He tossed it into the gutter and kept walking.

They hung their blank survey sheet before they left.

Mossgate tradition.

Niko pressed the pin in while Marta held the sheet and Bram blocked the wind with a lid.

Around them: old maps. A river. A forest. Grassland.

The pin holes in the plank above the three copies were a handspan to the left.

Someone had moved them.

Recently.

Nobody else looked up.

Farther along—*Don’t go east.* Niko stared at the nails. Brighter than the paper.

“How long has that been up?” he asked.

Nobody answered at first.

“Long enough,” Marta said.

“The nails are new.”

“Or cleaned,” Lumi said.

“Or someone cares about that warning more than the drawing,” Niko said.

“South. Trade Road,” Marta said. “East if we have to.”

“People are going to think we’re stupid,” Lumi said, stuffing a page away.

“Optimistic,” Niko said.

“Close enough,” Bram said, already walking.

---

Near the gate a baker was packing up. Bram stopped.

“We should buy bread.”

“We have rations,” Marta said.

“Rations aren’t—”

“I know.”

Bram bought four buns anyway. Marta paid before he found his coins. Lumi took hers and got flour on a book immediately.

“Morning,” Bram said to the baker, and they went through the gate.

Beyond it, the Trade Road disappeared beneath ancient trees.

Lumi nearly walked into a hanging sign. Niko looked back once.

The Wall still stood above the rooftops.

Their map wasn’t there yet.

Only a blank sheet.

It looked lonely.

He ran after the others.
